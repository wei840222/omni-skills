# Question Techniques

## Socratic method

Guide discovery with questions rather than answers.

**Core moves:**

1. **Clarify** — "What do you mean by X?"
2. **Probe assumptions** — "What are you taking as given?"
3. **Request evidence** — "How do you know that?"
4. **Explore implications** — "If that's true, what follows?"
5. **Challenge viewpoint** — "What is another way to see this?"
6. **Question the question** — "Why does this question matter?"

**When:** Teaching, coaching, philosophical inquiry, thinking a problem through.

## SPIN questioning

Sales / discovery framework (Neil Rackham).

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **S**ituation | Current state | "How does your team currently handle X?" |
| **P**roblem | Difficulties | "What challenges come up with that?" |
| **I**mplication | Stakes | "What happens to your results when that occurs?" |
| **N**eed-payoff | Value of change | "If you could fix that, what would change?" |

**Sequencing:** Establish Problem before Implication. Build the case before the pitch.

## The funnel

Start broad, then narrow.

```text
Wide: "Tell me about your morning routine"
      ↓
Narrower: "You mentioned coffee — walk me through that"
          ↓
Specific: "What made you switch to cold brew?"
```

**When:** Interviews and discovery while importance is still unclear.

---

## Five Whys

Repeat causal "why / what led to" moves to reach a root cause.

1. "Why did the deployment fail?" → Missing config
2. "Why was config missing?" → Not on the checklist
3. "Why not on the checklist?" → New service, checklist stale
4. "Why stale?" → No owner for checklist maintenance
5. "Why no owner?" → Nobody assigned during reorg

**Root framing:** Process gap during reorg, rather than a one-off technical miss.

**When:** Incident reviews, problem analysis, motivation mapping.
**Pace:** Prefer varied stems over a harsh why-stack (see anti-patterns).

## Ladder of abstraction

Move down for concrete evidence; up for pattern.

**Down:**

- "Can you give me an example?"
- "What did that look like in practice?"
- "Walk me through one instance"

**Up:**

- "What's the pattern here?"
- "Stepping back, what is this really about?"
- "What's the underlying principle?"

---

## Open vs closed

| Type | Use when | Examples |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Open** | Exploring | "How do you feel about X?" / "What happened?" |
| **Closed** | Confirming | "Was that before or after the meeting?" |

Default to open in discovery. Close only to nail a specific fact.

---

## The echo

Repeat their key word as a question.

Person: "It's been really frustrating."
You: "Frustrating?"

**Why it works:** Low-directive, invites elaboration, shows listening.

---

## The pause

Strategic silence after a high-value question.

- Count to five before filling the space
- Hold the rephrase and the multiple-choice rescue
- Let thinking time work

**When:** After important questions, when they are still forming an answer.

---

## Scaling questions

Make abstract confidence or progress concrete.

- "On a scale of 1–10, how confident are you?"
- "What would move it from a 4 to a 6?"
- "What was different when it was a 9?"

---

## Safe distance (hypothetical)

Use carefully for sensitive topics; prefer past behavior in research.

- "If you could change one thing without consequence, what would it be?"
- "Imagine a year from now this is solved—what happened?"
- "If a colleague brought you this problem, what would you tell them?"

---

## Pre-mortem

Project failure forward to surface risks.

- "Imagine this project failed completely. What went wrong?"
- "If we look back in a year and this did not work, what was the cause?"

**When:** Planning, risk review, unspoken concerns.
