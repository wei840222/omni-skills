# Question Contexts

## research

**User research / interviews**

**Goal:** Surface real behaviors, needs, and friction with minimal bias.

**Core principles:**

- Prefer past behavior ("Tell me about the last time…") over pure hypotheticals
- Follow emotion—when frustration or excitement appears, probe there
- Let them finish before the next ask

**Key moves:**

- Live leading-question detection with a neutral rewrite
- One adaptive follow-up from the last answer
- Tag insights lightly (pain, workaround, unmet need) when useful
- Silence calibration—wait before prompting

**Example sequence:**

1. "Walk me through how you currently handle X…"
2. Listen for friction
3. "You mentioned Y felt hard—tell me more about that"
4. "What have you tried so far?"
5. "What would 'working well' look like for you?"

## sales

**Sales discovery**

**Goal:** Qualify, uncover pain, map the decision path—pitch only after real pain is clear.

**Core principles:**

- SPIN order: Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff
- Hold the pitch until Problem and Implication are real
- Track qualification gaps as you go (budget, authority, need, timing, or equivalent)

**Key moves:**

- SPIN sequencing
- Pain signals (cost, urgency, repeated workarounds)
- Decision-process mapping (stakeholders, timeline, constraints)
- One implication question to deepen stakes before need-payoff

**SPIN examples:**

- Situation: "How does your team currently handle X?"
- Problem: "What challenges show up with that approach?"
- Implication: "What happens to your pipeline when that occurs?"
- Need-payoff: "If you could remove that constraint, what would change?"

## coaching

**Coaching / 1:1s**

**Goal:** Help the person find their own next step; questions over answers.

**Core principles:**

- Socratic stance—clarify, assume, evidence, imply
- Hold the mirror; they hold the pen
- Separate exploring from directing

**Key moves:**

- Session-aware follow-ups when state exists
- Deflection detection on surface answers
- Reflective feedback framed as questions
- Career or growth scaffolding without clinical therapy claims

**Strong coaching questions:**

- "What would success look like here?"
- "What's in the way?"
- "If you had to choose today, which way would you lean?"
- "What would you tell someone else in this situation?"
- "What's the cost of leaving this unchanged?"

Route full arcs, commitments, and miss-protocols to `coach`.

## journalism

**Journalism / investigation**

**Goal:** Reach verifiable claims, surface contradictions, handle pivots.

**Core principles:**

- Treat the first answer as a starting point
- Cross-check across sources and prior statements
- Sequence hard questions after enough rapport and facts

**Key moves:**

- Contradiction detection
- Evasion patterns (pivot, qualifier, adjacent answer)
- Power-map awareness (who they answer to, fear, depend on)
- Sensitive-topic framing without legal advice cosplay
- Gap analysis—what is missing from the record

**Handling pivots:**

- Direct redirect: "I asked about X; can we return to X?"
- Echo: "So you're saying…" (own or correct)
- Pause: let the silence work
- Third-party frame: "Others have said…—how do you respond?"

## learning

**Learning / understanding**

**Goal:** Build comprehension by active questioning.

**Core principles:**

- Question quality sets learning depth
- Assumptions stay invisible until named
- Complex topics split into smaller askable units

**Key moves:**

- Pinpoint where the mental model breaks
- Decompose topics into ordered questions
- Socratic counters to deepen thinking
- Distinguish factual vs conceptual vs philosophical asks
- Tighten vague questions into precise ones

**Learning question types:**

- Clarifying: "What do you mean by X?"
- Assumptions: "What are we taking as given?"
- Evidence: "How do we know this?"
- Implications: "If that's true, what follows?"
- Viewpoint: "How might someone else see this?"
- Meta: "Why does this question matter?"
