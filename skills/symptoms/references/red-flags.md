# When to seek urgent care

This file is a documentation stop-rule, not a treatment protocol. If the user describes any item below, the first sentence tells them to contact local emergency services or urgent in-person care now. Do not offer a diagnosis, a medicine, or a wait-and-see plan before that sentence.

These features come from public urgent-care guidance. Local emergency numbers differ; use the user's local number rather than assuming one country.

## Act first

| Reported feature | Why it changes the log | Public source |
|---|---|---|
| Chest pain, chest pressure, pain spreading to the arms, neck, jaw, back, or stomach, with breathlessness, sweating, nausea, or feeling faint | Heart-attack warning features; documenting can wait | NHS heart attack: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/ |
| Sudden face droop, arm weakness, speech trouble, or sudden vision loss | Stroke warning features; time-critical | NHS stroke symptoms: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/ |
| Swelling of the face, lips, or throat, wheeze, trouble breathing, or collapse after a possible allergen | Anaphylaxis features | NHS anaphylaxis: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/ |
| Fever plus a non-blanching rash, stiff neck, severe headache, confusion, or dislike of bright light | Meningitis features can worsen quickly | NHS meningitis: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/meningitis/ |
| Confusion, slurred speech, extreme shivering, mottled or very pale skin, not passing urine, severe breathlessness, or feeling they might die, especially with a possible infection | Sepsis features | NICE NG51: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng51 and NHS sepsis: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sepsis/ |
| Heavy bleeding that will not stop, seizure, collapse, or not responding | Immediate in-person care | NHS chest pain overview also routes sudden severe pain to emergency care: https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/chest-pain/ |

After the emergency instruction, log the report only if the user is still responding and wants it written. Mark the entry `urgent-care advised` and do not add a suspected diagnosis.

## Same-day clinician, not an emergency dispatch

Use this band when none of the act-first features are present, but the user reports a rapidly worsening symptom, a high fever they cannot control, new weakness, a symptom after a head injury, pregnancy with bleeding or severe pain, or a symptom in an infant. Tell them to contact a same-day clinician or urgent-care service. Still do not name a diagnosis.

Headache alone is common and usually not dangerous. WHO notes headache disorders are common and that most are primary headaches, while also listing warning features that need prompt assessment: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/headache-disorders. A sudden worst-ever headache, headache with fever and neck stiffness, headache after injury, or a new neurological deficit moves back to the act-first or same-day band above.

## Ordinary log

If the report is a familiar, stable symptom without the features above, continue with `references/proactive-questioning.md` and write the entry.
