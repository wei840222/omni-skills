# Domain Knowledge: Second-Order Effects & Systems Thinking

## Systems Thinking
Systems thinking interprets complex situations through wholes, feedback loops, and relationships rather than isolated parts. It is used to explore interventions and system change in complex contexts.

Key implications for this skill:
- Trace effects across loops and delays, not only linear cause→effect lists.
- Treat stakeholder maps as coupled subsystems: a gain for one actor can become a constraint for another.
- Prefer falsifiable predictions so later reviews can update `patterns.md`.

## Unintended Consequences
In the social sciences, unintended consequences (knock-on effects) are outcomes of purposeful action that were not intended or foreseen. Common groupings:
1. **Unexpected benefit** — an unforeseen positive outcome (serendipity).
2. **Unexpected drawback** — an unforeseen harm alongside the desired result.
3. **Perverse result** — an effect contrary to the original intention.

## Consequence Orders (operational definitions)
- **First-order**: immediate, locally visible outcomes of the decision.
- **Second-order**: outcomes caused by those first-order effects (adaptation, incentives, capacity pressure).
- **Third-order**: outcomes caused by second-order effects (culture, market structure, path dependence).

## Failure Modes to Watch
- Stopping at first-order and treating optimism as strategy.
- Ignoring inversion / negative second-order costs.
- Over-weighting near-term pain relative to durable third-order position.
- Confident point predictions without probability ranges or review dates.
