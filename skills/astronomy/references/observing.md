# Observing workflow

## Plan an observation

1. Collect the observer's location, local date and time, horizon constraints, light-pollution conditions, and available equipment.
2. Verify target altitude, season, phase, weather, and event timing with a current authoritative ephemeris, observatory, or space-agency source.
3. Recommend targets that are visible from the observer's hemisphere and conditions. State whether they are naked-eye, binocular, or telescope targets.
4. Explain the expected view realistically: aperture, magnification, seeing, airmass, Moon brightness, and exposure time affect the result.

## If information is missing

Ask for the missing location and date before naming a time-specific target. If the observer cannot provide them, offer a location-independent concept explanation or explain how to use a current sky map; do not present an unverified target as visible tonight.

## Solar observing

Use only a certified solar filter designed for the exact telescope, binocular, or camera system. Check the filter for damage before use and keep it secured during the observation. If the equipment or filter cannot be verified, direct the user to an established observatory or solar-viewing event rather than improvising a method.

## Observation log

For meaningful observations, record UTC or local time with timezone, coordinates or observing site, instrument and settings, filters, sky conditions, target identifier, and any processing steps. These details make comparisons and follow-up analysis possible.
