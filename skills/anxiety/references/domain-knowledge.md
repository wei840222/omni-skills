# Anxiety, CBT, and exposure reference

Load this reference when explaining the rationale for thought records, coping plans, or graded exposure. It supports education and tracking, not diagnosis or a substitute for clinical care.

## Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)

CBT commonly examines links among thoughts, feelings, and behaviors. For this skill, keep the record descriptive first, then use a thought record only when the user asks to examine a recurring interpretation or prediction.

- [National Institute of Mental Health — Psychotherapies](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies) describes CBT as helping people notice and change inaccurate or negative thinking patterns and behaviors.
- [American Psychological Association — Cognitive behavioral therapy](https://www.apa.org/ptsd-guideline/patients-and-families/cognitive-behavioral) explains that CBT addresses unhelpful thinking and behavior patterns.

## Exposure-based work

When the user has explicitly chosen exposure work, build a graded hierarchy, start with a manageable step, observe the outcome, and adjust one variable at a time. Route acute risk or medical red flags to `triage-rules.md` instead of continuing an exposure exercise.

- [Anxiety & Depression Association of America — Exposure Therapy](https://adaa.org/learn-from-us/from-the-experts/blog-posts/consumer/exposure-therapy) explains gradual, repeated exposure as a behavioral treatment approach for anxiety.

## Tracking boundaries

Record context, body signals, behavior, and outcome so patterns can be reviewed without treating the log as a diagnosis. If the user reports immediate safety concerns, prioritize urgent local help and the triage route over logging.
