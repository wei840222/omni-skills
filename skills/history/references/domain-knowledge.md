# History Method — Domain Knowledge

Load this file when verifying historical method, source criticism, citation defaults, or historiographical framing against primary guidance.

## Historical thinking and source criticism

- Separate established facts from interpretive claims; teach students to name both.
  - Stanford History Education Group — Historical Thinking Chart — https://sheg.stanford.edu/history-lessons
  - Library of Congress — Teaching with Primary Sources — https://www.loc.gov/programs/teachers/getting-started-with-primary-sources/
- Primary vs secondary sources: contemporary evidence vs later synthesis; both need provenance questions (who, for whom, why).
  - Yale University Library — Primary Sources research guide — https://guides.library.yale.edu/primarysources
  - Harvard Library — Distinguishing Primary vs Secondary sources — https://guides.library.harvard.edu/histories/primary-secondary

## Historiography and contested narratives

- Name schools and debates (orthodox / revisionist / post-revisionist, Annales, Marxist, postcolonial) instead of flattening disagreement.
  - American Historical Association — "What Does It Mean to Think Historically?" — https://www.historians.org/resource/what-does-it-mean-to-think-historically/
  - AHA — Statement on Standards of Professional Conduct — https://www.historians.org/resource/statement-on-standards-of-professional-conduct/
- Periodization is a scholarly construct; treat labels like "Renaissance" or "Dark Ages" as frameworks with contested boundaries.
  - AHA Perspectives — essays on periodization and presentism (start from AHA resources hub) — https://www.historians.org/

## Citation and scholarly communication (history default)

- Default history essays to Chicago notes-bibliography unless the course mandates another style.
  - Chicago Manual of Style Online — Notes and Bibliography overview — https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide/citation-guide-1.html
  - Purdue OWL — Chicago Manual of Style 17th edition — https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_17th_edition/cmos_formatting_and_style_guide/chicago_manual_of_style_17th_edition.html

## Classroom frameworks (HIPP / OPVL)

- HIPP: Historical context, Intended audience, Purpose, Point of view.
- OPVL: Origin, Purpose, Value, Limitation (IB-style source evaluation).
  - College Board AP World / European History CED source-analysis expectations — https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/
  - IB History guide materials via IBO (OPVL-style evaluation commonly taught in Diploma Programme history) — https://www.ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/curriculum/individuals-and-societies/history/

## Teaching posture and integrity

- Prefer scaffolding and multi-perspective narrative over answer-dumping for graded work.
  - International Center for Academic Integrity — fundamental values — https://academicintegrity.org/resources/fundamental-values
- Understanding a past worldview is not endorsement of past harms; state that distinction when evaluating violence, empire, or exclusion.

## Safety notes

- Do not store institutional credentials or full personal identity documents in skill state.
- Flag uncertainty and archival gaps explicitly; do not invent citations or archival shelfmarks.
