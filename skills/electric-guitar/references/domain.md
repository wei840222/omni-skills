# Electric Guitar Domain Knowledge

Load this reference for technique, troubleshooting, fretboard framing, and practice-tone defaults. Keep one correction in focus.

## Practice errors

- Noodling without purpose — structured beats random.
- Avoiding hard parts — loop the problem section; do not only play full songs.
- Speed before clean — sloppy fast becomes permanent sloppy.
- Neglecting rhythm — lead is fun; rhythm pays the bills.

## Left-hand traps

- Death grip — keep the thumb relaxed behind the neck near the middle finger.
- Flat barre — roll the index toward the bony edge rather than pressing the pad flat.
- Unintended muting — check finger clearance and arch before adding force.
- Over-stretching — move the hand; do not force a static stretch that causes pain.

## Right-hand traps

- Picking too hard — dynamics come from controlled attack, not constant force.
- All downstrokes when alternate picking is the goal — alternate is the speed foundation.
- Ignoring muting — palm mute for tight rhythm and noise control.

## Mistakes by level

**Beginners:** barre death grip, missing alternate picking, no metronome.

**Intermediate:** pentatonic rut, bends off pitch, rushing solos.

**Advanced:** stale licks, neglecting theory, chasing tone gear before clean playing.

## Technique priorities

| Technique | Why it matters | Clean cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alternate picking | Speed and even articulation | Even tick on both up and down strokes |
| Bending in tune | Pitch accuracy under expression | Matches fretted target note |
| Vibrato control | Signature sustain and feel | Steady width and rate, not random shake |
| Palm muting | Tight rhythm and noise floor | Choked attack without killing pitch when notes should ring |

## Fretboard knowledge

- CAGED system — five shapes that unlock the neck for movable vocabulary.
- Triads everywhere — inversions across string sets beat scale-run wandering.
- Pentatonic plus chord tones — escape the box by landing chord tones on changes.

## Troubleshooting map

| Symptom | First fix |
| --- | --- |
| Bends sound off | Fret the target, then bend to match; re-check fretted reference often |
| Solos sound random | Outline the chord of the moment; place chord tones on strong beats |
| Rhythm feels sloppy | Mute and click first; restore fretted notes only after pulse is steady |
| Can't play fast cleanly | Drop to ~60% tempo; rebuild only while alternate picking stays even |
| Tone is muddy while practicing | Lower gain; use bridge for cut or neck for warmth; practice EQ is not band EQ |

## Tone practice defaults

- Less gain usually improves clarity while learning.
- Bridge pickup tends to cut; neck pickup tends to warm.
- Dial practice EQ for note definition, not stage volume.
- Change one tone control at a time and re-test the same riff.

## Safety

If fretting-hand, picking-hand, wrist, or shoulder pain is sharp, radiating, numb, or lasts after rest, stop the drill. Recommend a qualified teacher or clinician. Resume later with lighter pressure, shorter sets, and open-position material before high-tension bends, wide stretches, or aggressive palm-muted speed work.
