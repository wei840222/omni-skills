# Campaigns And Testing

Use this for newsletters, promotions, and controlled experiments.

## Campaign Types

| Type | Job | Caution |
|------|-----|---------|
| Newsletter | Relationship and value | Low direct conversion is normal |
| Promotional | Clear offer and urgency | Overuse trains complaints |
| Transactional | Receipts, confirmations | Highest opens; do not smuggle heavy promo without consent rules |
| Re-engagement | Win-back + easy exit | Must include preference / unsubscribe path |
| Announcement | Launch or major update | Segment to interested cohorts |

## Subject And Preview

- Aim ~40–50 characters; mobile truncates earlier (~35–40).
- Personalization (name/company) can lift opens ~10–20% when authentic.
- Curiosity can lift opens; clarity usually wins clicks — balance to the goal.
- Prefer natural language; avoid ALL CAPS, “free”, “act now”, and punctuation spam.
- Preview text is a second subject line — replace generic “View in browser”.

## Body Craft

- One goal per email; multiple CTAs dilute response.
- Write for skimmers: short paragraphs, bold key phrases, bullets.
- P.S. lines get read — secondary CTA or key proof belongs there.
- Mobile-first: single column, large tap targets (majority of opens on mobile).
- Plain text can outperform heavy HTML for personal-style messages.

## Timing And Frequency

- Tue–Thu mid-morning is a common baseline — always test the actual audience.
- B2B: business hours; B2C: evenings/weekends can work.
- Frequency follows value density; let subscribers choose cadence when possible.
- Use ESP send-time optimization when available, but do not treat it as a substitute for list quality.

## A/B Testing

- Change one variable at a time: subject, send time, CTA, from name.
- Need meaningful sample sizes (often 1,000+ per variant when available).
- Subject tests: sample first, then send winner to remainder.
- Measure opens for subjects, clicks for content, conversions for offers.
- Document every test so the team does not re-run settled experiments.
