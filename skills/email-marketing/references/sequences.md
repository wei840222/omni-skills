# Sequences And Automation

Use this when designing or debugging automated email flows.

## Welcome Sequence (example: 5–7 emails over ~2 weeks)

| Step | Timing | Job |
|------|--------|-----|
| 1 | Immediate | Deliver promised lead magnet + set expectations |
| 2 | Day 1 | Best content or quick win |
| 3 | Day 3 | Story / origin / values |
| 4 | Day 5 | Social proof or case study |
| 5 | Day 7 | Soft offer or deeper engagement |
| 6 | Day 10 | Address a common objection |
| 7 | Day 14 | Clear primary CTA |

- First email has the highest open rate — make the promise real.
- Space most nurture sends 1–3 days apart; daily is too aggressive for many audiences.

## Common Triggers

| Trigger | Flow |
|---------|------|
| Sign up | Welcome |
| Purchase / activation | Onboarding + cross-sell |
| Abandoned cart | Recovery at ~1h, 24h, 72h (tune to business) |
| No open ~30 days | Re-engagement |
| Link click | Tag + relevant follow-up |
| Date-based | Birthday, renewal, anniversary |

## Design Rules

- One primary goal per message inside the sequence.
- Exit criteria when the user converts, unsubscribes, or becomes a customer of a different track.
- Do not stack promotional blasts on top of a dense automation without frequency caps.

## Failure Modes

- Welcome that only pitches → early unsubscribes
- Cart recovery that ignores already-purchased state → trust damage
- Re-engagement with hard sell and no preference center → complaint spike
