# Writing standards — leads, sources, fact-check

Load this file whenever drafting or reviewing article structure, attribution, or claim safety.

## Lead hook formulas

Open with a pattern that earns the next sentence. Keep the first sentence ≤25 words when the channel allows; put the core who/what/stakes inside the first ~100 words.

| Pattern | Example shape | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Question hook | "What if the assumption behind X is wrong?" | Features, explainers |
| Statistic hook | "N% of [group] still do X every day" | Reported pieces with a verified number |
| Scene hook | "At [specific time], [concrete action] changed the stakes" | Features |
| Conflict hook | "The fight over X is really about Y" | News analysis, opinion |
| Straight news lead | Who + what + when + where + why in one tight sentence | Breaking/hard news (inverted pyramid) |

Rules:

- Do not open with vague throat-clearing ("In today's world…").
- Do not use a statistic hook unless the user supplied a verifiable figure or an on-record source.
- For hard news, prefer the inverted pyramid: most newsworthy facts first, then supporting detail (NN/g inverted-pyramid guidance).

## Source hierarchy

Rank evidence before drafting body claims:

| Rank | Share target (reported pieces) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | ≥60% of material claims | Participants, original documents, datasets, direct observation the user provides |
| Expert | 20–30% | Named specialists with relevant credentials |
| Secondary | ≤20% | Other journalism, secondary summaries |

Attribution format:

```text
"Quoted words," said Name, title/organization.
```

Anonymous sources only when the information is unavailable on the record elsewhere **and** the user accepts the anonymity reason in the text. Never invent an anonymous source.

IFJ Global Charter anchors: respect facts; distinguish fact from commentary; report only from known origins; correct errors promptly in spirit when revising.

## Three-layer fact-check protocol

Apply before delivery on every non-obvious claim:

1. **Source credibility** — Credentials, conflicts of interest, publication/track record.
2. **Information accuracy** — Cross-check with ≥2 independent sources when the claim is consequential and sources exist.
3. **Context integrity** — Quotes and numbers keep surrounding meaning; no selective clipping that reverses sense.

Red flags that force extra verification or explicit hedging:

- Superlatives ("first ever", "unprecedented") without primary proof
- Statistics that perfectly serve one party's interest
- Claims that seem too good or too bad to be true
- Single-source market-moving or reputational allegations

If verification cannot finish in-session: keep the claim out, label it as unverified user-supplied, or ask for the missing primary document. Do not silently "smooth" uncertainty into fact.

## Form-specific voice

| Form | Stance | Required moves |
|---|---|---|
| News reporting | Objective; minimal author opinion | Multiple perspectives when conflict exists; clear time/place; inverted pyramid |
| Opinion / editorial | Clear position | Stated thesis early; evidence-based reasons; explicit counterpoint acknowledgment |
| Feature | Narrative allowed | Scene and character only with attributed or user-supplied detail; facts still cited |

Separate labels in the piece when mixing analysis into news ("Analysis:" / clearly marked commentary), consistent with IFJ fact-vs-commentary duty.
