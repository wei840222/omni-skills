## When to load

Load this skill to write, rewrite, or translate **Spanish** that should sound like a person, not a textbook. Prefer this skill over generic `translate` when tú/usted choice, muletillas (`o sea`, `pues`, `es que`), diminutives, expressive wording, interjections, or a locked regional variety change the draft.

For Spain / Mexico / Argentina / Chile / Colombia travel routing, load the matching destination skill instead. Hand multi-pair localization catalogs to `translate`. For Portuguese or French targets, load `portuguese` or `french` as the primary skill.

This skill is stateless. It does not store local configuration or persistent user state.

## Workflow

1. Identify audience, relationship, channel, source text, requested tone, and any named region. When those details are missing, write warm casual peer Spanish with **`tú`** (or **`vos`** only when the user asks for Argentine/Rioplatense voice) and state the register/region assumption in one short note when the choice matters.
2. Choose one address form and keep it for the whole draft:
   - **`usted`**: strangers initially, elderly, professional, or explicitly formal
   - **`tú`**: friends, peers, internet, and most casual channels outside voseo regions
   - **`vos`**: only when the user asks for Argentina / Rioplatense (or another explicitly voseo locale)
3. Lock one regional variety end to end when a region is known or assumed:
   - Spain: `vale`, `tío`, `mola`, `flipar`, `currar`
   - Mexico: `órale`, `chido`, `padre`, `güey`, `chamba`, `ahorita`
   - Argentina: `che`, `boludo` (peer-only), `re copado`, `laburar`, `morfar`
4. Draft for the selected register. Preserve names, numbers, dates, commitments, and how sure the source is. For casual text, use fragments, diminutives, muletillas, and light reactions that fit the speaker and channel—do not force a slang quota.
5. Read `references/domain.md` for human-facing Spanish drafts, rewrites, and reviews; use its formality, connectors, expressiveness, and delivery guidance. Load `references/sources.md` when a tú/usted, voseo, or lexical claim needs verification.
6. Run the delivery check, then return the Spanish text first.
7. If address form or region is unclear after drafting, rewrite once toward casual **`tú`** for peer chat or careful **`usted`** for unfamiliar recipients, keep one region, and state the assumption briefly rather than stopping without a draft.

## Formality default

Casual Spanish is warm and direct. Unless the audience is professional, institutional, older, unfamiliar, or explicitly formal, lean casual among peers and on the open internet. Prefer `vale` / `bueno` over stiff `de acuerdo` / textbook completeness. For unknown recipients in work or institutional settings, prefer **`usted`** rather than inventing intimacy.

| Situation | Default | Delivery rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official, academic, or unfamiliar institution | `usted` + clearer greetings | Complete sentences; avoid heavy slang and intimate interjections |
| Work chat with known colleagues | soft `usted` or established `tú` by workplace norm | Clear and friendly; light muletillas only if that chat already uses them |
| Friend, peer chat, or social post | `tú` (or requested `vos`) | Short lines, fragments, selective diminutives and reactions |
| Public or mixed audience | Neutral everyday | Stay readable; skip intimate slang stacks unless requested |

## Address and tú / usted / vos

- **`tú`:** safe default for friends, peers, and most Spanish internet writing outside an explicit voseo request.
- **`usted`:** reserved for strangers initially, elders, and explicitly formal or institutional copy.
- **`vos`:** use only for Argentine/Rioplatense (or another user-named voseo locale); keep verb forms aligned (`vos tenés`, not mixed `tú tienes` mid-draft).
- Keep pronouns, clitics, verb agreement, and greetings aligned with the chosen address for the whole draft. Mid-text tú/usted/vos drift is a Required failure.

## Scope boundary

- In scope: drafting, translating, and revising Spanish phrasing for messages, comments, posts, and short copy.
- Out of scope: Spain/Mexico/Argentina/Chile/Colombia travel planning (destination skills), legal advice, full grammar courses, or Portuguese/French-first writing (`portuguese` / `french`).

## Delivery check

Before sending:

1. Confirm one tú/usted/vos choice and matching greetings or verb forms.
2. Confirm one regional variety when slang or interjections appear; do not mix Spain + Mexico + Argentina marker sets in the same reply.
3. Facts, names, numbers, dates, commitments, and uncertainty from the source are preserved.
4. Muletillas, diminutives, fragments, and slang appear only when the channel and relationship support them.
5. A native reader would not screenshot the draft as stiff textbook Spanish for the chosen register.
6. For legal, medical, financial, or publication-sensitive Spanish, keep the requested formality and recommend a qualified native-speaker review.
