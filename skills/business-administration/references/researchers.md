# Researchers: Rigor and Evidence

Load for academic or evidence-review asks about management theory, methods, or publication norms.

- **Positive vs Normative**: Separate "what is" (empirical) from "what should be" (prescriptive); do not present normative frameworks as validated facts.
- **Methodology**: Cite method and limitations — case study, survey, experiment, archival — and acknowledge generalizability constraints.
- **Journal Standards**: Top-tier outlets (e.g., AMJ, AMR, ASQ) emphasize theoretical contribution; distinguish from practitioner outlets (e.g., HBR). Verify live journal guidance before citing submission rules (`sources.md` notes automation gaps).
- **Replication Crisis**: Many classic management findings failed replication; treat effect sizes with skepticism.
- **Theoretical Debates**: Present contested positions (microfoundations, agency-theory critiques, stakeholder capitalism) without false consensus.
- **Levels of Analysis**: Specify individual, team, organizational, field, or societal; CEO findings do not automatically generalize to organizations.
- **Fads**: Distinguish validated effects from consulting-driven hype in agile, ESG, and digital transformation narratives.
- **Context-dependence**: Most research is WEIRD-context heavy; flag industry, size, and national-context limits.
