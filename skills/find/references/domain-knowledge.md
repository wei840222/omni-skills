# Information Seeking Behavior and Strategies

## Core Models

### Information Search Process (ISP)
Developed by Carol Kuhlthau, the ISP models the stages an individual goes through when seeking information. It incorporates thoughts, feelings, and actions, highlighting that uncertainty is highest at the beginning of a search and decreases as the search progresses. A searcher starts with uncertainty, navigates confusion or doubt, and ideally achieves relief or satisfaction upon finding the information.

### Information-Seeking Activities (Ellis's Model)
David Ellis modeled information-seeking behavior based on activities rather than a linear process. These activities map directly to tactical search steps:
- **Starting:** Initial activities forming the search.
- **Chaining:** Following references or links (e.g., citations).
- **Browsing:** Semi-directed or structured looking through a domain.
- **Differentiating:** Filtering and selecting sources based on quality and relevance.
- **Monitoring:** Keeping track of developments.
- **Extracting:** Systematically pulling out relevant material from sources.
- **Verifying:** Checking the accuracy of the information found.
- **Ending:** Final search to check all material covered.

### Information Foraging
Derived from anthropological foraging theories by Stuart Card, Ed H. Chi, and Peter Pirolli, this model suggests that information seekers use "scents" (clues like links, summaries, and images) to estimate how close they are to target information. They will abandon a path if the scent becomes weak.

## Application to Search Execution
When locating information, combining these models yields a robust progressive expansion strategy:
1.  **Assess Information Scent:** Look for strong clues pointing to the right direction. Don't waste time on low-scent paths.
2.  **Employ Chaining:** Once a reliable initial source is found, use its references to find deeper or more authoritative information.
3.  **Differentiate Sources:** Always evaluate the authority, recency, and relevance of the source before extraction.
4.  **Verify (Triangulation):** Do not rely on a single source for critical information. Cross-reference findings to ensure accuracy.
