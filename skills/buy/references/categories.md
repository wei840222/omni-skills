# Category-Specific Guidance

## Electronics & Tech

**Research:**

- Check product release cycles — a new model soon usually means wait or deeper discount on the outgoing unit
- Compare manufacturer refurbished (often strong warranty vs unknown open-box)
- Extended warranties are rarely worth it except for high-failure-cost devices (for example some laptops)

**Timing:**

- Common deal windows: Black Friday, Prime Day, back-to-school (Aug), January clearance
- New flagship phone launches often discount prior models
- Prefer early-cycle purchases when longevity matters; late-cycle buys accelerate obsolescence

**Red flags:**

- "Open box" from unknown sellers without return clarity
- Third-party marketplace sellers for high-demand SKUs with thin history
- Bundle pricing that hides weaker accessories

## Vehicles (Used)

**Research:**

- Use multiple valuation views (KBB / Edmunds class tools when reachable) for a range, not a single magic number
- VIN history (Carfax, AutoCheck, or equivalent)
- Maintenance records and independent inspection

**Negotiation:**

- Discuss only total out-the-door price rather than monthly payments
- Get financing pre-approval before dealer F&I pressure
- End of month / quarter can increase quota pressure
- "I'm comparing two similar cars" creates competition without bluffing details you cannot support

**Red flags:**

- Won't allow independent inspection
- Salvage / flood / rebuilt title without full disclosure
- Price far below market without documented defects
- Pressure to sign before reading paperwork

For a financed primary vehicle when affordability dominates, also consider `money`.

## Services (Contractors, Professionals)

**Before hiring:**

- Get at least three quotes
- Ask for references and actually contact them
- Written scope of work before any payment

**Pricing check:**

- Use local rate ranges (directory / marketplace quotes) as orientation only
- Ask "what does this usually cost for this scope?" before accepting a number

**Red flags:**

- Large deposit upfront (commonly treat >30% as elevated risk unless materials justify it)
- No written contract
- Cash-only insistence
- Refusal to pull permits when required

---

## B2B / SaaS

**Leverage points:**

- End of quarter / year (quota pressure)
- Multi-year commit often unlocks larger discounts — verify in writing
- Competing vendor quotes
- Annual vs monthly billing (ask for annual discount explicitly)

**Traps:**

- Auto-renewal with price escalators
- Per-seat pricing that scales painfully
- Data export fees / lock-in
- "Fair use" limits on "unlimited" plans

**Must ask:**

- Total cost including implementation and training
- What happens to data if we leave?
- Historical price-increase pattern

---

## Real Estate

**Before offers:**

- Comparable **sales** (rather than listings) in the area
- Days on market (more can mean leverage)
- Previous sale price when public records are available

**Negotiable:**

- Price, closing costs, repairs, appliances, closing date

**Always:**

- Independent inspection (mandatory)
- Pre-approval before serious viewing / offer work

For a primary-home purchase system (listings, offer ladder, closing readiness), prefer `home-buying`.

---

## Subscriptions

**Audit triggers:**

- No login / use in 60+ days
- Cheaper alternative exists
- Redundant with another service
- Recent silent price increase

**Cancellation leverage:**

- Ask for retention / loyalty desk
- "What can you offer to keep me?"
- Competing offer in hand (truthful only)

If the user wants ongoing inventory, renewal dates, or recurring-spend tracking, continue in `subscriptions`.
