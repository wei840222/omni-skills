# Negotiation Tactics

Buyer-side coaching only. Do not send offers, place bids, or commit funds unless the user explicitly authorizes that action. For live counterparty autonomy with hard limits, prefer `negotiate`.

## Before negotiating

Set three numbers:

- **Target:** what you want to pay
- **Walk-away:** maximum you will accept
- **Opening:** first offer (below target when the market supports it)

Research what others **paid** (sold comps) — that is leverage. Asking prices are not comps.

## Opening moves

**Anchor first** — state your number before they lock theirs when you have comps.

> "I'm looking to spend around $X"

**The flinch** — react to their price, then silence.

> "Oh, that's more than I expected."

**Counter the first offer** — even if it appears favorable, counter once when the market supports it.

## Mid-negotiation

**Broken record** — politely repeat the constraint.

> "I understand, but my budget is still $X."

**Trade concessions** — every concession given should pair with getting something.

> "I could go to $X if you include Y."

**Higher authority** — create a real exit ramp (partner, finance, manager), not a fake one.

> "I need to check with my partner."

## Closing

**Walk away** — must be genuine. Actually leave.

> "Thank you, I'll think about it."

Sellers often re-engage with a better number after a real walk-away. Treat any "70%" style anecdote as folklore, not a guarantee.

**Split the difference** — use once, only at the end.

> "You're at $X, I'm at $Y, let's meet at $Z."

## Scripts by scenario

### Retail store

> "I saw this for $X at [competitor]. Can you match?"
> [If no] "What's the best you can do?"
> [Still no] "Is there a manager with more flexibility?"

### Used goods (Craigslist, FB Marketplace, similar)

> "Based on recent sales, similar items went for $X. Would you take that?"
> "I can pick up today if we can agree on $X."

Prefer public meetup spots; never wire money for local used goods.

### Monthly bills

> "I've been a customer for X years. I'm seeing better rates elsewhere. What can you do?"
> "I'd like to speak with your retention department."

### SaaS / B2B

> "We're evaluating competitors. What can you offer to win our business?"
> "If we commit to annual/multi-year, what discount is available?"
> "This needs to come in under $X to get budget approval."

Get every discount, renewal cap, and data-export term in writing.

### Car dealership

> "I've researched this model. Fair market price is $X. I'm ready to buy today at that price."

Discuss only the total out-the-door price rather than monthly payments. Keep trade-in and financing as separate negotiations.

## Counter-tactics

| Their move | Your response |
|------------|---------------|
| "This is our best price" | "I understand. Let me think about it." (walk) |
| "Limited time offer" | "If it expires, I'll find another." |
| "Someone else is interested" | "They should buy it then." |
| "I need to check with my manager" | Wait. It is often a tactic; keep your walk-away firm. |
| Gift-card / crypto / wire-only | Walk. Treat as scam signal. |
