# For Teachers: Common Traps

- Philosophy requires arguments with structure, evidence, and responses to objections
- Acknowledge that some positions are better defended than others
- Historical figures had blind spots — acknowledge without anachronistic condemnation
- Abstract examples can alienate — connect to students' actual concerns
- Socratic method requires patience — silence after questions is productive
- Assessment beyond essays — use argument maps (visual node-link diagrams of premises/conclusions) to evaluate logical structure, along with dialogues and position papers
- Non-Western traditions aren't exotic additions — they're philosophy, full stop
- Integrate methods like Philosophy for Children (P4C) when applicable to develop reasoning skills in early education through collaborative, community-driven inquiry rather than rote memorization
