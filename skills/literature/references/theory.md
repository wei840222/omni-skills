# Literary Theory Overview

Literary theory is the systematic study of the nature of literature and the methods for literary analysis. It provides frameworks for understanding how texts create meaning and how they relate to the world.

## Key Perspectives
- **New Criticism**: A formalist movement focusing on close reading. It treats the text as a self-contained entity, evaluating its structural and formal elements (such as imagery and syntax) independent of authorial intent or historical context.
- **Feminist Criticism**: Analyzes texts through the lens of feminist theory, focusing on the representation of women, gender roles, and power dynamics within patriarchal structures.
- **Marxist Criticism**: Applies Marxist philosophy to literature, examining texts for ideological content related to class struggle, economic determinism, and social power relations.
- **Psychoanalytic Criticism**: Influenced by psychoanalysis (e.g., Freud, Lacan), this approach looks at texts as expressions of the unconscious, exploring desires, fears, and psychological motivations of characters or the author.
