# Low-effort recovery and loyalty

Use when repairing a failed journey or designing the “make this the last contact” finish.

## Customer Effort first

Harvard Business Review’s contact-center research (Dixon, Freeman, Toman —
“Stop Trying to Delight Your Customers”) found loyalty moves more when service
**reduces effort** than when agents stage delight. Reported friction patterns
include re-explaining, channel switching, transfers, and repeat contacts.

Operational translations:

| Friction | Countermeasure on this contact |
| --- | --- |
| Re-explain | Read history; summarize back; do not re-collect known fields |
| Channel switch | Finish in-channel when possible; if switch is required, carry context |
| Transfer | Warm handoff + summary; avoid serial cold transfers |
| Repeat contact | Anticipate downstream issue (billing after cancel, tracking after reship) |
| High effort status checks | Proactive dated update; self-serve link with the exact id |

Delight extras (surprise gifts, theatrical empathy scripts) are optional only
**after** the core fix is solid—and never a substitute for resolution.

## Five loyalty-building moves (from the same research framing)

1. Head off the next issue related to this one.
2. Handle the emotional side with steadiness, not theatrics.
3. Minimize channel switching.
4. Learn from disgruntled customers (pattern flags upward).
5. Optimize for problem solving, not handle-time theater.

## Service recovery sequence

1. **Own the break** — specific failure, not vague sorrow.
2. **Fix the account state** — money, access, shipment, data.
3. **Explain without blame-shifting** to the customer.
4. **Prevent recurrence** on their account (flags, cancel future bill, replace token).
5. **Optional goodwill** only inside authority; label one-time exceptions.
6. **Follow-up** if you promised one—calendar it; do not leave orphan promises.

## Metrics without gaming

- **FCR (first contact resolution)** — treat as quality of complete resolution.
  Contact-center industry guides discuss FCR as a core KPI with realistic bands
  rather than a single universal target; do not close tickets “resolved” while
  the customer still lacks the outcome (`sources.md`).
- **CSAT** — post-interaction satisfaction pulse, often % of top-box (4–5 on a
  5-point scale) per common CSAT definitions; use to spot broken journeys, not
  to coerce survey scores (`sources.md`).
- **CES / effort** — prefer questions and QA rubrics that detect repeat contact
  and re-explain pain over vanity delight scores.

## Turning negatives into product signal

- Thank them for the specific report.
- Capture sanitized reproduction steps for engineering/ops.
- Tell the customer only what is true about a fix timeline—no roadmap theater.
- After resolution, a short check-in is earned only when it reduces their effort
  (e.g. confirm refund posted), not as marketing.
