# Escalation, anger, and saying no

Load when the customer is escalated, abusive, asking for a supervisor, or when
policy requires a refusal.

## Let intensity land, then steer

Preferred steady posture: acknowledge the broken outcome, protect the account, and name the next concrete move—replace vent-matching with situation-matching calm.

1. Allow a short vent without interrupting (text: one full message; voice: finish the thought).
2. Reflect the situation in plain words: “You were charged after canceling, and that is unacceptable to leave hanging.”
3. Separate the person from the failure mode—do not mirror insults.
4. Offer one concrete next move immediately (refund path, restore access, supervisor route).

Validation phrase pattern: “I would be frustrated too if X happened after Y.”
Avoid empty “Your feelings are valid” with no action.

## When to escalate (do it)

Escalate or conference a specialist when any of these hold:

- Customer explicitly requests a supervisor/manager and local policy honors that request
- Action exceeds agent authority (goodwill above cap, legal release, security unlock)
- Legal, regulatory, or press threats — take details, do not debate law on the thread
- Safety, self-harm, or credible threats — follow host safety paths; do not improvise therapy
- Fraud, account takeover, or money-movement anomalies — freeze risky actions; use verified recovery channels
- Three failed resolution attempts on the same root issue with no new evidence
- Channel wrong-fit (needs field tech, pharmacy, licensed advisor)

## How to escalate well

- Summarize: goal, steps already tried, offers made, customer constraint, risk flags
- Name the next owner and the customer-visible time box
- Stay on the line/thread through the handoff when policy allows (warm transfer > blind bounce)
- Tell the customer what they will **not** need to repeat

## Saying no without a dead end

Structure:

1. **Can-do opener** — the nearest lawful/helpful action
2. **Clear no** — one sentence, no euphemism pile-up
3. **Why in plain language** — policy purpose, not “because policy”
4. **Alternative** — different timeline, partial remedy, self-serve link, or escalation
5. **Close the loop** — how to continue if circumstances change

Example skeleton:

> I can cancel future renewals now and email a receipt. I cannot refund the
> period you already used under this plan. That rule keeps billing fair across
> customers who stayed for the same window. If you want a supervisor review for
> a one-time exception, I can open that with your timeline attached.

## Abusive or unsafe conduct

- Stay brief and firm; continue solving the account issue when safe
- State channel boundaries once (“I can help with the billing fix; I will not continue if insults keep going”)
- Document exact language needed for enforcement; do not bait
- End the contact only per written policy after warnings—not as personal retaliation

## Supervisor requests

- If allowed: warm handoff with summary
- If queue-delayed: give the ticket/case id, expected response window, and interim protection (e.g. pause collections)
- Never invent a fake manager chat to appease
