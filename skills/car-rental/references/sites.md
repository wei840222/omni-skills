## Price comparison strategy

### Short rentals (1–7 days)

1. Check aggregators (Kayak, Rentalcars).
2. Note the best credible total.
3. Recheck the same company direct — sometimes cheaper.
4. Add AutoEurope / DiscoverCars for budget alternatives.
5. Recalculate with insurance, fuel, airport fees, and deposit holds.

### Medium term (1–4 weeks)

1. Ask aggregators for monthly / weekly rates.
2. Compare subscription quotes.
3. Convert daily × days vs monthly packages.
4. Prefer city pickup when airport markups dominate.

### Long term (1+ months)

1. Subscription first.
2. Local dealer lease quotes.
3. Traditional rental monthly rate.
4. Run rent/lease/buy break-even via `references/comparison.md`.

## Red flags

- Headline price far below peers — list exclusions (insurance, mileage, fees).
- Unknown operator — require recent reviews before booking.
- Heavy prepay — less flexibility and harder refunds.
- Vague pickup location — may imply unpaid shuttle time.
- "Free cancellation" — read the actual cutoff.

## Research notes (Gate 6)

1. **Dynamic pricing** — Rates move with inventory, lead time, and competitors.
   Re-check before travel when free cancellation allows rebooking.
   Source: https://www.nerdwallet.com/blog/travel/how-to-rent-a-car/
2. **Aggregator incompleteness** — Young-driver, one-way, and local mandatory
   insurance fees may appear only at final checkout.
   Source: https://support.kayak.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001137374-How-do-I-find-the-best-car-rental-deals
3. **Debit vs credit deposits** — Debit cards are often refused or need large
   holds plus return-travel proof; prefer a physical credit card in the
   primary driver's name.
   Sources: https://www.hertz.com/rentacar/reservation/reviewactivate.do?method=debitCards · https://www.creditkarma.com/credit-cards/i/rent-car-debit-card
