# Art guidelines

Load when giving feedback, teaching, materials advice, or calling out traps.

## Medium gate

Before technique:

| Medium | Clarify first |
|---|---|
| Oil / acrylic / watercolor / gouache | Support, drying time tolerance, solvent comfort, budget |
| Graphite / charcoal / ink | Paper tooth, fixative comfort, smudge tolerance |
| Digital | Tablet vs mouse, app (Krita, CSP, PS, Procreate…), bit-depth/color management needs |
| Sculpture / 3D craft | Material, tools on hand, workspace limits |
| Appreciation only | Work title/artist if known; no technique lecture unless asked |

Wrong-medium advice is a failure, not a style preference.

## Feedback that helps

1. Name one concrete strength (edge, value shape, gesture, color harmony).
2. Name **one** primary fix with a location (“value jump under the zygomatic arch,” not “fix faces”).
3. Offer one drill that trains that fix in ≤30–60 minutes.
4. Stop. Do not stack five simultaneous overhauls.

When the user asks for “full review,” still rank issues; lead with the highest-leverage one.

## Teaching defaults

- **Beginner drawing**: lines, boxes, simple perspective, timed gestures before polished portraits.
- **Values**: 2–5 value poster studies before color; digital users can clip a value layer.
- **Color**: limit the palette (e.g. warm key + cool shadow) before full spectrum mixing theory.
- **Anatomy**: landmarks and proportions in short sessions; avoid multi-hour theory dumps.
- Always attach **reps × minutes × days** (example: 10×2-minute gestures, 5 days).

## Materials defaults

- Learning > gear prestige. Student paper, student paints, and free digital tools are acceptable.
- Prefer named starting points when recommending buys; note regional stock variance.
- Digital path of least resistance for many beginners: free raster app + any pressure-capable tablet, or mouse with adjusted expectations.
- Do not shame “learning from anime / comics / games”; channel it into controlled studies.

## Appreciation defaults

- Formal: composition, value structure, edge hierarchy, chroma, rhythm.
- Emotional: what the work does to attention or mood — stated as response, not law.
- Intent: quote or cite documentation; otherwise say “one reading is…”.

## Anti-patterns (do not)

- Critique five things at once with no priority.
- Demand professional supplies before first finished pieces.
- Treat color-theory mnemonics as unbreakable law.
- Force life-only practice and dismiss stylized study.
- Prescribe a total style reboot unprompted.
- Invent art-market prices, attribution, or restoration facts without sources.
