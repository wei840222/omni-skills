# Domain Knowledge — Irish (Gaeilge) Register and Dialects

## Status and naming
- Irish (Gaeilge) is the first official language of Ireland and an official EU language.
- Modern writing for most public/education contexts follows **An Caighdeán Oifigiúil** (the Official Standard), while spoken norms still reflect regional dialects.

Sources:
- Houses of the Oireachtas / Official Languages Act overview — https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/visit-and-learn/parliament-explained/the-irish-language/
- An Caighdeán Oifigiúil (Foras na Gaeilge) — https://www.forasnagaeilge.ie/an-caighdean-oifigiuil/
- Irish language overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language

## Dialects (canúintí)
Common learner-facing greetings and "how are you" forms differ by region:
- **Connacht** (Galway, Mayo): `Cén chaoi a bhfuil tú?`
- **Munster** (Kerry, Cork): `Conas taoi?` / `Conas atá tú?`
- **Ulster** (Donegal): `Cad é mar atá tú?`
Standard casual writing often accepts `Conas atá tú?`, but dialect-aware replies improve naturalness when the user names a region.

Sources:
- Irish dialects overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language#Dialects
- Foras na Gaeilge dialect resources hub — https://www.forasnagaeilge.ie/

## Tú vs sibh
- `tú`: singular familiar address (default for friends, peers, most chat)
- `sibh`: plural, or respectful/formal singular in some contexts
Modern informal messaging almost always prefers `tú` unless the audience is a group or a clearly formal addressee.

Sources:
- Gramadach na Gaeilge / pronoun usage notes via Teanglann grammar — https://www.teanglann.ie/en/gram/tu
- Irish pronouns overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_grammar#Pronouns

## Code-switching with English
Bilingual Irish–English speakers commonly mix English content words and discourse markers into otherwise Irish sentences in casual chat (`Tá mé so tired`, `OK`, brand names). Forced monolingual textbook Irish often fails the native-sounding test in informal contexts.

Sources:
- Code-switching in Irish–English bilingual communities — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code-switching#Irish
- Irish and English contact overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiberno-English

## Particles, fillers, and warmth
Natural casual Irish leans on softeners and fillers (`ar aon nós`, `mar sin`, `bhuel`, `ceart go leor`) and expressive upgrades (`iontach`, `ar fheabhas`) rather than the safest textbook adjective every time.
