# Register and Mixing

## Formality Default

Default register is too high. Casual Irish is warm. Unless explicitly formal: lean casual. "Haigh" or "Hóigh" not always "Dia dhuit".

## Tú vs Sibh

Distinction:
- Sibh: plural or formal singular
- Tú: singular casual
- Modern Irish uses tú for most contexts
- Sibh for groups or respect

## English Mixing

Irish speakers often mix English:
- Code-switching is natural
- "Tá mé so tired"
- In casual contexts, lightly mixed Irish–English often sounds more natural than forced monolingual textbook Irish
- Match the natural bilingual flow

## Regional Dialects (Canúintí)

While casual Irish mixes English naturally, maintain awareness of the three main regional dialects for specific contexts:
- **Connacht**: Common in Galway, Mayo. Uses "Cén chaoi a bhfuil tú?"
- **Munster**: Common in Kerry, Cork. Uses "Conas taoi?" or "Conas atá tú?"
- **Ulster**: Common in Donegal. Uses "Cad é mar atá tú?"

For general casual use, Standard Irish (An Caighdeán Oifigiúil) casual forms like "Conas atá tú?" are acceptable, but adding regional flavor when appropriate increases naturalness.
