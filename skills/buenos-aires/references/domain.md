# Domain — Buenos Aires core rules

## 1. Identify user context first

- **Role:** visitor, resident, tech/remote worker, student, entrepreneur, digital nomad
- **Timeline:** short visit, planning to move, already there
- Load the matching reference files; do not dump the whole package

## 2. Economic and payment context

Argentina has a long history of high inflation and changing FX/payment rules. Treat **blog “blue dollar” spreads and fixed ARS-per-USD tables as perishable**.

Durable guidance:

- Separate **official** channels from **parallel/market** anecdotes; do not coach illegal activity
- Many visitor and expat workflows mix **foreign cards**, **some ARS cash**, and sometimes **legal securities/FX products**—confirm what is available to *this* passport and bank near travel dates
- Local peso earners and USD earners experience the city differently; say which lens you are using
- Electronics and imports often cost more than US/EU shelf prices

See `references/cost.md` for range frameworks. Re-check `references/sources.md` before lease or salary math that must be current.

## 3. Cultural context

- Strong European/Italian influence on food, cafés, and late dining
- Dinner often late; nightlife starts later than US/Northern Europe norms
- Tango is cultural infrastructure, not only a tourist show
- Football (Boca vs River) is high-emotion—match days change crowd and transit patterns
- See `references/culture.md`

## 4. Weather reality

- **Summer (Dec–Feb):** hot/humid peaks; January exodus is common
- **Winter (Jun–Aug):** cool/gray; many buildings lack strong central heating—indoor cold is real
- **Shoulder:** Mar–May and Sep–Nov often nicest for walking
- See `references/climate.md`

## 5. Cost reality (framework, not live quotes)

For **USD-earning** visitors/expats, BA is often affordable relative to global hubs, with large variance by barrio and building quality. Prefer ranges and tradeoffs over fake precision.

Illustrative **order-of-magnitude** bands (USD-earner lens; re-verify near decision):

| Item | Framework |
| --- | --- |
| 1BR rent, desirable barrios | Hundreds of USD/month possible; premium towers much higher |
| Outer/value barrios | Materially cheaper; trade commute and amenities |
| Public transit | Low absolute cost with SUBE once set up |
| Dining out | Wide band; beef/wine value is a common highlight |
| Services (cleaning, laundry) | Often cheap vs US/EU |
| Imported electronics | Frequently expensive |

See `references/cost.md` for structured tables—treat any numeric cell as **orientation**, not a live listing.

## 6. Transit options

- **Subte:** core metro backbone; crowded at peaks
- **Colectivos:** dense bus network
- **Ride-hail / taxis:** common supplement
- **SUBE:** primary public-transport card product—register/load via official guidance
- Dense central living often makes a private car optional or counterproductive
- See `references/transport.md` and official SUBE/tourism mobility pages in `references/sources.md`

## 7. Neighborhood matching (starting heuristic)

| Profile | Often start looking |

| Families | Belgrano, Núñez, Caballito |
| Budget-conscious | Almagro, Boedo, parts of Villa Crespo |

| Tech offices | Often Palermo / Norte corridor—confirm commute |

Always override heuristics with the user’s actual commute and late-night return path. Detail files: `references/neighborhoods-*.md`.

## 8. Visa and stay orientation

Argentina is comparatively approachable for many nationalities, but **rules are passport- and category-specific**.

Orientation only (verify live):

- Many passports enter as tourists for a limited stay; extensions and overstay consequences are real administrative processes
- Temporary and permanent residency paths exist via Migraciones—requirements differ by ground (work, rentista, family, etc.)
- “Digital nomad” products and enforcement posture change; never promise a loophole
- Citizenship timelines are often shorter than many peer countries **after** lawful residency—still not automatic

See `references/visas.md` and Migraciones URLs in `references/sources.md`.

## Buenos Aires-specific traps

- Stale dual-rate blog math treated as current law
- January service closures and empty-city dynamics
- Phone theft and distraction theft
- Under-heated winter apartments
- Sunday and afternoon closures outside busy corridors
- Import price shock on electronics
- Cubierto / tipping norms misunderstood
- Assuming English coverage outside tourist/tech bubbles
- La Boca: Caminito tourist pocket ≠ unrestricted roaming

## Safety awareness (orientation)

Generally manageable for a large city with street-smart habits: conceal phones, prefer attended ATMs, know which pockets feel wrong after dark, and treat express-kidnapping stories as rare-but-teach-opsec rather than panic. Detail: `references/safety.md`. Tourism health/security page in `references/sources.md`.

## Legal orientation (not advice)

- Follow current national/city rules on substances, protests, and consumer rights
- Photography of people: ask when in doubt
- This skill does not replace a lawyer or gestor for filings

## Expat community

Large English-friendly pockets in Palermo and coworking scenes; integration still improves sharply with Spanish. Social apps and local clubs matter more than any single “expat office.”
