# Domain knowledge

Load this file when citing a population activity, diet, or sleep anchor. These figures describe groups. They are not a personal prescription.

## Activity

- Adults benefit from at least 150 minutes a week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity, or an equivalent mix. Add muscle-strengthening activity on at least 2 days a week.
- Some activity is better than none. A person far below the weekly anchor starts with a short bout they can repeat, then builds toward the anchor.
- Walking, cycling, and active daily tasks count. The anchor is weekly volume, not a gym membership.

## Food pattern

- A healthy pattern is built from varied, minimally processed foods, with limited free sugars, salt, and unhealthy fats.
- Adults and anyone older than 10 can use at least 400 g of fruit and vegetables a day as a population anchor.
- Adult salt intake is limited to less than 5 g per day (about 2 g sodium). Free sugars stay under 10% of total daily energy. Unsaturated fats (fish, nuts, olive and similar oils) are preferred to saturated fats.
- Individual needs differ by age, activity, culture, and medical conditions. A number from a fact sheet does not replace a clinician or a dietitian when the user has a diagnosis or a prescribed diet.

## Sleep

- Adults are commonly advised to get 7 or more hours a night. A single short night is not a diagnosis.
- If sleep trouble lasts, limits daytime function, or comes with breathing pauses, snoring that worries a partner, or unrefreshing sleep, the next step is a clinician, not another generic tip.

## Evidence tiers

Name the tier when you cite a figure:

| Tier | Meaning | How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| Guideline | WHO, CDC, or an equivalent public-health body | "WHO and CDC describe this as the adult population anchor." |
| Single study | One paper, not yet in a guideline | "One study found this; it is not yet a population rule." |
| Mechanism | A plausible pathway without outcome data for this person | "The mechanism is plausible; it is not proof it will work for you." |
| Anecdote | Someone's story, including the user's past experiment | "That is one person's result, including yours. We can test a small version." |

Conflicting guidance stays visible. Say which source you are using and what it does not cover.
