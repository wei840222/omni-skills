# Change strategy

Load this file when the user wants to start or restart a wellness habit.

## One change

Pick one behavior for this week. A plan that changes sleep, food, and training together is a restart risk, not a stronger plan. If the user lists several goals, ask which one matters this week and park the rest in one sentence.

## Minimum effective dose

Start with a dose they can finish on a busy day.

| Goal | First dose | Done looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Move more | 5-minute walk after an existing cue | They walked, even if slowly |
| Strength | 1 set of a familiar move, 2 days this week | They finished the set without pain that changes their gait |
| Sleep window | Fixed wake time, same 7 days | They got up at that time, even after a short night |
| Food pattern | Add one fruit or vegetable serving they already buy | That serving was eaten, not a new diet |
| Stress | 2-minute pause before a recurring meeting | They paused once |

Raise the dose only after the small version happened on most planned days. Missing one day keeps the same dose. Two missed weeks means shrink the dose, not add a new goal.

## Stack on an existing cue

Attach the action to something they already do: after brushing teeth, after the commute, with the first coffee. A new time block with no cue is harder to keep than a small action on an old cue.

## Baseline before comparison

Use 2 to 4 weeks of their own notes before comparing them with a population anchor. Ask what a normal week already contains: work hours, current movement, sleep window, foods they like, injuries, and medicines they want a clinician to know about. Population averages in `references/domain-knowledge.md` describe groups. Their notes describe them.

## Pain and medical constraints

Stop the activity and recommend a clinician when movement causes chest pain, joint pain that changes how they walk, dizziness, or a symptom listed in `references/safety-boundaries.md`. A past injury or a current medicine is a constraint to name, not a reason to invent a modified clinical program.
