# Writing effective quiz questions

Ground item craft in established multiple-choice guidance (see `sources.md`). Prefer positive stems and one clear best answer unless the product explicitly needs multi-select.

## Question structure

**Stem:** the question or incomplete statement.

- Clear, concise, single interpretation
- All necessary context inside the stem
- Phrase positively (prefer “Which is true…” over “Which is NOT…”)

**Correct answer:** obviously correct to someone who knows the material.

**Distractors:** wrong answers that look plausible to someone who does not.

## Good distractors

Distractors should be:

- Grammatically consistent with the stem
- Similar length and specificity to the correct option
- Drawn from common misconceptions, near-miss concepts, or partial truths
- Not jokes, absurd outliers, or pattern-giveaways

**Avoid:**

- “All of the above” / “None of the above” as a default crutch
- One obviously wrong option among three strong ones
- Correct answer always in the same letter position
- Options that do not fit the stem grammatically

**Good distractor sources:**

- Frequent learner mistakes
- Related but incorrect concepts
- Partial procedures missing a critical step
- Terms that sound right in casual speech but are wrong technically

## Item types

### Multiple choice

Best for recognition and applied discrimination.

```text
What is the capital of Australia?
A) Sydney      ← common misconception
B) Melbourne   ← second-largest city
C) Canberra    ← correct
D) Brisbane    ← another major city
```

### True / false

Best for binary facts; use sparingly (high guess rate).

- One clearly true or false claim
- Avoid double negatives
- Prefer precise scope words; bare “always/never” often makes items too easy or unfair

### Fill in the blank

Best for recall of terminology.

- Blank a key concept, not a trivial word
- One acceptable answer set (list equivalents if needed)
- Enough context around the blank

### Matching

Best for relationships and definitions.

- More options than stems when feasible (reduces elimination)
- Homogeneous option set
- One clear relationship being tested

### Ordering / sequencing

Best for processes and timelines.

- Distinct, non-interchangeable steps
- Roughly 4–7 items; longer lists become tedious

## Difficulty calibration

| Level | Intent |
|-------|--------|
| Easy | Direct recognition of a taught concept |
| Medium | Apply the concept in a short scenario |
| Hard | Discriminate near-miss options or multi-step reasoning |

Do not spike difficulty with obscure trivia outside the stated objective.

## Knowledge item checklist

- [ ] One concept only
- [ ] Objective alignment stated or obvious
- [ ] Single best answer (or explicit multi-select rules)
- [ ] Plausible distractors
- [ ] No all/none-of-the-above default
- [ ] Positive framing when possible
- [ ] Explanation ready for learning-mode feedback

## Personality / outcome item checklist

- [ ] Each option maps to a defined trait or outcome
- [ ] No dead options that never affect results
- [ ] Tone stays respectful across outcomes
- [ ] Result copy is specific enough to feel personal
