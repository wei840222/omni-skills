# Operating Patterns and Learning Limits

Stories can generate hypotheses; they cannot predict a specific product’s revenue, timeline, or conversion. Use the patterns below to select a test, then rely on the product’s own evidence.

## Reusable patterns

- Build from a problem the team can observe closely, then verify that a distinct customer will pay for the outcome.
- Ship the smallest credible version that delivers the promised outcome and preserves customer support, billing, and refund obligations.
- Revisit positioning after meaningful evidence: a new outcome, customer segment, proof point, or product capability can justify a new launch message.
- Prefer focused distribution and direct customer learning before investing heavily in channels whose economics are not measured.
- Price for a viable delivery model, including support, service reliability, refunds, and future maintenance.

## Evidence log

For each learning cycle, record:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | “Teams with recurring reporting work will choose the monthly package.” |
| Audience and channel | “Operations leads from the existing waitlist.” |
| Change | “New reporting package and landing-page message.” |
| Primary metric | “Completed purchase per eligible visitor.” |
| Guardrails | “Refund rate, support hours, complaint rate.” |
| Outcome and decision | “Keep, revise, or retire—with a dated reason.” |

## Capacity and quality

Fast learning is valuable when customers receive the promised outcome. Preserve reliability, privacy, accessibility, and support quality as constraints in every experiment. If a result improves conversion while worsening a guardrail, investigate the trade-off rather than treating the headline metric as a win.
