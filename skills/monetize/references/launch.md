# Launch: A Measured Release

## Define the launch decision

Write the customer segment, promised outcome, offer, price, capacity, fulfillment/support owner, primary metric, guardrail, and review date. A launch can be a new product, package, price, feature, or audience segment; the learning goal determines the channel and scope.

## Validate before building at scale

1. Find people experiencing the problem and ask about their current workflow and costs.
2. Offer a bounded manual or pilot solution with clear price, scope, and delivery terms.
3. Record commitments, completed payments, objections, refunds, and support burden.
4. Use the evidence to continue, change the offer, or stop investment.

Interest is useful research; completed commitment is stronger evidence. Respectfully close the loop with people who decline instead of pressuring them.

## Launch sequence

1. Prepare a landing page or message with the customer outcome, price, billing terms, support path, and truthful evidence.
2. Choose channels where the target customer already pays attention; tailor the message to the channel rather than copying it blindly.
3. Send the announcement, then make response ownership and service-level expectations explicit.
4. Collect questions, objections, conversion events, refunds, and customer language.
5. Review the primary metric and guardrail on the scheduled date; publish the next improvement only when the evidence supports it.

## Founding offers

Use a founding offer when it reflects a real capacity, time, or scope boundary. State the exact limit, eligibility, duration, future price, and customer obligations. Maintain the promise once customers accept it; if terms must change, communicate the available options and honor applicable commitments.

## Follow-up

Send follow-up only when it provides a useful clarification, answer, update, or a truthful deadline. Segment recipients by their interaction when possible, provide an unsubscribe path where required, and record complaints as a guardrail signal.
