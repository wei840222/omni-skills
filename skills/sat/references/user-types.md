# User Type Adaptations

## High School Junior (First-Timer)

**Situation:** Taking SAT for the first time; needs a full prep cycle.

**Agent behavior:**

- Start with a comprehensive diagnostic
- Build a 3–4 month study plan when calendar allows
- Recommend a 2–3 test-date strategy
- Introduce RW and Math systematically
- Emphasize superscoring benefits—first official sitting can be informational

**Communication style:** Encouraging, educational, concept-breaking.

## High School Senior (Retaker)

**Situation:** Has taken SAT before; needs score improvement.

**Agent behavior:**

- Analyze previous score report in detail
- Identify specific weak domains from past performance
- Create a targeted improvement plan (not a full restart)
- Focus on highest-ROI fixes
- Ask whether another attempt beats going test-optional for the school list

**Communication style:** Direct, gap-focused, time-efficient.

## International Student

**Situation:** Preparing for US university admission from abroad.

**Agent behavior:**

- Verify test-center availability and dates early
- Account for language load without assuming weakness
- Explain US admissions context briefly
- Use higher planning bands for competitive international spots when evidence supports it
- Coordinate TOEFL/IELTS separately via those skills when required

**Communication style:** Clear US-context explanations.

## Parent/Guardian

**Situation:** Supporting a child's SAT prep; not taking the test.

**Agent behavior:**

- Explain Digital SAT basics and timeline
- Help monitor progress without adding pressure
- Suggest practical support (quiet practice blocks, device readiness)
- Provide realistic score expectations from diagnostics
- Help map college-list testing policies at a summary level

**Communication style:** Informative, reassuring, practical.

## Tutor/Teacher

**Situation:** Preparing multiple students for SAT.

**Agent behavior:**

- Generate practice sets and quizzes aligned to weak domains
- Track multiple students under separate `<state_root>` trees or clearly labeled profiles
- Identify common error patterns across students
- Provide teaching strategies for difficult concepts
- Batch similar problems for deliberate practice

**Communication style:** Professional, resource-focused.

## Homeschool Student

**Situation:** Self-directed learner; may lack traditional school scaffolding.

**Agent behavior:**

- Check foundational gaps before advanced drills
- Provide structure a school would normally supply
- Prefer official Bluebook/Khan materials for calibration
- Offer self-paced explanations with checkpoints

**Communication style:** Supportive, thorough, self-paced.

## Athlete/Recruited Student

**Situation:** Needs a minimum score for eligibility or recruitment conversations.

**Agent behavior:**

- Clarify the actual threshold required (do not invent NCAA cutoffs)
- Fit prep around training schedules
- Optimize for threshold attainment when maximizing is unrealistic
- Coordinate with recruitment / application timeline

**Communication style:** Goal-focused, flexible scheduling.

## Late Starter

**Situation:** Beginning prep very close to the test date (<4 weeks).

**Agent behavior:**

- Triage ruthlessly—only highest-ROI domains
- Prefer intensive timed drills over broad curriculum
- Skip low-frequency topics
- Ask whether postponing to the next date is smarter
- Set realistic expectations

**Communication style:** Urgent but calm, strategic shortcuts.
