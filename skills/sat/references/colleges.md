# College Admissions Planning

Planning bands below are **approximate middle-50% style targets**, not guarantees. Always verify each school's current testing policy and published ranges.

## Score Targets by College Tier

| Tier | Examples | Planning band (Total) |
|------|----------|------------------------|
| Elite (Ivy+) | Harvard, MIT, Stanford | 1550+ |
| Highly Selective | Duke, Northwestern, UCLA | 1500+ |
| Selective | Boston U, UC Davis, NYU | 1400+ |
| Competitive | State flagships, strong privates | 1300+ |
| Less Selective | Regional schools | 1200+ |

Above a school's middle-50% helps; below does not automatically disqualify when other factors are strong—or when the school is test-optional.

## Test-Optional Landscape

Many colleges remain test-optional or test-flexible. Practical heuristic:

- **Score above ~50th percentile for the school** → Strong case to submit
- **Score below ~25th percentile** → Withhold if optional
- **In between** → Weigh GPA rigor, essays, and full application context

Policies change year to year—open the school's admissions testing page.

## Superscoring Strategy

Many selective colleges superscore (best section scores across dates).

**Example:**

- Test 1: RW 720, Math 680 → 1400
- Test 2: RW 690, Math 730 → 1420
- Superscore: RW 720 + Math 730 → **1450**

**Strategy:**

1. Plan 2–3 test dates when calendar allows
2. After the first official score, focus prep on the weaker section
3. Even if a single sitting total drops, superscore may still rise

**Schools that require single-sitting / all scores:** some state schools and international universities. Verify before promising a superscore plan.

## Score Choice

College Board Score Choice lets students choose which test **dates** to send in many cases.

- Confirm whether each college allows Score Choice or requires all scores
- Do not assume Georgetown-style all-scores rules apply everywhere—check the school
- Watch College Board's current Score Choice guidance via the scores hub in `sources.md`

## Timeline Planning

### Junior Year

- Fall: Diagnostic, begin prep
- Spring: First SAT attempt (often Mar/May)
- Summer: Intensify if needed

### Senior Year

- Aug/Oct: Common final attempts for ED/EA
- November: Last comfortable window for many RD plans
- December: Some schools still accept for RD—verify

**Test early when possible** — earlier attempts reduce senior-fall pressure.

## International Students

Additional considerations:

- SAT accepted at many US universities; still confirm each target
- Some require English proficiency (TOEFL/IELTS) separately—route those workflows to `toefl` / `ielts`
- Test-center capacity can be limited—register early
- Competitive international pools often need higher planning bands

## Beyond the Score

SAT is one factor. Colleges also evaluate:

- GPA and course rigor
- Essays
- Extracurriculars
- Recommendations
- Demonstrated interest (for some schools)

A strong SAT opens doors; it does not guarantee admission.

## Financial Aid Impact

Some merit scholarships use score thresholds:

- Check each school's merit matrix
- Thresholds can justify extra prep weeks
- Do not invent dollar amounts—open the school's published grid

## Building a College List

Use the score to build a balanced list:

- **Reach:** score at or below ~25th percentile
- **Match:** near ~50th percentile
- **Safety:** above ~75th percentile

Aim for a mix (for example 2–3 reach, 4–5 match, 2–3 safety), adjusted to application capacity.
