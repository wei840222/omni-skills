# Founder domain playbooks

Load this file when the user needs expanded stage guidance beyond the always-on
core rules in `SKILL.md`. Keep recommendations tied to stated facts; do not
invent traction, revenue, or investor intent.

## Early stage

- Talk to customers before large build programs — assumptions are usually wrong.
- Validate the problem before locking a solution path.
- MVP means embarrassingly small — ship the smallest artifact that tests the hypothesis.
- Speed of learning beats product polish early on.
- Choose cofounders like a long partnership — complementary skills, aligned values, tested under stress.

## Product-market fit

- Users that would be "very disappointed" without you — classic dependence metric (often cited ~40%+ among recent actives; verify before quoting as law).
- Retention matters more than acquisition — a leaky bucket remains empty.
- Pull beats push — customers seeking you out beats constant persuasion.
- PMF often feels like demand you can barely keep up with.
- Before PMF, scale only motions that already work for the dependent cohort.

## Fundraising

- Raise when you have momentum, rather than desperation.
- Know your numbers cold — ARR, growth rate, burn, runway, unit economics.
- VCs pattern-match — know what pattern you fit.
- SAFE vs priced round: name trade-offs on both sides before recommending.
- Many meetings per single yes is normal — short reject streaks offer limited company signal alone.

## Team

- First ~10 hires define culture — choose carefully.
- Hire for slope over experience early — fast learners beat credential-only operators when infrastructure is thin.
- Fire fast when performance stays poor after clear expectations — one bad hire poisons teams.
- Equity is precious — be generous while remaining prudent.
- Founder doing everything limits scaling — delegate before breaking.

## Execution

- Focus is saying no to good ideas — in addition to declining bad ones.
- Metrics: one north star, 2–3 supporting — more usually means lost focus.
- Weekly cadence: set goals, execute, review, repeat.
- Ship, then iterate from feedback rather than waiting for perfect.
- Default to action — analysis loops that stop changing decisions waste runway.

## Pivoting

- Pivot on strategy while maintaining vision when the problem still matters.
- Data shows what requires change — customers suggest what might work next.
- Pivots require conviction — half-pivots fail.
- Runway determines pivot options — earlier pivots with months of cash beat last-gasp rewrites.
- Kill projects before they kill you — sunk cost is sunk.

## Survival

- Cash is oxygen — know runway in weeks, rather than only months.
- Revenue solves most arguments — paying customers validate more than decks.
- Cut costs in chunks that move runway, rather than demoralizing slices.
- Down rounds happen — survive now, optimize valuation optics later when alive.
- Mental health is essential operating capacity — burnout helps nobody.

## Founder psychology

- Imposter feelings are common — treat them as a frequent founder state, not unique proof of unfitness.
- Highs run too high and lows too low — expect regression toward the mean.
- Find founder peers — they understand loads others miss.
- Separate identity from company outcomes — personal worth remains distinct.
- Ask for help — many founders do, quietly.

## Common mistakes

- Building in stealth too long — secrecy rarely is the moat.
- Hiring senior too early — expensive operators need infrastructure and clarity.
- Chasing press over customers.
- Raising too much and losing discipline — constraints can preserve focus.
- Optimizing for valuation theater over real progress.
