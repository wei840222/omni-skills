# Goal-setting knowledge

## Goal-setting theory

Goal-setting research by Edwin A. Locke and Gary P. Latham associates specific, challenging goals with stronger performance than vague “do your best” directions, subject to commitment, feedback, task knowledge, and situational constraints. Use the theory to help the user define a clear outcome, calibrate challenge to their circumstances, and review evidence rather than to impose an acronym or rigid formula.

Core considerations:

1. **Clarity:** make the desired outcome observable.
2. **Challenge:** set a meaningful level that remains feasible in the user’s context.
3. **Commitment:** connect the goal to the user’s own reason for pursuing it.
4. **Feedback:** use reviews and progress evidence for course correction.
5. **Task complexity:** break complex goals into milestones that reduce overload.

## Implementation intentions

Implementation intentions are if-then plans that link a likely situation to a chosen action. Use them for a concrete recurring blocker: “If it is 8:00 AM on Sunday, then I will review my goals.” Revisit the plan when the situation or constraint changes.
