# Yoga practice guide

Use this guide for general education and adaptable practice cues. It does not replace an assessment from a qualified instructor or health professional.

## Alignment principles

- Stack joints for stability: place the ankle under the knee and the knee under the hip in standing work.
- Establish length before depth: extend the spine before folding or twisting.
- Keep a small bend in standing knees when a locked knee would hyperextend.
- Let shoulders move away from the ears; press through the hands to support the upper back.
- Root through contact points to lengthen through the crown.

## Modifications and props

- Use blocks to bring the floor closer while keeping useful alignment.
- Bend the knees in forward folds when hamstrings or the back of the body are limiting spinal length.
- Use a wall for balance work so attention can stay on the pose.
- Use a strap to extend a comfortable reach; choose a half bind when a full bind compromises the shoulder.
- Treat child's pose as an available rest option.
- Place a block under the hand in triangle or the sitting bones in seated work; use blankets for kneeling, under the head, or under the hips; use a bolster for supported fish or under the knees in savasana.

## Breath and cueing

- Use a gentle ujjayi-style breath only when it remains comfortable; lengthen or open on an inhale and deepen or twist on an exhale.
- Let steady breathing set the effort level. If the breath becomes labored, reduce depth, load, or duration before continuing.
- Cue the action to take: “reach the arms up,” “bring the navel toward the spine,” or “track the knee over the second toe.”
- Offer a clear option for each side and name right or left explicitly.

## Common form checks

| Pose | Check | Adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downward Dog | Shoulders crowd the ears or the back rounds into a “banana” shape. | Press the floor away, rotate upper arms outward, and bend the knees to lengthen the spine; heels need not reach the floor. |
| Warrior I | The front knee travels past the ankle or loses its second-toe track. | Adjust stance length and width; guide the knee over the second toe. |
| Chaturanga | Shoulders drop below elbows or elbows wing outward. | Keep shoulders at elbow height and draw elbows toward the ribs. |
| Forward Fold | The low back rounds before the hips hinge. | Bend knees and hinge from the hips while maintaining spinal length. |

## Sequencing and style selection

1. Start with gentle movement and breath connection, such as cat/cow or accessible sun-salutation variations.
2. Build toward standing poses and balance work after the body is warm.
3. Place the most demanding pose after preparation, then cool down with seated poses, twists, and forward folds.
4. Finish with a rest period that suits the practitioner; longer savasana is useful when time and comfort allow.

| Style | Typical emphasis | Useful when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hatha | Alignment and held shapes | Learning fundamentals or moving deliberately |
| Vinyasa | Breath-linked flow | Seeking a more continuous, active practice |
| Yin | Longer, supported holds | Exploring quieter flexibility work |
| Restorative | Full prop support and rest | Recovery and low-intensity practice |
| Ashtanga | Set, demanding sequences | An experienced practitioner wants structured endurance work |
| Hot yoga | Heat plus physical practice | Only when heat tolerance and hydration are appropriate |

## Progression and safety

- Begin with a sustainable schedule and increase frequency, duration, or intensity gradually; consistency matters more than forcing range.
- Use discomfort as a signal to adjust and stop for sharp pain, dizziness, numbness, or other concerning symptoms.
- Practice under qualified instruction when learning unfamiliar poses. New practitioners should start with accessible variations rather than extreme positions.
- Ask a health professional and the instructor for individualized modifications when pregnancy, age-related balance concerns, injury, severe high blood pressure, glaucoma, lumbar-spine disease, or knee/hip conditions affect practice.
- During pregnancy, avoid hot yoga because overheating is a concern; discuss suitable movement and position changes with a health professional.
- Treat inversions, deep backbends, and loaded wrist or neck positions as progressions requiring preparation and an option to stop.

## Practice mindset

- Use drishti, a steady gaze point, when it supports balance and focus.
- Compare sensation with pain: use a tolerable range and adjust rather than competing with another person or a previous practice.
- Expect flexibility to change gradually over weeks or months; supporting strength may adapt sooner.
