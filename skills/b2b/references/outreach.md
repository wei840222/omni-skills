# Outreach Practices — B2B

Load when drafting or reviewing prospecting touches. Keep claims tied to verified research; label unknowns instead of inventing customer logos or percentage lifts.

## Personalization tiers

| Tier | Effort | Typical use |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **1:1** | Deep research, fully custom | Strategic accounts / high ACV |
| **1:few** | Segment-specific hooks | Industry or persona clusters |
| **1:many** | Templates + tight variables | Volume prospecting after ICP filter |

## 3×3 research method (time-boxed)

Aim for **up to three relevant insights in about three minutes** when public data exists:

1. Company news, filings, product launches, or hiring spikes
2. Prospect’s public posts, talks, or stated priorities
3. Industry pressure that makes the problem timely

If the clock runs out with fewer than three solid insights, ship a narrower note on what you verified and ask the user for internal context—do not pad with generic flattery.

## Cold email structure

```text
Subject: specific + curiosity (initiative, metric, or trigger)

[Verified observation — 1 sentence]
[Problem you address — 1 sentence, no feature dump]
[Proof only if real and named by the user or public — 1 sentence]
[Single clear CTA question]

[Name]
```

**Replace these weak openers:**

| Avoid leading with | Prefer |
|--------------------|--------|
| “I hope this finds you well” | The verified observation |
| Product autobiography | Buyer problem + relevance |
| Multiple CTAs | One low-friction question |
| “I noticed you work at…” | A concrete initiative or trigger |

## Cold call frame

1. Pattern interrupt — open differently than a scripted pitch
2. Permission — check timing
3. Reason — why them, why now (from research)
4. Interest — one relevant challenge question
5. CTA — book a next step; leave closing the deal for later stages

## Follow-up cadence (default heuristic)

Calibrate to channel norms and any user-supplied sequence. Example review ladder:

| Day | Action | Note |
|-----|--------|------|
| 0 | Initial email | Research-backed |
| 3 | Follow-up with new value | New insight or asset |
| 7 | Different channel | LinkedIn or phone |
| 14 | Break-up / permission close | Creates clarity |
| 30 | Nurture touch | Long-term value only |

Honor explicit opt-out immediately. Silence alone is not a permanent “no,” but after the break-up note, move the account to nurture rather than daily pings.

## Subject-line patterns

- Mutual connection context (only if true)
- Question about a **specific** initiative
- Competitor or market trigger (factual, non-slanderous)
- Idea tied to a stated goal
- Metric language the account already uses publicly

Prefer those over empty “Quick question,” “Following up,” or “Checking in.”

## Evidence rules for drafts

- Do **not** invent customer names, dollar savings, or “reduced X by Y%” claims.
- If the user supplies a case study, quote it tightly and keep it optional.
- If research is thin, output a short draft plus a bullet list of missing inputs required for a stronger version.
