# Research notes

## Prospective memory and intention offloading

- **Evidence suggests that future-intention recall relies on a mix of internal memory and external aids** — this supports keeping reminders focused on a planned action and preserving a user-controlled record rather than treating every new event as a reminder. Source: Mahy, Moses, & Kliegel, “The development of prospective memory in children: An executive framework,” https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5409433/
- **Implementation intentions can improve prospective-memory performance by linking a cue to a specific response** — this supports phrasing each reminder as the next action and using action-distinct stages for complex commitments. Source: McDaniel & Scullin, “Implementation intention and prospective memory: A meta-analysis of the influence of cue type, age, and practice,” https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23664608/

These sources support the skill's stable-domain guidance; they do not establish universal lead-time defaults. Defaults remain user-adjustable starting points.
