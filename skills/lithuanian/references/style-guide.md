# Lithuanian style guide

Use this reference after selecting the audience and register in `SKILL.md`. Examples illustrate choices; match the user's wording and the recipient rather than forcing every feature into an output.

## Formality and address

- **Casual / known peer:** `Labas`, `Sveikas` / `Sveika`, and second-person singular `tu` can fit.
- **Professional, institutional, unfamiliar, older, or explicitly formal:** retain `Jūs` and formal greetings such as `Laba diena` where appropriate.
- Keep address, verb forms, greeting, and closing in the same register. Ask for the intended audience when it changes the choice materially.

## Conversational rhythm

Particles can make a casual message sound less translated when used sparingly:

| Marker | Typical effect | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gi` | shared emphasis | `Kas gi čia?` |
| `juk` | shared or obvious context | `Juk žinai.` |
| `nu`, `tai`, `va` | conversational flow | `Nu, tai susitinkam vėliau.` |
| `žinok`, `klausyk` | direct conversational lead-in | `Žinok, truputį vėluosiu.` |

A message does not need a filler or particle to be natural. In formal or concise writing, use none.

## Expressiveness and diminutives

- Prefer the intensity that matches the source: `labai gerai` can remain straightforward; `žiauriai gerai`, `super`, `nerealu`, or `kosmosas` fit only a clearly casual audience.
- Diminutives can signal warmth: `kavutė`, `arbatėlė`, `pinigėliai`. Use them for a relationship and context that support them; they can sound ironic, childish, or overly familiar elsewhere.
- Peer slang such as `seni`, `seniuk`, `blemba`, and `blin` is audience-specific. Keep it out of professional, institutional, sensitive, or mixed-audience text.

## Syntax and output check

Lithuanian verb inflection often makes subject pronouns unnecessary. Prefer a clear natural sentence over a word-for-word English order, while preserving emphasis and facts.

Before delivery:

1. Confirm `tu` or `Jūs` matches the recipient and remains consistent.
2. Remove any filler, slang, diminutive, or intensifier that changes the speaker's implied relationship or tone.
3. Keep names, dates, quantities, commitments, and safety-sensitive wording faithful to the source.
4. For publication, legal, medical, or brand-critical text, label this as a draft and route it to a qualified native reviewer when one is available.

## Verified language resources

For terminology, orthography, or a high-stakes wording decision, consult a current authoritative resource before finalizing rather than treating this guide's examples as a substitute for expert review:

- **Lithuanian Language Resources Information System (E. KALBA):** https://lkiis.lki.lt/ — language-resource portal maintained by the Institute of the Lithuanian Language.
- **E. KALBA Standard Lithuanian Dictionary:** https://ekalba.lt/bendrine-lietuviu-kalbos-zodynas — check established meanings and standard forms.
- **State Commission of the Lithuanian Language consultations:** https://www.vlkk.lt/konsultacijos — consult current official language recommendations when the question is normative.

If an authoritative source, a qualified native reviewer, or the user's style guide conflicts with a casual example here, follow the applicable source and explain the register choice succinctly.
