# Schedule and Capacity

Most academies do not have a marketing problem first. They have a capacity design problem that makes growth unstable.

## Capacity Equation

Track capacity as:
- seats available
- seats sold
- minimum viable class size
- teacher hours available
- room or live-slot availability

Revenue plans that ignore one of these will break later.

## Before Opening a New Group

Check:
- who teaches it
- when it runs
- where it happens
- how many students are needed to keep margin healthy
- whether it steals students from another group

## Timetable Rules

- protect prime-time slots for highest-demand offers
- keep transitions realistic between classes, rooms, or calls
- prefer standardized slots that serve many families over one-off custom times
- standardize make-up rules so support does not negotiate every absence manually

## Utilization Signals

Watch for:
- classes consistently near zero occupancy
- one teacher overloaded while others stay underused
- too many cancellations in a specific slot
- waitlists in one level and empty seats in another

These are structural signals, not just daily annoyances.

## Multi-Program Conflicts

Programs compete for:
- rooms
- teacher attention
- admin follow-up
- student energy and family schedule

When conflicts appear, rank offers by:
1. margin
2. strategic importance
3. retention value
4. staffing difficulty

## Decision Rule

If a scheduling fix makes operations simpler but hurts the student experience badly, it will return later as churn.

Choose the simplest plan that still feels reliable and fair to students and teachers.

## Evidence anchors

- **Utilization beats headline rate:** tutor or teacher economics follow *paid student time ÷ paid staff time*. Cancellations, prep gaps, and subject mismatch raise true labor cost even when the list price looks healthy.
- **Planning bands for staff utilization:** roughly **55–65%** is common during launch or weak cancellation control; **70–82%** is often a healthier steady state; **85%+** can look efficient while creating burnout, weak prep, and churn.
- **Delivered sessions, not scheduled sessions:** break-even planning should use delivered sessions and contribution per session after staff pay, payment fees, and materials.
- **Peak-hour clustering:** evening demand often concentrates in a few prime windows. Idle off-peak rooms or underfilled groups are structural occupancy problems, not marketing failures.
- **Course capacity headroom:** many education programs run with unused seats; fill empty seats in existing slots before opening low-occupancy fringe groups.

## Failure recovery

| Trigger | First repair | If still failing |
|---|---|---|
| New group requested without teacher/room/min size | Block open enrollment until all three are named | Offer waitlist into an existing viable slot |
| One teacher overloaded, peers underused | Rebalance groups by level and travel/transition time | Hire or reduce offers that only the overloaded teacher can run |
| Class below minimum viable size for two cycles | Merge, retarget, or close; protect margin and teacher morale | Keep only if strategic pipeline value is explicit and funded |
| Waitlist in one level, empty seats in adjacent level | Run placement review and transfer offers | Adjust marketing to the real inventory |
