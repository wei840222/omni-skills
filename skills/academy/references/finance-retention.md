# Finance and Retention

Academies usually think growth first and collections later. The stronger move is to design pricing and retention together.

## Pricing Structure

Clarify:
- one-time fee vs recurring fee
- deposit requirements
- installment logic
- pause policy
- make-up policy
- refund boundaries

If these are fuzzy, support and finance end up negotiating manually.

## Cash Collection Rules

Track a short ladder:
1. payment due
2. soft reminder
3. failed payment follow-up
4. escalation before the next class block
5. pause or stop according to policy

Make the ladder explicit so nobody improvises.

## Discount Discipline

Discounts should have a reason:
- early commitment
- sibling or volume logic
- low-demand slot fill
- strategic scholarship

Random discounts train the market to wait and hurt trust with current students.

## Retention Economics

The most useful numbers are often:
- average lifetime value
- renewal rate
- occupancy by class
- delinquency rate
- revenue per teacher hour

Those numbers show whether the model is healthy, not just whether money came in this month.

## Save vs Exit

Not every student should be saved.

Try to save when:
- the issue is schedule, onboarding, teacher fit, or payment friction

Let go cleanly when:
- the academy is the wrong fit
- expectations are impossible
- behavior hurts staff or class quality

Good exits protect reputation and team energy.

## Evidence anchors

- **Retention leverage:** classic service economics show small retention gains can move profit disproportionately; treat renewal and save motions as growth work, not back-office cleanup.
- **Re-enrollment timing:** start enrollment/re-enrollment communication **60–90 days** before the earliest due date, inside the channels families already use for schedules and progress.
- **Policy simplicity:** a few clear payment plans plus autopay beat a maze of one-off exceptions that staff cannot administer evenly.
- **Early delinquency talks:** wait until a family is multiple periods behind and catch-up becomes unlikely. Use a written ladder, offer partial-payment plans, and keep academic and billing owners coordinated.
- **Internal collection rhythm (adapt to local law):** clear invoice → soft reminders in the first period → personal outreach with options → formal notice with stated consequences → pause/stop or external referral only per published policy.

## Failure recovery

| Trigger | First repair | If still failing |
|---|---|---|
| Failed payment before next class block | Soft reminder + one-click retry + documented plan options | Escalate per ladder; coordinate with student-risk owner if learning risk is also present |
| Discount requests during slow fill | Prefer slot-fill or early-commit logic with an end date | Reject open-ended discounts that train the market to wait |
| High refunds after week two | Audit promise vs delivery, placement, and onboarding | Freeze the offer narrative until delivery can keep it |
| Strong acquisition, weak LTV | Rebuild renewal path with progress evidence and next-level offer | Cut low-retention programs or redesign packaging |
