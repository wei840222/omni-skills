# Admissions and Enrollment

Admissions is not just lead capture. It is the system that turns the right prospects into students without breaking delivery.

## Funnel Stages

Use a simple progression:
1. inquiry
2. qualified lead
3. assessment or trial
4. offer sent
5. deposit or payment
6. enrolled
7. onboarded

Each stage needs:
- owner
- expected response time
- exit criteria
- next action

## Qualification Questions

Before pushing an offer, confirm:
- student age or level
- goal and urgency
- preferred format and schedule
- budget reality
- decision-maker
- blockers that could kill enrollment later

## Response Standards

- inbound inquiries should get a useful reply fast, not just a generic acknowledgment
- trials and assessments need a defined follow-up window
- every lead ends the day with a next step or a clear disqualification

## Offer Discipline

Send complete offers only.

Every offer should state:
- program or class
- start date
- frequency
- duration
- price
- payment terms
- what happens if they pause or miss sessions

## Enrollment Guardrails

Enroll only when the class has:
- healthy occupancy economics
- an assigned teacher
- room and timetable certainty
- clear onboarding

A misaligned enrollment often costs more than waiting for a fit.

## Common Conversion Leaks

- slow response after inquiry
- trial felt good but there was no immediate close sequence
- pricing was discussed without anchoring the outcome
- schedule mismatch discovered too late
- parents or decision-makers were not included early enough

## Best Next Move

When the user asks for growth help, first find whether the real leak is:
- lead quality
- conversion script
- offer clarity
- schedule fit
- trust and proof

Default to funnel diagnosis before buying more ads.

## Evidence anchors

Use these as diagnostic baselines, then replace with the academy's own funnel data:

- **Speed-to-first-contact:** measure a live useful human contact rather than an auto-reply. Set a response target that the staffed operating hours can sustain, then improve it with the academy's own response-time and conversion data.
- **Stage definitions matter:** calculate lead-to-enroll and applicant-to-enroll separately because they use different denominators. Compare a rate only to the same stage in the academy's own history.
- **Show-up risk:** track no-shows by appointment type; treat confirmation, calendar hold, and same-day ping as part of the offer system when the academy's data shows a preventable no-show pattern.
- **Payment friction:** when cash timing—not fit—is the blocker, make approved payment terms visible inside the offer rather than surprising the prospect at checkout.

## Failure recovery

| Trigger | First repair | If still failing |
|---|---|---|
| Inquiry older than same business day with no useful reply | Owner sends a specific next-step message within the hour and logs stage + due time | Reassign owner; add after-hours coverage rule |
| Trial booked but unconfirmed | Confirm time, location/link, prep, and decision-maker attendance | Offer one alternate slot; if no reply, mark disqualified with reopen path |
| Offer sent with vague terms | Rewrite offer with program, start, frequency, price, payment, pause/miss rules | Require manager review before resend |
| Conversion weak while lead volume is high | Audit speed-to-contact, show-up, schedule fit, and decision-maker inclusion before buying more ads | Freeze paid acquisition until the leak is named |
