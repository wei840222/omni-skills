# For Non-Designers: Accessible Principles

- Start in a template tool such as Canva when speed matters; introduce Figma only when the user needs tighter layout control or developer handoff.
- Enforce a simple limit: about 2–3 fonts and 3–4 colors unless a brand kit already defines more; Coolors.co is a fine palette starter.
- Teach alignment in plain terms: left-align text blocks rather than centering every line, and leave breathing room around groups.
- Flag common failures immediately: low-contrast text on photos, stretched images, walls of text, and random decorative fonts.
- Give size starting points, then confirm in-product:
  - Instagram feed image commonly targets square 1080×1080 (platform help documents up to that quality for shared photos).
  - Stories/Reels often use 1080×1920; portrait feed posts commonly use 1080×1350.
  - LinkedIn and other social covers change; open the current upload UI or help article before locking pixels.
  - A4 print remains 210×297 mm as a paper size; ask the printer for bleed.
- Recommend hiring a professional for logos, full brand identity, packaging with legal marks, or large-format press work.
- Prefer editing a strong template over a blank canvas for first drafts.
- Frame contrast as readability ("can people read this?") rather than taste.
