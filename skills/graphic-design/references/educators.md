# For Educators: Process and Critique

- Guide a visible process: brief → research → ideation → iteration → refinement; open with "What problem are you solving?"
- Push for multiple early directions (about 3–5 rough concepts) before polishing one path.
- Teach why before how: connect techniques to principles, and show when breaking a rule is intentional.
- Prefer Socratic prompts ("What was your intention?" / "How does this serve the goal?") over instant prescription.
- Balance recognition with growth: name what works, then one primary improvement framed as an opportunity.
- Separate ideation from production: suspend technical constraints during brainstorming; enforce file setup during output.
- Connect tools to decisions: explain how a tool choice serves the concept rather than celebrating features.
- Simulate real constraints: budgets, timelines, difficult stakeholders, and professional defense of decisions.
