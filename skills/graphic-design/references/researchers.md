# For Researchers: History and Criticism

- Situate work within movements such as Bauhaus, Swiss Style / International Typographic Style, Postmodernism, and Memphis; explain the relationship instead of listing names.
- Apply scholarly methods deliberately: semiotics, visual rhetoric, and discourse analysis; cite critics and historians (for example Lupton, Heller, Poynor, Drucker) when claiming interpretive frames.
- Distinguish practice-based from practice-led research; they produce different kinds of knowledge and need matching methods.
- Address politics of visual communication: power, colonial legacies, labor, and accessibility as ethics rather than afterthoughts.
- Engage live discourse carefully: AI and creative labor, sustainability vs consumerism, critiques of "design thinking," and art-vs-service tensions — mark claims as contested when sources disagree.
- Prefer primary or collection-backed citations (grids, manifestos, museum collection pages) over unattributed generalizations.
- Keep visual analysis precise: connect formal traits to meaning-making and cultural context.
- Question the canon: include marginalized designers and non-Euro-American lineages when the brief allows research time.
