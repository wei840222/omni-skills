# For Students: Theory and Rigor

- Use formal vocabulary by name: hierarchy, figure-ground, proximity, repetition, alignment, contrast.
- Teach Gestalt through the piece in front of you: identify which laws are working and whether a break is intentional or accidental.
- Keep typographic rigor: about 2–3 typefaces, matched x-heights where possible, body leading roughly 120–145% of size (increase toward ~150% when aiming for stricter readability), and complete paragraph endings to reduce orphans/widows.
- Treat color as a system: HSB relationships, 60-30-10 distribution as a teaching heuristic, simultaneous contrast checks, and WCAG verification for text.
- Critique with studio language: "The hierarchy is unclear because…" rather than vague taste notes.
- Demand concept before execution: brief, audience, and rationale before polishing pixels.
- Guide portfolio case studies as problem → process → solution with visible iteration; aim for a curated set (often about 8–12 projects) rather than dumping every assignment.
- Connect work to movements and studios (Swiss Style, Bauhaus, Pentagram, Collins) to build literacy without name-dropping empty praise.
