## Jakob Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics
*Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/*

1. **Visibility of system status:** The design should always keep users informed about what is going on, through appropriate feedback within a reasonable amount of time.
2. **Match between system and the real world:** The design should speak the users' language. Use words, phrases, and concepts familiar to the user, and use terminology they understand.
3. **User control and freedom:** Users often perform actions by mistake. They need a clearly marked "emergency exit" to leave the unwanted action without having to go through an extended process.
4. **Consistency and standards:** Ensure different words, situations, and actions consistently mean the same thing. Follow platform and industry conventions.
5. **Error prevention:** Good error messages are important, and you must proactively prevent problems from occurring.
6. **Recognition rather than recall:** Minimize the user's memory load by making elements, actions, and options visible. Maintain necessary context visibly so the user can rely on recognition.
7. **Flexibility and efficiency of use:** Shortcuts — hidden from novice users — may speed up the interaction for the expert user.
8. **Aesthetic and minimalist design:** Remove irrelevant or rarely needed information to keep the interface minimalist.
9. **Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors:** Error messages should be expressed in plain language (no error codes), precisely indicate the problem, and constructively suggest a solution.
10. **Help and documentation:** It's best if the system doesn't need any additional explanation. However, it may be necessary to provide documentation to help users understand how to complete their tasks.

## Cognitive Load Theory in UX
*Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/minimize-cognitive-load/*

- **Intrinsic Cognitive Load:** The effort required to understand the task itself. Reduce this by simplifying the underlying concepts when possible.
- **Extraneous Cognitive Load:** The effort required to process the interface. Reduce this by relying on established mental models, grouping related information, and maintaining visual clarity.
- **Germane Cognitive Load:** The effort required to learn new patterns. Keep this strictly focused on the core value proposition of the product.

## Friction Reduction and Progressive Disclosure
- **Smart defaults:** Reduce decisions by providing a good default rather than more options.
- **Pre-fill from context:** Location, previous selections, account data.
- **Auto-save progress:** Preserve user work continuously.
- **Progressive Disclosure:** Show only what's needed for current task—hide advanced options until relevant.
- **Empty states:** Guide to first action rather than showing "Nothing here."

## Edge Cases and Reversibility
- **Empty state:** First time, cleared, filtered with no results.
- **Loading state:** Skeleton preferred over spinner for known layouts.
- **Error state:** Explain what went wrong and how to recover.
- **Partial state:** Some data available, some loading/failed.
- **Offline state:** Clarify what works, what's queued, and what's unavailable.
- **Trash over permanent delete:** Recovery possible.
- **Preview before commit:** Show the effect of an action.
- **Draft states for complex work:** Allow users to pause and resume work in multiple sessions.
