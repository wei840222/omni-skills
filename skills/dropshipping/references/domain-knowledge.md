# Dropshipping Domain Knowledge

## Concept and Economics

Drop shipping is a retail fulfillment model in which the seller accepts customer orders without holding inventory. The seller forwards order and shipping details to a manufacturer, wholesaler, another retailer, or fulfillment partner that ships directly to the customer.

Economics depend on cost of goods, shipping, payment/platform fees, marketing CAC, returns, and chargebacks. Real margin must include all of those costs—not just product + shipping.

## Market Growth

Cross-border ecommerce and low inventory requirements continue to attract new sellers, but competition and fee stacking keep many niches thin-margin. Treat market-size headlines as directional context only; validate niche demand and unit economics before scaling ads.

## Risk Factors

- **Quality control**: The seller owns the customer relationship but has limited control over storage, packing, and shipping quality.
- **Profit margins**: Missing bulk discounts and middleman fees usually compress margins versus holding inventory.
- **Supply chain**: Overseas suppliers commonly quote 15–30 day shipping; stockouts and tracking gaps are operational defaults to plan for, not exceptions.

## History

Amazon’s early catalog strategy used a drop-shipping-like model for long-tail books: a small set of popular titles stayed in stock while many other titles were fulfilled by publishers/wholesalers shipping in Amazon packaging.
