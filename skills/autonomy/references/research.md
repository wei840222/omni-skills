# Human-Automation Trust Research

Use this reference when explaining why autonomy should expand through a consented pilot instead of an unbounded takeover.

## Calibrated trust and reliance

- NIST's AI Risk Management Framework treats human-AI interaction and oversight as governance concerns. For this skill, translate that into visible task boundaries, meaningful human approval, and reviewable records rather than implied standing authority.
- Lee and See's review of trust in automation describes calibrated trust: reliance should match the system's demonstrated capability. Start with a bounded pilot and use its observed outcomes before proposing a wider authority level.
- Parasuraman, Sheridan, and Wickens describe automation as a spectrum across functions and levels. Keep the delegated function and level explicit, rather than treating "autonomous" as a blanket permission.

## Applying the research

1. Use only evidence surfaced in the active conversation to identify a candidate task.
2. Propose a limited pilot with a measurable success signal and a route back to the previous level.
3. Ask for explicit approval before the pilot and again before expanding its scope.
4. Report what happened at the agreed cadence so the user can calibrate reliance from evidence.

## Sources

- Lee, J. D.; See, K. A., *Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance*: https://doi.org/10.1518/001872008X288574
- Parasuraman, R.; Sheridan, T. B.; Wickens, C. D., *A Model for Types and Levels of Human Interaction with Automation*: https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354
