# Client Relationship Management

## Engagement and Commercial Models

Client engagements typically follow several models depending on the structure, timeframe, and deliverables involved:

1. **Fixed-Price / Project-Based**: Suitable when scope is strictly defined with clear deliverables and a specific end date. This transfers the risk of scope creep to the provider unless mitigated with rigorous change orders.
2. **Monthly Retainer**: Provides continuous availability or services on a subscription basis. Retainers should cap hours or specifically define scope to prevent them from acting as unbounded employment arrangements.
3. **Hourly / Day Rate**: Usually applied when scope cannot be predetermined. While flexible, it places an income cap relative to hours worked and penalizes efficiency.
4. **Value-Based Pricing**: Fees are based on the quantifiable value or outcome delivered rather than time spent. This requires strong alignment on baseline metrics before work begins.

## Core Best Practices

- **Explicit Scope and Change Management**: Never absorb "small extras" without documentation. Every deviation should be processed as a paid change order or explicitly logged to justify future renewals.
- **Durable Documentation**: Client histories, including emails, contacts, pricing, and project milestones, must be maintained locally rather than relying on memory or unstructured chat logs.
- **Boundary Defense**: Strong relationships are maintained by setting correct expectations at kickoff (response times, communication channels, working days) and defending them continuously.

## Financial Risk Management

- **Concentration Limits**: A single client should ideally not exceed 25-30% of trailing-12-month revenue to mitigate existential risk if the relationship ends.
- **Payment Ladders**: Do not wait until cash is short to chase invoices. Implement a fixed procedural ladder that starts at the due date and escalates regardless of immediate financial need.
