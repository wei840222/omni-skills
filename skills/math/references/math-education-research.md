# Mathematics education reference

Read this reference when selecting instruction for persistent misconceptions, designing a lesson sequence, or deciding whether a student needs more representation, practice, or diagnostic questions.

## Evidence-informed teaching sequence

1. Elicit the learner's current strategy with a worked example or a brief explanation request.
2. Use concrete objects or visual representations when the learner needs to connect quantity to notation; then connect representations explicitly to symbols.
3. Model one worked example, have the learner complete a closely related step, and reduce support as they demonstrate understanding.
4. Use a short check that targets the specific misconception before moving to a new procedure.
5. For multi-step work, ask the learner to explain the purpose of each step; distinguish a remembered procedure from a justified transformation.

## Common misconception checks

| Topic | Diagnostic prompt | Productive response |
|---|---|---|
| Equality | “What does `3 + 4 = __ + 5` mean?” | Treat `=` as a relation between equal quantities, not an instruction to compute only the left side. |
| Fractions | “Why is `1/2 + 1/3` not `2/5`?” | Use common-sized parts or an area model, then derive a common denominator. |
| Exponents | “Does `(a + b)^2` equal `a^2 + b^2`?” | Expand the product and identify the cross term `2ab`. |
| Square roots | “Does `sqrt(a + b)` equal `sqrt(a) + sqrt(b)`?” | Test a small numerical counterexample and discuss when algebraic identities apply. |

## Sources

### U.S. Institute of Education Sciences — practice guidance

- **Assisting Students Struggling with Mathematics: Intervention in the Elementary Grades** — https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Docs/PracticeGuide/rti_math_pg_042109.pdf
  - Key takeaway: use explicit instruction, visual representations, deliberate practice, and regular cumulative review for students who struggle with mathematics.
- **Teaching Math to Young Children** — https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/21
  - Key takeaway: build foundational number knowledge through intentional instruction, mathematical language, and guided practice.

### National Council of Teachers of Mathematics — instructional principles

- **Principles to Actions: Ensuring Mathematical Success for All** — https://www.nctm.org/PtA/
  - Key takeaway: establish clear goals, implement tasks that promote reasoning, connect representations, facilitate discourse, and use evidence of student thinking.
