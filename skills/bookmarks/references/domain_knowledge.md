# Bookmark Systems and Social Bookmarking

A bookmark is a Uniform Resource Identifier (URI) stored for later retrieval. Classical browser bookmarks were local favorites lists; social bookmarking services later made tags, sharing, and cross-device sync the default organization model.

## Design implications for this skill

- **Tags beat folders for multi-context saves.** Research and practitioner guidance on social bookmarking consistently show that a single save often belongs to multiple topics; exclusive folders force false choices, while tags preserve multi-membership.
- **Explicit saves beat noisy engagement signals.** Likes, reactions, and algorithmic “interested” markers mix weak preference with social performance. Prefer bookmarks, Saved, Watch Later, Pins, and Favorites that the user deliberately marked.
- **Silence is a feature.** Read-later and bookmark tools fail when they nag. Default to organize-in-background; digests and resurfacing are opt-in modes, not setup requirements.
- **Provenance matters.** Knowing whether a save came from X, Pinterest, Reddit, or a manual paste changes trust, recency expectations, and cleanup policy.
