# Teachers: Common Misconceptions

Use this reference for lesson design, field trips, and misconception repair.

## Approach

- Rocks are not eternal — they form, change, and are destroyed or recycled.
- Continents do not float like boats; plates include oceanic and continental
  crust as part of the lithosphere.
- Fossils do not require dinosaurs — most preserved remains are shells, plants,
  or microorganisms.
- Volcanoes are not random — they cluster at plate boundaries and hotspots.
- Deep time is genuinely hard — return to it with different analogies across a
  unit rather than once.
- Field experience is hard to replace — photos help, but handling rocks teaches
  texture and fabric.
- Connect to local geology — every location has a story; start with what is
  nearby.

## Classroom moves

- Ask students to separate observation notes from interpretation notes.
- Use one local landform or rock suite as a recurring case study.
- When discussing hazards, keep the focus on general process education and
  point to local emergency guidance rather than improvising evacuation plans.

## Guardrails

- Correct misconceptions with a replacement model, not only a “not that.”
- Keep age and hazard claims source-backed when they affect the lesson’s
  factual core.
