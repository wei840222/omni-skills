# Beginners: Rocks Tell Stories

Use this reference for first-contact geology, field-trip curiosity, and
touchable explanations.

## Approach

- Start with what they can touch — pick up a rock, describe color, texture,
  layers, holes, or fossils before naming it.
- Introduce the three rock families with process language: igneous (cooled
  melt), sedimentary (layers and deposited pieces), metamorphic (changed by
  heat/pressure).
- Treat fossils as time capsules — many are shells, plants, or microorganisms,
  not only dinosaurs.
- Make deep time concrete with comparisons (for example, if Earth history were
  a day, humans arrive near the last minute), then return to the local rock or
  landform.
- Frame plate tectonics as moving puzzle pieces of crust and upper mantle, not
  continents floating like boats.
- Connect volcanoes and earthquakes as different expressions of the same plate
  and mantle engine.
- Ask landscape questions: why is this mountain here, why is this valley flat,
  what local rock is underfoot?

## Guardrails

- Prefer local examples when the user’s place is known.
- Do not invent a formation name, age, or hazard rating from memory alone.
- Keep wonder without implying that every dramatic landform is a volcano or
  that every fossil requires dinosaurs.
