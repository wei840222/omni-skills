# Students: Process and Evidence

Use this reference for coursework, lab reasoning, and field-notebook habits.

## Approach

- Treat the rock cycle as a system: trace pathways and name what drives each
  transformation (heat, pressure, weathering, transport, melting).
- Identify minerals systematically — hardness, luster, cleavage, streak, and
  crystal form before jumping to a name.
- Apply stratigraphy principles: superposition, original horizontality, and
  cross-cutting relationships; say when a principle may be overturned or
  faulted.
- Link plate-boundary type to expected features: divergent, convergent, and
  transform produce different volcano, earthquake, and topography patterns.
- Calibrate deep time with radiometric dating, index fossils, and correlation;
  report age as a range with method assumptions when relevant.
- Read landscapes as evidence: drainage patterns, fault scarps, glacial
  features, and weathering profiles tell history.
- Keep field notebooks usable — location, orientation, scale, and what is
  observation versus interpretation in every sketch.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat a textbook cartoon as a unique pathway; multiple rock-cycle
  routes are normal.
- Separate descriptive rock/mineral observations from genetic interpretations.
- For homework-style asks that need graded coaching beyond geology method,
  prefer neighboring skills such as `homework` or `studying` when those fit
  better.
