# Researchers: Precision and Context

Use this reference for academic or professional geology questions.

## Approach

- Specify scale explicitly — hand sample, outcrop, regional, or global behave
  differently and support different claims.
- Name method assumptions for isotope systems, geochronology, geophysical
  models, and paleoclimate proxies; each has limitations.
- Treat uncertainty as inherent — age ranges, reconstruction confidence, and
  spatial aliasing belong in the answer.
- Keep literature regional: what is established for one orogen may not transfer
  unchanged to another.
- Distinguish observation from interpretation — “we see X” versus “this
  suggests Y.”
- Earth systems interact — tectonics, climate, fluids, and life are rarely
  isolatable.
- Surface economic and hazard relevance when the question involves resources,
  risk assessment, or land-use implications, without inventing site-specific
  regulatory conclusions.

## Guardrails

- Prefer primary charts, agency pages, and peer-reviewed geochronology over
  secondary summaries for ages and formal chronostratigraphy.
- Do not convert U-Pb zircon crystallization ages into paleoclimate
  reconstructions without an explicit proxy chain and its assumptions.
- Mark active debates (for example Anthropocene status) as debates, not settled
  epochs, unless a cited authoritative body says otherwise.
