## City snapshot

- Romania's capital and largest city (~1.8M city; larger metro), mixing Belle Époque remnants, communist-era blocks, and rapid post-2000 growth.
- EU member since 2007; currency remains **RON** (leu). EUR is widely accepted in tourist and higher-end zones but is not the legal tender.
- Tech and outsourcing hub (Pipera corridor) with a growing startup scene; English is common in tech/younger cohorts and uneven elsewhere.

## Durable traps

1. **Rush-hour traffic** — 07:00–10:00 and 17:00–20:00 are brutal on main boulevards; metro is usually faster for covered corridors.
2. **Street numbering** — Addresses can be confusing; prefer a map pin over a bare street number when meeting someone.
3. **Airport / night taxis** — Prefer Bolt/Uber or an official stand; treat solicitors in arrivals halls as high-risk.
4. **ATM / FX fees** — Prefer BRD/BCR ATMs or Revolut/Wise-style cards; random ATMs can add fees.
5. **Cash pockets** — Cards are common, but small shops and some markets still expect cash—carry modest RON.
6. **Winter street wear** — Potholes and ice after freeze–thaw cycles; watch footing and car suspension.
7. **District heating schedules** — Older blocks may run on city heating calendars; ask before winter leases.
8. **"5 minutes" flexibility** — Local time estimates often expand; build buffers into appointments.
9. **Sunday hours** — Many shops reduce hours or close; plan errands for weekdays when possible.
10. **Old Town noise** — Centru Vechi / Lipscani nights are loud; pick lodging for sleep needs, not just proximity.

## Language posture

- Romanian is Latin-based and official; English works in tech, hospitality, and many young adults.
- Lead with English in tourist/tech bubbles; switch to Romanian basics (or `romanian` skill) for bureaucracy and neighborhood shops outside those bubbles.
