# Sources — dreams

Gate 6 research anchors for dream recall, journaling practice, and lucid-dream induction. Prefer primary guidance over pop-symbol dictionaries. Re-verify before presenting time-sensitive clinical claims.

## Dream recall and journaling

- [Harvard Health — Dreams](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mind-and-mood/dreams) — accessible overview of dreaming, recall limits, and when dream distress warrants clinical attention.
- [Sleep Foundation — Dream Journal](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/dreams/dream-journal) — practical capture timing, fragment logging, and pattern review habits.
- [American Psychological Association — Dreams and dreaming](https://www.apa.org/topics/sleep/dreams) — psychology framing; separate personal meaning-making from clinical diagnosis.

## Lucid dreaming research and methods

- [LaBerge & Rheingold context via Lucidity Institute methods overview](https://www.lucidity.com/) — MILD/WBTB lineage and reality-check practice notes (treat vendor pages as method references, not medical advice).
- [NIH/PMC literature entry point for lucid dreaming](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=lucid+dreaming) — locate peer-reviewed lucid-dream and nightmare-treatment studies before citing effect sizes.
- [AASM / clinical sleep medicine gateway](https://aasm.org/) — route parasomnia, narcolepsy, and disorder questions to clinical sleep standards rather than dream interpretation.

## Nightmares and safety boundaries

- [Veterans Affairs — Nightmare treatment overview (imagery rehearsal context)](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/type/nightmare_treatment.asp) — evidence-oriented framing for recurring nightmares; do not improvise trauma therapy inside a dream journal skill.
- [NHS — Sleep paralysis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-paralysis/) — patient-facing explanation and when to seek help.

## Practice notes for this skill

- Log first, interpret second; counts come from the user’s journal files.
- Label symbolic readings as optional and plural.
- Chronic insomnia, apnea signs, dream enactment with injury, or trauma-linked nightmare disorder leave this skill’s lane — hand off to `sleep` and real-world clinicians.
- Do not cite closed-access papers by invented quotations; link the index page and quote only text actually retrieved.
