# Turkish Domain Knowledge

Use this file for register, particles, expressiveness, reactions, and delivery checks while drafting Turkish. It describes common conversational patterns; it is not a license to invent intimacy, slang, or a dialect the user did not ask for. Keep glosses aligned with `references/sources.md`.

## Formality Default

Default AI register is often too high. Casual Turkish among peers is warm and direct. Unless the audience is professional, institutional, older, unfamiliar, or explicitly formal, lean casual:

- Prefer `Selam` over stiff `Merhaba` among peers
- Prefer `Tamam` over composed `Peki` in peer chat
- Short affirmations such as `hı hı` can replace a formal `Evet` in speech-like chat when the relationship supports it

For unknown recipients in work or institutional settings, prefer careful `siz` rather than inventing intimacy.

| Situation | Default | Delivery rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official, academic, or unfamiliar institution | `siz` + clearer greetings | Complete sentences; avoid heavy slang and intimate particles |
| Work chat with known colleagues | soft `siz` or established `sen` by workplace norm | Clear and friendly; light particles only if that chat already uses them |
| Friend, peer chat, SMS, or social post | `sen` | Short lines, natural fillers, selective reactions |
| Public or mixed audience | Neutral everyday | Stay readable; skip intimate slang stacks unless requested |

## Sen vs Siz

Critical distinction:

- **`siz`**: elders, professional or institutional contexts, strangers initially
- **`sen`**: friends, peers, most Turkish internet once the relationship is clear
- Overusing `siz` among peers sounds cold; inventing `sen` for unfamiliar formal recipients is a Required failure

Keep pronouns, verb agreement, and greetings aligned with the chosen address for the whole draft. Mid-text `sen`/`siz` drift is a Required failure.

## Particles and Softeners

Use particles for conversational role, not as a quota:

- **`ya`**: softening / emphasis (`Gel ya`, `Ne ya?`)
- **`ki`**: emphasis after an evaluation (`Çok güzel ki`)
- **`işte`**: "well / you see"
- **`ha`**: confirmation seeking
- Spoken casual Turkish often drops formal `-DIr` copular endings

## Fillers and Flow

Optional conversational choices that keep prose from sounding textbook-stiff:

- `yani`, `hani`, `şey`
- `işte`, `aslında`, `mesela`
- `falan`, `filan` ("and so on / whatever")
- `şimdi` as a discourse filler, not only the literal "now"

Keep factual messages direct; insert a filler only where it helps the speaker's flow.

## Casual Shortcuts

Spoken / chat reductions that fit peer register:

- `Değil mi?` → `Di mi?`
- `Nasılsın?` → `Naber?`
- `Bir şey` → `bişey` / `bişi`
- `Ne yapıyorsun?` → `Napıyon?`

Do not force reductions into formal `siz` drafts.

## Expressiveness

Match the source sentiment rather than replacing every ordinary word:

- `İyi` → `Süper` / `Harika` / `Efsane` / `Müthiş` when enthusiasm is intended
- `Kötü` → `Berbat` / `Rezalet`; crude intensifiers only when the source clearly wants them
- `Çok` → `Aşırı` / `Mega` / `Acayip` when intensity still fits

## Common Expressions

Choose expressions that fit the speech act:

- Agreement: `Aynen`, `Aynen öyle`
- Surprise: `Yok artık!`, `Hadi ya!`
- Softeners: `Boşver`, `Takma kafana`
- Everyday courtesy: `Kolay gelsin`, `Afiyet olsun`

## Reactions

Preserve the speaker's actual reaction:

- `Cidden?`, `Ciddi misin?`, `Yok artık!`
- `Vay be!`, `Oha!`, `Eyvah!`
- `Süper!`, `Harika!`, `Efsane!`
- Light `haha` / laughter tokens in chat when the channel already uses them

## Doubling for Emphasis

Turkish often doubles for emphasis when the speaker wants intensity:

- `çok çok güzel`
- `hemen hemen`
- `ufak tefek`

Use doubling to match intensity, not as decoration on every line.

## Religious and Cultural Expressions

These appear naturally in many everyday Turkish conversations when the speaker would use them:

- `İnşallah`, `Maşallah`
- `Allah korusun`, `Allah Allah`
- `Çok şükür`

Use them when they fit the speaker and context; do not stack them as cultural flavoring on every draft.

## Delivery Check

Before sending:

1. Confirm one `sen`/`siz` choice and matching greetings or verb forms.
2. Facts, names, numbers, dates, commitments, and uncertainty from the source are preserved.
3. Particles, fillers, reductions, and slang appear only when the channel and relationship support them.
4. A native reader would not screenshot the draft as stiff textbook Turkish for the chosen register.
5. For legal, medical, financial, or publication-sensitive Turkish, keep the requested formality and recommend a qualified native-speaker review.
