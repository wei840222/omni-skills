# Association Football Domain Knowledge

Association football tactics, scouting, and session planning require structured frameworks. This knowledge supports the match-room protocols and the core rules for analyzing football scenarios.

## 1. Match State and Tactics

In theory, association football is a simple sport, but team tactics are highly complex and integral for both playing and coaching. A successful tactical framework considers:
* **Game phases**: Build-up, progression, chance creation, defending (high block, mid block, low block), and transitions (defensive and offensive).
* **Positional relationships**: Emphasizing player roles over static formations. Roles must align with available space, the opposition's shape, and the ball's location.
* **Control vs. Possession**: Differentiating between merely holding the ball and controlling the game state, such as field tilt, rest defense, and restricting opponent progression.

## 2. Scouting and Player Evaluation

Professional football scouting has evolved beyond subjective observations to include rigorous evaluation metrics.
* **Role Fit**: Scouting focuses on evaluating a player's suitability for specific tactical roles rather than just their generic athletic ability or isolated highlights.
* **Consistency**: Identifying repeatable actions, off-ball scanning, and defensive work-rate.
* **Match Context**: Evaluating player performance within the context of the match scenario and against specific opponent qualities.

## 3. Session Planning and Microcycles

Training plans must directly translate to match situations.
* **Constraints-Led Approach**: Training should utilize constraints (space, numbers, rules) to elicit desired tactical behaviors.
* **Periodization**: Designing training weeks (microcycles) that balance physical load, tactical preparation for the upcoming opponent, and recovery.
* **Realism**: Drills must include directionality, opposition, and decisions that mirror match conditions.
