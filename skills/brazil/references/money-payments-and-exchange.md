# Money, Payments, and Exchange

## Default Payment Model

- In large cities and standard tourism corridors, cards work well for hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, and ride-hailing.
- Keep a small cash buffer for kiosks, beach vendors, small-town purchases, tips, or payment failures.
- Build the payment strategy around redundancy (mix of cards, cash, and app alternatives).

## PIX and CPF Friction

- PIX is dominant in Brazil, but short-stay foreigners should treat it as a bonus, not a guaranteed tool.
- Some ticket sites, event systems, and small operators may ask for CPF or prefer PIX at checkout.
- When that happens, switch to foreigner-safe channels: hotel desk, major OTAs, reputable tour providers, or in-person payment where card acceptance is clear.

## Exchange Strategy

- Recommend official exchange offices or reliable ATMs over street exchange.
- Use regulated exchange channels and compare foreign-card fees before travel.
- Reject dynamic currency conversion when terminals offer to charge in your home currency.

## Cash and ATM Rules

- Withdraw in daylight, in malls, banks, or busy transport nodes instead of isolated machines.
- Split cash between wallet, bag, and accommodation safe.
- Refill cash modestly and carry only what is needed for the day.

## Budget Reality

- Rio, Sao Paulo, and Noronha punish bad convenience decisions more than they reward theoretical savings.
- Cheap lodging far from the plan can increase ride-hail cost, safety stress, and lost time.
- The most common hidden costs are baggage fees, airport transfers, ferries, park fees, premium beach areas, and last-minute flights.
