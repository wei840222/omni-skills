# Domain knowledge

Load this file when a technique claim needs a source, or when choosing among CBT, exposure, ACT, and mindfulness.

## What the cited guidance supports

- Cognitive behavioral therapy treats psychological problems by changing unhelpful thinking and behavior. A typical course is about 12 to 16 sessions. Core moves include recognizing distortions, reevaluating thoughts against reality, and facing fears instead of avoiding them.
- For anxiety disorders, psychotherapy (including CBT) and medication are both established options. CBT teaches a different way of thinking, behaving, and reacting. Exposure therapy is a specific CBT form that targets fears underlying an anxiety disorder so the person can engage in feared activities.
- Mindfulness-based and acceptance-based approaches (MBSR, MBCT, ACT) are part of the current psychotherapy landscape alongside CBT, not replacements for crisis care.
- Mental-health support at work is most useful when people can seek help without penalty. Technique coaching complements care; it does not replace it.
- Suicidal ideation is a clinical emergency for human responders. Ask directly, stay with the person, and connect them to local crisis resources. In the United States, 988 is the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

## Scope boundary

These pages describe clinical methods delivered by trained providers. This skill only coaches the portable self-help steps already in the technique references. It does not claim to deliver a course of therapy.
