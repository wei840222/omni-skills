# Acoustic Guitar Technique and Care Reference

## Effective practice

Use one correction at a time and verify it with a short, observable check before moving to the next exercise. Keep remaining suggestions outside the current practice block until that check is complete.

Before advising, gather the smallest set of facts that changes the plan: playing style, genre, current ability, and the user's next goal. Begin with the technique they can practice today.

## Fingerstyle and strumming

**Travis picking** pairs a steady, alternating thumb bass with notes plucked by the fingers on higher strings. Start with the bass alone, add one melody finger, then join the complete pattern only after the pulse stays steady.

**Strumming** is driven primarily by wrist rotation. Use a relaxed grip and let the forearm provide the larger movement needed for accents and dynamics. For muted or lifeless strums, isolate one variable at a time: grip tension, wrist motion, pick angle, then accent placement.

### Build clean fundamentals

- Let the wrist lead strumming and use the arm for broader dynamics.
- Use relaxed minimum fretting pressure; keep non-fretting fingers clear of neighboring strings.
- Establish thumb independence with a repeating bass pattern before adding melody notes.
- Shape dynamics deliberately: relaxed strokes for soft passages and a controlled accent for stronger beats.
- For a barre chord, place the thumb behind the neck near the middle finger and roll the index finger slightly toward its bony edge.

## Left-hand mechanics

For a conventional barre chord, put the thumb on the back of the neck roughly behind the middle finger. Use the least pressure that produces a clear note. When a chord buzzes or sounds muted, test each string separately, move the fretting finger closer to the fret, and retest before increasing pressure.

## Diagnose the symptom

| Symptom | First correction | Check the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chords sound muted | Place each fretting finger close behind its fret and clear adjacent strings. | Pick each string individually, then play the full chord. |
| Strumming feels stiff or lifeless | Loosen the grip and move from the wrist. | Record four bars with quiet and accented beats. |
| Fingerpicking is uneven | Loop thumb plus one finger before adding another finger. | Keep the bass pulse even for one minute at a comfortable tempo. |
| Barre chords sound muddy | Adjust the index-finger angle and thumb position, then reduce pressure. | Test each string before returning to the progression. |
| Fingertips hurt during barre work | Recheck angle and minimum pressure; shorten drill length. | Stop if pain is sharp or persists; seek a qualified teacher or clinician. |

## Instrument care

Wood instruments respond to their environment. A common storage target is about 45–55% relative humidity; measure the case environment with a hygrometer and prefer the guitar maker's model-specific storage and humidification guidance when available. If the guitar develops a crack, a lifted bridge, or a sudden playability change, pause home adjustments and consult a qualified repair technician.
