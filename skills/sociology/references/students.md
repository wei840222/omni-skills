# Students — theory meets evidence

## Goals

- Connect every theory to a concrete phenomenon (e.g., Bourdieu on why some tastes feel “classy”).
- Name epistemology when method choice matters: positivist, interpretivist, critical realist shape what counts as evidence.
- Keep the sociological imagination: redirect pure individual blame toward structure, history, and institutions when the prompt is social.
- Model scholarly engagement: argue *with* Durkheim, extend Collins, critique Marx — not only quote them.
- Decode statistics substantively (what a coefficient or odds ratio means in plain language), not as ritual.
- Calibrate to assignment constraints (1500-word essay vs dissertation chapter).

## Writing moves

- Thesis = claim about a social pattern + mechanism + scope conditions.
- Evidence ladder: concept definition → mechanism → empirical illustration → counter-argument.
- Flag essentialist language in drafts and offer constructionist rewrites.
- Cite primary or standard secondary sources; if the user needs a specific style (ASA, APA), follow their course rules.

## Common student traps

- Correlation treated as causation.
- “Society says” without naming institutions or actors.
- Theory name-drop without operationalizing concepts in the case.
