# Running Training Guide

Load for plan design, pacing, taper, form, injury prevention, and race-day defaults. Keep medical red flags clinician-bound.

## Intensity distribution

- Target roughly **80% easy / 20% hard** of weekly running time (Seiler-style polarization for endurance athletes).
- **Easy** means full sentences are speakable (Zone 2). If easy runs feel like tempo, slow down before adding mileage.
- Heart-rate zones (rule of thumb; measured max preferred):
  - Zone 1 ~50–60% HRmax — recovery
  - Zone 2 ~60–70% — aerobic base
  - Zone 3 ~70–80% — moderate / tempo-adjacent
  - Zone 4 ~80–90% — threshold
  - Zone 5 ~90–100% — VO2max intervals
- Prefer Tanaka-style HRmax estimates over `220 − age` when no lab/field max exists; a measured max overrides estimates.

## Volume progression

- Increase weekly mileage by at most ~10% when healthy and sleeping well; treat this as a ceiling heuristic, not a guarantee against injury.
- Insert a deload (~30% volume cut) about every 3–4 weeks, or earlier if resting HR / mood / niggles worsen.
- New runners: one quality session/week. Experienced: usually two, with easy days between.

## Workout menu

| Workout | Purpose | Default prescription |
|---------|---------|----------------------|
| Easy run | Aerobic base, recovery | Conversational; most weekly minutes |
| Long run | Endurance | Easy effort; grow duration before pace |
| Tempo | Lactate threshold | Comfortably hard 20–40 min continuous or broken |
| Intervals | VO2max / speed | 400–1600 m repeats with full recoveries |
| Strides | Form / turnover | 4–8 × 20–30 s accelerations after easy runs |

## Periodization sketch

1. **Base** (4–8 weeks) — mostly easy volume; optional strides
2. **Build** (4–6 weeks) — add one quality session, grow long run
3. **Peak** (2–3 weeks) — keep intensity, hold or slightly trim volume
4. **Taper** — cut volume ~40–60%, keep short sharpness; ~2 weeks for marathon, ~1 week for half, shorter for 5K/10K

## Pacing and race day

- First kilometer/mile should feel too easy; aim for even or negative splits.
- Hills: hold effort, let pace float.
- Heat/humidity: slow the pace target; HR drifts up at the same speed.
- Race-day adrenaline is borrowed speed — do not spend it in the first third.
- Arrive early for bathroom, warm-up, and corral logistics.
- Kit/fueling: only what was rehearsed.

## Form cues

- Cadence often lands near **170–180 steps/min** for many runners; shorter steps reduce overstride braking (individual variation exists).
- Land near center of mass; heel contact is acceptable when not overstriding.
- Arms swing forward-back without crossing midline; eyes ~10 m ahead.

## Injury prevention

- Most overuse injuries follow **too much, too soon** (volume, intensity, or both).
- Strength 2×/week emphasizing glutes, hips, and core.
- Replace shoes roughly every **300–500 miles / 500–800 km** when cushioning dies (brand and gait vary).
- Dynamic warm-up before; mobility/foam rolling after if useful.
- Stop for pain that worsens during the run; monitor pain that fades after warm-up only if gait stays normal.

## Fueling timing

| Window | Default |
|--------|---------|
| 1–2 h pre-run | Familiar carbs, low fiber/fat; ~200–300 kcal when needed |
| Run <60 min | Water |
| Run 60–90 min | Optional ~30 g carbs if gut-trained |
| Run >90 min | ~30–60 g carbs/hour from gels/chews/drink already tested |
| 0–30 min post | Protein + carbs to start recovery (about 15–25 g protein + carbs) |

Exact race-nutrition products and medical diets are out of scope — keep advice portable and gut-tested.

## Mental defaults

- Break races into segments ("to the next aid station").
- Short mantras beat catastrophic distance math.
- Smile as a low-cost perceived-effort cue when appropriate.

## Red lines (stop / escalate)

- Sharp joint pain, chest pain, dizziness, syncope symptoms, or progressive limp → stop; seek clinical care.
- Persistent night pain, swelling, or stress-fracture suspicion → clinician, not another hard session.
- Do not diagnose injuries or promise finish times.
