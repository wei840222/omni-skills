Advise-mode skill for **human runners**: prescribe intensity balance, pacing,
injury-prevention habits, race prep, and fueling timing. It does not store
local training logs. Route quantified strength/concurrent-load math to
`fitness`, general gym coaching to `personal-trainer`, and meal planning to
`calories`.

## When to Use

- Building or adjusting a run week (easy volume + quality sessions)
- Diagnosing medium-effort every day, stalled pace, or early race fade
- Preparing a 5K, 10K, half, or marathon with taper and race-day defaults
- Responding to niggles, shoe mileage questions, or form cues
- Not for medical diagnosis, live form spotting, or guaranteed race times

## Core Rules

1. **Protect easy days** — ~80% of weekly running time conversational / Zone 2; ~20% hard. Easy days that feel "medium" erase recovery and stall adaptation.
2. **Progress volume slowly** — treat ~10%/week mileage jumps as a ceiling rule of thumb, not a law; cut volume sooner after illness, heat, or new shoes/surfaces.
3. **One quality focus at a time** — beginners: ≤1 quality session/week; experienced: usually ≤2. Separate hard days with easy or rest.
4. **Race by effort, not borrowed adrenaline** — start slower than goal pace; negative-split when possible; heat raises HR at the same pace.
5. **Pain that worsens mid-run stops the session** — fade-after-warmup soreness can be monitored; sharp joint pain or progressive limp → stop and escalate to a clinician, not "push through".
6. **Nothing new on race day** — shoes, gels, kit, and fueling already rehearsed in training.
7. **Fuel by duration** — <60 min: water; 60–90 min: optional ~30 g carbs; >90 min: ~30–60 g carbs/hour from products already tested.
