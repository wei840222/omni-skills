# Architecture Assistance Rules

## Design fundamentals

- Form follows function — spaces serve purpose before aesthetics.
- Circulation defines experience — movement through space matters as much as the rooms.
- Natural light transforms mood, energy use, and daily rhythm; orientation is a first-order decision.
- Scale to the human body — ceiling heights, doorways, and clearances relate to people using the space.

## Space planning

- Ask about daily routines before drawing floor plans — usage patterns drive layout.
- Public-to-private gradient: entry → living → sleeping.
- Adjacencies reduce friction — kitchen near dining; bathrooms reachable from bedrooms without long detours.
- Storage is usually underestimated; plan it explicitly.

## Working with clients

- Clarify budget early and design within reality.
- Shared visual references establish vocabulary and prevent misunderstandings.
- Trade-offs must be explicit: a larger kitchen means a smaller living room unless volume grows.
- Phased construction is valid when budget is limited — design for future expansion.

## Site and context

- Sun-path analysis informs orientation for passive solar, daylighting, and summer shading.
- Topography affects both design options and construction cost.
- Decide what to frame (views) versus screen (neighbors, roads).
- Buildings sit in neighborhoods, climates, and cultures — context shapes appropriate solutions.

## Sustainability

- Passive design first: orientation, insulation, and shading reduce loads before mechanical systems.
- Prefer local materials when they fit climate and reduce transport impact.
- Evaluate lifecycle cost (decades of operation), not only first cost.

## Materials

- Material properties constrain form — wood, steel, and concrete span and weather differently.
- Climate drives choice: freeze–thaw, humidity, UV, salt air.
- Specify materials owners can actually maintain; local availability affects cost and schedule.

## Common mistakes

- Designing for photos rather than living — comfort and maintenance must travel with aesthetics.
- Empty rooms deceive — plan with furniture drawn to scale.
- Underestimating space for mechanical systems — ducts, stacks, and equipment need volume.
- Trend-chasing over durable, context-fit solutions.

## Presenting ideas

- Align on diagrams and concepts before detailed drawings.
- Walk the experience: entry, turn, framed view, destination.
- Physical or 3D models help non-designers more than plans alone.
- Attach cost implications to each major decision point.
