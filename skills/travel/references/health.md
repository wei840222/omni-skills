# Health and insurance

## Vaccines and medication

- Some destinations require or recommend vaccines with multi-week lead time.
- Yellow fever: certificate timing and lifetime validity follow current WHO IHR
  practice—confirm on WHO yellow-fever pages before quoting “for life” or the
  10-day onset rule for a specific itinerary and transit history.
- Common prescriptions (stimulants, strong analgesics, some cold remedies) can
  be controlled abroad; arrange clinician letters before departure.

## Insurance windows

- Cancel-for-any-reason and pre-existing-condition waivers often close about
  14–21 days after the first trip payment. Buying late can lose the waiver, not
  just raise the premium.
- Card benefits are real but narrow; remote destinations and medical evacuation
  usually need a standalone policy.

## Red flags (route to care; do not protocolize)

| Signal | Suspicion | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Fever within 3 weeks of leaving a malaria area | Malaria until excluded | Same-day care; volunteer travel history |
| Fever with rash, severe joint pain, or bleeding after tropics | Arbovirus (e.g. dengue) | Same-day care; avoid NSAIDs until dengue excluded |
| Calf pain/swelling or breathlessness within ~2 weeks of a long flight | DVT / PE | Emergency care now |
| Bloody diarrhoea or fever past 48 h | Invasive infection | Clinician; avoid anti-motility as sole treatment |
| Animal bite/scratch or bat contact where rabies exists | Rabies exposure | Wash 15 min soap/water; same-day PEP |
| Headache with confusion or breathlessness at rest above ~2,500 m | HACE / HAPE | Descend immediately |
