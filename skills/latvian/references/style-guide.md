# Latvian style guide

Use this reference after selecting the audience and register in `SKILL.md`. Examples illustrate choices; match the user's wording and the recipient rather than forcing every feature into an output.

## Formality and address

- **Casual / known peer:** `Čau`, `Sveiks` / `Sveika`, and second-person singular `tu` can fit.
- **Professional, institutional, unfamiliar, older, or explicitly formal:** retain `jūs` and formal greetings such as `Labdien` where appropriate.
- Keep address, verb forms, greeting, and closing in the same register. Ask for the intended audience when it changes the choice materially.

## Conversational rhythm

Particles can make a casual message sound less translated when used sparingly:

| Marker | Typical effect | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `nu` | soft lead-in / filler | `Nu, paskatīsimies.` |
| `jau` | shared or completed emphasis | `Es jau atnācu.` |
| `tak` | insistent emphasis | `Pasaki tak godīgi.` |
| `vai` | polar question marker | `Vai viss kārtībā?` |
| `gan` | soft reassurance / "quite" | `Gan jau tiksim galā.` |

A message does not need a filler or particle to be natural. In formal or concise writing, use none.

## Fillers, expressiveness, and reactions

- Light fillers such as `tipa`, `kā`, `zini`, `klau`, `vispār`, and `starp citu` fit peer chat only.
- Prefer intensity that matches the source: `labi` can remain straightforward; `super`, `forši`, `lieliski`, `baigi`, or `mega` fit only a clearly casual audience.
- Natural reactions: `Tiešām?`, `Nopietni?`, `Ko?`, `Oho!`, `Vau!`, `Nu nē!`, `Forši!`, `Super!`
- Set phrases: `Nav problēmu`, `Mierīgi`, `Labi`, `Okei`, `Sapratu`
- Peer slang and heavy internet English mixing are audience-specific. Keep them out of professional, institutional, sensitive, or mixed-audience text.

## Syntax and output check

Latvian inflection often makes subject pronouns optional when the verb form is clear. Prefer a clear natural sentence over a word-for-word English order, while preserving emphasis and facts.

Before delivery:

1. Confirm `tu` or `jūs` matches the recipient and remains consistent.
2. Remove any filler, slang, or intensifier that changes the speaker's implied relationship or tone.
3. Keep names, dates, quantities, commitments, and safety-sensitive wording faithful to the source.
4. For publication, legal, medical, or brand-critical text, label this as a draft and route it to a qualified native reviewer when one is available.

## Verified language resources

For terminology, orthography, or a high-stakes wording decision, consult a current authoritative resource before finalizing rather than treating this guide's examples as a substitute for expert review:

- **Latviešu valodas aģentūra (Latvian Language Agency):** https://valoda.lv/ — state language policy, consultations, and public guidance.
- **Valsts valoda hub:** https://valoda.lv/valsts-valoda/ — official-language policy pages and normative context.
- **Konsultācijas:** https://valoda.lv/konsultacijas/ — agency consultation entry points for contested wording.
- **Tēzaurs:** https://tezaurs.lv/ — large contemporary Latvian lexical resource (inflection tables and corpus examples when JS is enabled).
- **Māci un mācies:** https://maciunmacies.valoda.lv/ — agency learning portal for levels, materials, and self-study tools.

If an authoritative source, a qualified native reviewer, or the user's style guide conflicts with a casual example here, follow the applicable source and explain the register choice succinctly.
