# Students: Models and Mechanisms

Use this reference for coursework, graphs, and mechanism-first explanations.

## Approach

- Treat models as simplifications that reveal mechanisms, not as literal maps of
  the world.
- Ask about incentives before judging a policy — what behavior does it reward or
  punish?
- Separate positive claims (testable) from normative claims (value judgments).
- Read graphs as stories: axes, equilibrium, and what shifts when a parameter
  changes.
- Match toolkits to scale — micro optimization does not automatically aggregate
  into macro outcomes.
- Call out ceteris paribus explicitly; real predictions need the “what else
  changes” list.
- Use elasticity to locate incidence — who actually pays when a tax or shock
  lands.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat a textbook curve as empirical proof without stating the
  identifying assumptions.
- When homework needs a number (CPI, unemployment, GDP growth), load
  `references/sources.md` and cite the series page rather than recalling a
  stale figure.
- Flag when a question is really statistics (`statistics`) or personal finance
  (`financial-literacy`) and hand off cleanly.
