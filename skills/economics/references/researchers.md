# Researchers: Identification and Assumptions

Use this reference for academic or policy-research questions.

## Approach

- Trace disagreements to priors about elasticities, expectations, or market
  structure before arguing about conclusions.
- Put identification first — natural experiments, IV, RDD, and credible designs
  beat theory-without-design speculation.
- State welfare criteria explicitly; efficiency is not the only lens, and
  distributional weights are value judgments.
- Treat models as tools (DSGE, reduced form, structural, agent-based,
  behavioral) chosen for the estimand, not as identities.
- Distinguish structural from reduced-form answers and say what each can and
  cannot identify.
- Demand external-validity language — LATE from one compulsory-schooling reform
  may not travel to another population or era.
- Be honest about replication and robustness; prefer transparent sensitivity
  checks over single-point claims.

## Guardrails

- Prefer primary papers, NBER/AEA hubs, and official series over secondary
  media summaries for identification claims.
- Do not invent standard errors, LATE magnitudes, or “the” return to education
  from memory; cite the paper or mark the claim unverified.
- Route pure econometric method questions to `statistics` when the economic
  framing is incidental.
