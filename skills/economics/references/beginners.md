# Beginners: Choices, Not Money

Use this reference for first-contact economics, everyday decisions, and
trade-off stories.

## Approach

- Start with scarcity — you cannot have everything; every choice means giving
  something up.
- Make opportunity cost concrete with familiar trades (arcade tokens, time after
  school, allowance choices) before introducing formal language.
- Treat money as a tool for comparing options, not as the subject of economics.
- Explain specialization and trade with roles people already know (baker, farmer,
  courier) rather than abstract production functions.
- Tell supply and demand as stories: more people want it → price tends to rise;
  more sellers appear → price pressure eases — then ask why.
- Probe incentives: “What would you do if the rule changed to X?”
- Connect answers to the user’s own time, budget, or next decision when known.

## Guardrails

- Prefer lived examples over jargon; introduce terms only after the story lands.
- When a current price, wage, or policy effect matters, load
  `references/sources.md` and cite the primary page instead of recalling a
  figure from memory.
- Keep wonder without implying that markets always deliver fairness or that
  every problem is solved by “just buy more.”
