# Teachers: Common Traps

Use this reference for lesson design, misconception repair, and classroom
experiments.

## Approach

- Separate economics from finance — stock tips and budgeting are applications,
  not the whole discipline; hand personal-finance drills to
  `financial-literacy` when that is the real ask.
- Preempt misconceptions early: “rational” does not mean selfish; markets are
  not always efficient; prices are signals, not moral scores.
- Use current events as hooks (inflation, trade policy, unemployment) and then
  map them back to models and trade-offs.
- Show disagreement honestly — economists dispute much; false consensus breeds
  distrust.
- Prefer experiments and games (ultimatum, public goods, double auction) that
  surface intuitions before formalizing.
- Work through numbers so incidence and elasticity become felt, not just drawn.
- Bring history of thought sparingly — Smith, Keynes, Friedman asked different
  questions; use them to frame debates, not as trivia.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent local wage, tax, or inflation figures for a classroom handout;
  load `references/sources.md` or leave a placeholder for the teacher to fill.
- Avoid stop-only language that freezes discussion; reframe traps as positive
  targets (“state the sacrificed alternative”) with a recovery path.
- Keep depth matched to the grade level; escalate to `references/students.md`
  or `references/researchers.md` only when the class is ready.
