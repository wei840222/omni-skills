# Provider Comparison — Internet

## Before comparing

1. **Service address** — coverage and technology often change street-by-street (`<SERVICE_ADDRESS>` held by the user)
2. **Current contract** — end date, promo expiry, early-termination / device payoff, retention windows
3. **Usage profile** — concurrent streams, upload-heavy work, cloud backups, real-time games, household size

## Comparison framework

| Factor | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Real price | Rate **after** promotional period (often month 13+) |
| Total cost | Install + router/ONT fees + 24 months of service ± penalties |
| Technology | FTTH usually beats FTTC/cable/DSL on symmetric upload and stability — verify what is actually provisioned |
| Upload | Critical for calls/backups; marketing tables often hide it |
| Contract length | Shorter terms trade monthly price for flexibility |
| Fair use | “Unlimited” may still throttle or de-prioritize |
| Outage process | How incidents are filed and what evidence helps |

## Hidden costs to flag

- Router/ONT rental that outlives the promo
- Bundled TV/mobile lines that auto-renew at full price
- Installation waived only with a long commitment
- Annual “adjustment” clauses in the terms
- Mandatory technician visits or reactivation fees

## Negotiation tactics

### New service

- Ask for the best currently bookable offer (public web rates are often incomplete)
- Bring a specific competing quote (speed, term, total cost)
- Request explicit waiver of install/router fees in writing

### Retention (existing customer)

- Start from cancellation/retention, not generic sales
- Have the competing offer details ready
- Best leverage is commonly near promo expiry / 30–60 days before term end
- Get any new rate, term, and fee waivers in a confirmation channel the user controls

## Contract checklist

Before the user signs or authorizes a switch:

- [ ] Post-promo monthly price confirmed
- [ ] Early-termination and device payoff math checked
- [ ] Access technology verified (FTTH vs FTTC vs cable vs DSL/fixed wireless)
- [ ] Upload floor adequate for the workload
- [ ] Coverage validated at the exact service address
- [ ] Price-lock / adjustment language reviewed
- [ ] Installation window and self-install constraints understood

## Privacy

Do not paste full addresses, government IDs, or account secrets into third-party “coverage check” tools the user does not trust. Prefer official provider checkers the user opens themselves.
