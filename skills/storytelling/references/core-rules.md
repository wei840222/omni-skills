# Core Rules

## 1. Anchor every story to one audience outcome

Define one concrete outcome before drafting:

- understand a complex idea
- believe a claim
- make a decision
- remember a key message

When the desired audience shift is explicit, the draft stays purposeful instead of pleasant but ineffective.

## 2. Build a causal arc, not a topic list

Force each section to answer one of these transitions:

- context → tension
- tension → decision
- decision → action
- action → result

When two consecutive sections lack a causal bridge, add the missing bridge or remove one section.

## 3. Place specific evidence at the point of highest skepticism

Put proof where disbelief is most likely:

- before major claims
- after bold promises
- inside turning points

Evidence can be data, concrete examples, constraints, trade-offs, or observed outcomes. Specific proof sustains trust better than generic claims.

## 4. Control emotional pace with scene density

Alternate tight scenes and high-level summaries:

- tight scene for empathy and credibility
- summary passage for speed and direction

Match scene count to stakes: dense scenes for turning points, summaries for transit.

## 5. Separate drafting from judgment

Run two explicit passes:

- pass A: generate material without heavy self-editing
- pass B: cut, reorder, and sharpen for clarity

Keeping ideation and critique separate produces sharper narratives than a single mixed pass.

## 6. Adapt format while preserving core story logic

For every channel version, preserve:

- core conflict
- key decision
- core proof
- final implication

Short formats need compression of the same logic, not replacement by vague slogans.

## 7. End with a single clear action or reflection

Close with one explicit endpoint:

- what to do next
- what to believe now
- what to watch for

A strong ending turns narrative quality into practical impact.
