# Sources

Domain craft notes for Gate 6. Prefer primary/authoritative references over marketing blogs. Re-check mutable claims before asserting new numbers or named frameworks as mandatory.

## Narrative structure and arc

1. Freytag, Gustav. *Technique of the Drama* (historical exposition of dramatic structure; use as historical map, not a rigid modern template).
   - Overview context: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramatic_structure>
2. Field, Syd. *Screenplay: The Foundations of Screenwriting* — three-act pressure and turning points for choice/execution beats.
3. McKee, Robert. *Story: Substance, Structure, Style and the Principles of Screenwriting* — causal motivation and scene design discipline.

## Evidence, clarity, and nonfiction persuasion

4. Heath, Chip & Heath, Dan. *Made to Stick* — concrete detail, unexpectedness, and sticky takeaways for transfer endings.
5. Duarte, Nancy. *Resonate* — audience journey between what-is and what-could-be for talks and pitch narratives.
   - Author resources: <https://www.duarte.com/resonate/>
6. Harvard Business Review — storytelling for leaders and case narrative craft (verify the specific article before citing claims):
   - <https://hbr.org/topic/subject/storytelling>

## Oral and short-form adaptation

7. TED Speaker Guide — oral clarity, early stakes, and one clear idea per talk:
   - <https://storage.googleapis.com/ted-speaker-guide/TED_Speaker_Guide.pdf>
8. Nielsen Norman Group — scannability and message clarity for short web/social compression:
   - <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/>

## Practice notes for this skill

- Treat classic screen/drama structure as a diagnostic map for missing tension or turning points, not as a forced template for every business story.
- Nonfiction stories still need verifiable proof at skepticism peaks; literary technique does not replace evidence.
- Channel rewrites keep the same four cores: conflict, decision, proof, implication.
