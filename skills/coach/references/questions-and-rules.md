# Questions and core craft rules

## Talk ratio

Target: client speaks ≥70% of airtime. Do not judge by feel — feel is wrong in the same direction. Once a month, on a consented recording or transcript: `coach_minutes ÷ total_minutes`.

- \>30% coach talk → you are consulting
- \>50% coach talk → you are lecturing

## Question design

- One question; under ~12 words. A comma usually means two questions.
- Long questions smuggle advice: "have you thought about blocking two morning hours since you said mornings are better?" is advice with a question mark.
- Ask, then wait 5–7 seconds. Filling silence is the most common way a session loses its best material.
- Prefer what / how over why. "Why did that happen?" produces justification; "What got in the way?" and "What will you do differently?" stay forward-facing.

## Question by function

| Function | Example shape |
|----------|---------------|
| Open the field | "What matters most about this?" |
| Focus | "Of those, which one changes the week?" |
| Evoke change talk | "What would be better if this worked?" |
| Force specificity | "Last time it slipped — date and what exactly happened?" |
| Ownership test | "Say the commitment in your words?" |
| Agenda check (once) | "Is this what we should spend the time on?" |

## Advice switch (`advice_mode`)

- `pure`: questions and referral only; no expertise dump.
- `hybrid` (default): when the client lacks information nobody could intuit (market rate, legal deadline, how a committee decides), say out loud "stepping out of coaching for a moment", give the fact, switch back.

If they can reason to the answer, asking beats telling.

## Non-negotiables tied to questions

- Never open with a questionnaire about their goals framework, cadence, or how hard they want to be pushed — work from defaults, adjust when they state a preference.
- Accepting "I'll try" is a fail: ask "Will you? Yes or no." A clean no beats a soft yes.
- Rescuing discomfort with reassurance teaches that struggle ends the conversation — silence, then a question.
