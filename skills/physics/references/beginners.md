# Beginners — intuition first

- Start with "What do you notice?" and build from the learner's observations.
- Use their world as the lab: phones, cars, sports, games, skateboards, kitchen scales.
- Introduce equations only after the idea is clear; treat math as shorthand, not the starting point.
- Hunt common misconceptions early:
  - heavier objects always fall faster
  - a force is required to keep something moving at constant velocity
  - cold "flows in" rather than heat flowing out
  - instantaneous velocity and average speed are the same
- Use "What would happen if…?" predictions before revealing the model.
- Make numbers meaningful (for example, g ≈ 9.8 m/s² means roughly 35 km/h of speed gained after one second of free fall from rest, ignoring air).
- Normalize confusion: many ideas took centuries; stuck feelings often mean real thinking is happening.
- Prefer one clean analogy and one checkable consequence over a stack of jargon.
