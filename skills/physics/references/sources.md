# Physics reference sources

Verify quantitative claims, constants, and standards against primary or
evaluated sources before presenting them as precise current values.

## Constants, data, and particle properties

- NIST CODATA internationally recommended values of the fundamental physical constants — https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Constants/ — authoritative constants and uncertainties.
- NIST Physics Laboratory / Physical Measurement Laboratory overview — https://www.nist.gov/pml — measurement science entry points.
- Particle Data Group (PDG) Review of Particle Physics — https://pdg.lbl.gov/ — particle properties, astrophysics/cosmo summaries, reviews.
- NIST Chemistry WebBook — https://webbook.nist.gov/chemistry/ — evaluated thermophysical and spectroscopic data when chemistry boundary cases appear.

## Literature and preprints

- arXiv physics — https://arxiv.org/archive/physics — open preprints; distinguish preprint from peer-reviewed version.
- INSPIRE-HEP — https://inspirehep.net/ — high-energy and related literature index.
- Google Scholar — https://scholar.google.com/ — discovery; always open the primary paper before citing a result.
- Living Reviews in Relativity (Springer) — https://link.springer.com/journal/41114 — maintained review articles in gravity/relativity.

## Education, demos, and frameworks

- PhET Interactive Simulations — https://phet.colorado.edu/ — research-based interactive physics simulations.
- AAPT (American Association of Physics Teachers) — https://www.aapt.org/ — teaching resources and community standards context.
- Next Generation Science Standards — https://www.nextgenscience.org/ — U.S. K–12 framework reference (local curricula may differ).
- NASA Science — https://science.nasa.gov/ — space and Earth-science mission/education context.

## Verification practice

1. For a constant or conversion factor used numerically, open CODATA/NIST (or PDG for particle data) and note the value and uncertainty you rely on.
2. For a "current consensus" claim, prefer a living review, PDG narrative, or primary paper over memory.
3. Label textbook Newtonian/Maxwellian results separately from active observational tensions or speculative extensions.
4. Do not invent DOIs, experimental significances, or unpublished numbers.
