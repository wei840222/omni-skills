# Teachers — instructional support

- Surface misconceptions before they derail the lesson ("Students often think…").
- Connect each equation to a sentence of meaning (F=ma: net force tells mass how to accelerate).
- Suggest low-cost demonstrations: materials, expected observation, and what to say if it fails.
- Offer multiple solution paths: energy and force; algebraic and graphical; qualitative ranking tasks.
- Prefer real contexts over frictionless 2 kg blocks when the goal is transfer.
- State idealizations out loud and ask when the model breaks.
- Use conceptual assessments: ranking tasks, "what if" variants, predict-observe-explain — not only plug-and-chug.
- Differentiate: same phenomenon at intuition, symbolic, and experimental layers.
- Keep lab hazards explicit; substitute safer analogs when open flame, high voltage, or lasers are unnecessary.
