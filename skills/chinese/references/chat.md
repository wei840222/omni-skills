# Chat — WeChat and Messaging Conventions

Chinese messaging has unwritten rules. Breaking them marks you as foreign or AI.

---

## Punctuation Rules

### Drop Final Period

In chat, don't end messages with 。:

**AI-typical:**
> 好的，我明天会提交报告。

**Human:**
> 好的，我明天会提交报告

**Exception**: If the message is formal or long (3+ sentences), a final 。is acceptable.

### Exclamation Marks

Humans use ！more than AI expects:

- 好的！(enthusiastic)
- 收到！(acknowledged)
- 谢谢！(grateful)

But not after every sentence. One ！at the end of a message is common. Multiple ！！！shows strong emotion.

### Question Marks

In casual chat, humans often drop the ？and just ask:

> 你明天来吗

Not wrong. Context makes it clear it's a question.

### Commas vs Periods

Humans chain clauses with ，instead of breaking into sentences:

**AI-typical:**
> 我到了。你在哪里？

**Human:**
> 我到了，你在哪

---

## 哈哈哈 Length Matching

哈哈哈 is not just "haha" — its length signals engagement:

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 哈 | Polite acknowledgment, not really funny |
| 哈哈 | Mild amusement |
| 哈哈哈 | Actually funny |
| 哈哈哈哈 | Very funny |
| 哈哈哈哈哈哈 | Can't stop laughing |

**Rule**: Match the other person's energy. If they send 哈哈哈哈, don't reply with just 哈哈.

**Variants**: 2333 (from old forum culture), xswl (笑死我了), 笑死

---

## Never Open with 在吗

在吗 (Are you there?) is considered rude or anxious. It forces the other person to commit before knowing what you want.

**Bad:**
> 在吗？

**Good:**
> 在吗，有个事想问你 (state the thing)
> 忙吗？想请教个事 (give context)
> (just say what you need)

**Exception**: If you're close friends and it's clearly casual, 在吗 is acceptable. But even then, better to just ask.

## Emoji and 表情包 Calibration

### Emoji Density

| Context | Emoji use |
|---------|-----------|
| Work chat with boss | Minimal or none |
| Work chat with peers | 1-2 per message max |
| Friends | Moderate, match their style |
| Family | Can be heavy, especially older relatives |
| Flirting | Playful, but don't overdo |

### Common Emoji Meanings

| Emoji | Typical use |
|-------|-------------|
| 😂 | Actually funny |
| 🤣 | Very funny |
| 😅 | Awkward, embarrassed |
| 🙏 | Thanks, please |
| 👍 | Good, agreed |
| ❤️ | Close relationships only |
| 😊 | Polite warmth |
| 🤔 | Thinking, skeptical |

### 表情包 (Sticker Packs)

Stickers are huge in Chinese chat. They convey tone better than words.

**Rules:**

- Match the other person's sticker energy
- Don't send stickers in formal/work contexts unless the culture allows it
- Stickers can replace words: send a 好的 sticker instead of typing 好的
- Avoid outdated stickers (older than 2 years) — check slang.md for dating

## Group Chat Dynamics

### Speaking in Groups

- Don't dominate. Short messages, frequent.
- React to others before adding your own point.
- Use @ sparingly. @someone only when you need their attention.
- Don't send long messages. Break them up or save for private chat.

### Topic Switching

Groups often have multiple conversations happening. Humans don't signal topic switches:

**AI-typical:**
> 关于刚才说的项目，另外，午饭吃什么？

**Human:**
> (project discussion)
> (new message) 午饭吃啥

No transition needed. Context makes it clear.

### Leaving Conversations

Humans don't announce they're leaving:

**AI-typical:**
> 我先去忙了，回头聊。

**Human:**
> (just stops responding)
> Or: 先忙 / 回聊

## Voice Message Conventions

### When to Send Voice

- Close friends/family: voice is fine
- Work: avoid unless necessary (driving, hands full)
- Strangers/acquaintances: text first

### Voice Message Length

- Keep it under 30 seconds
- If longer, warn first: 发个长语音可以吗
- Never send 60-second voice messages without asking

### Receiving Voice

- Don't expect immediate reply. Voice is asynchronous.
- If you reply with text, that's fine.
- Don't forward voice messages without permission.

## Red Packet (红包) Etiquette

### Sending

- Amount matters: 6.66, 8.88, 18.88 are lucky
- Avoid 4 (sounds like 死)
- Group red packets: split randomly or evenly
- Occasion: holidays, birthdays, "just because" for close friends

### Receiving

- Always say thanks: 谢谢老板 / 感谢
- Don't complain about amount
- Reciprocate eventually

---

## Timing and Response Expectations

### Response Speed

- Close friends: immediate to hours, no pressure
- Work: within business hours, same day
- Elders: respond promptly, they worry

### Late Night Messages

- After 11pm: only for emergencies or close friends
- If sending late, acknowledge: 这么晚打扰了
- Don't expect immediate reply

### Morning Messages

- Before 8am: avoid unless urgent
- If sending early, acknowledge: 早起打扰了

---

## Key Principles

1. **Brevity over completeness** — say what's needed, not everything
2. **Match energy** — if they're casual, be casual; if formal, be formal
3. **Context over clarity** — humans assume shared context, don't over-explain
4. **Flow over structure** — messages are streams, not essays
5. **Relationship over rules** — close friends get more leeway
