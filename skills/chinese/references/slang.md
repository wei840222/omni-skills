# Slang — Currentness and Audience Calibration

Internet slang changes quickly. Treat its currentness as a question to verify for the actual audience, not as a fixed vocabulary list.

## Decide whether slang belongs

Use this sequence before adding slang:

1. Identify the region, platform, relationship, and register.
2. Inspect recent language from the intended audience or the account's approved examples.
3. Confirm the term's meaning and tone in that setting.
4. Use plain Chinese if the term is unclear, contested, or likely to distract.

Formal documents, contracts, academic writing, and first-contact business correspondence normally benefit from plain language. Casual peer conversation may support slang when the reader uses the same register.

## Appetite levels

| Level | Use | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| none | formal or broad-audience writing | Use standard vocabulary only. |
| light | mixed-age or workplace chat | Use only terms the recipient or channel already uses. |
| current | familiar peers or casual social posts | Verify the term against recent audience evidence. |
| heavy | a clearly shared niche community | Match the community's demonstrated vocabulary and boundaries. |

When the appropriate level is uncertain, choose a lower level or plain wording.

## Evidence sources

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Recent, user-provided examples from the recipient, account, or community.
2. Recent public posts from the same platform and audience segment, checked for context rather than raw frequency.
3. A current, attributable language reference that explains origin and meaning.
4. A native review when the wording will be public, commercial, or relationship-sensitive.

Record a durable preference only when the user asks to save it; follow `references/memory-template.md`.

## Common failure modes

| Signal | Response |
| --- | --- |
| The term means different things in different groups | Use a clear standard phrase or explain the intended sense. |
| The term is absent from current audience examples | Leave it out; do not try to sound younger. |
| A formal context needs a vivid phrase | Use a precise standard expression, not a meme. |
| A platform-specific term appears outside its community | Replace it with wording the target reader will understand. |
| The text sounds like a list of trend words | Keep the one term that adds meaning and rewrite the rest plainly. |

## Review checklist

- [ ] The audience and platform are known or explicitly assumed.
- [ ] Each slang term has a current, contextual meaning.
- [ ] The register stays consistent with the surrounding text.
- [ ] Removing the term would not make the message less clear; if it would not, prefer the plain version.
- [ ] No dated-status claim is presented as a permanent fact.
