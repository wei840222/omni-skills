# Slang — Internet Slang Dating and Calibration

Chinese internet slang evolves rapidly. This guide provides methodology for tracking, dating, and calibrating slang usage.

---

## Dating Methodology

### How to Date a Slang Term

1. **Origin search**: Find earliest usage (Weibo post, forum thread, video)
2. **Peak detection**: When was it most popular? (Search volume, frequency in feeds)
3. **Decline markers**: When did it become "cringe" or overused?
4. **Current status**: Is it still active, dead, or ironic?

### Sources for Dating

- Weibo trending history (if available)
- Bilibili video comments (timestamped)
- Zhihu questions about "XXX是什么意思" (indicates term is new to mainstream)
- Meme databases (if maintained)
- Personal observation + community feedback

## Appetite Levels

Slang usage varies by context and audience. Four levels:

### Level 0: None (无)

**Context**: Formal writing, business communication, academic papers
**Examples**: Zero slang
**Rule**: Never use internet slang in formal contexts

### Level 1: Light (轻)

**Context**: Semi-formal chat, workplace groups, mixed-age audiences
**Examples**: 哈哈, OK, 666, 点赞
**Rule**: Only universally understood terms, no niche references

### Level 2: Current (中)

**Context**: Friends, peer groups, casual social media
**Examples**: 绝绝子, yyds, 破防了, 内卷
**Rule**: Use terms from last 1-2 years, avoid outdated slang

### Level 3: Heavy (重)

**Context**: Close friends, niche communities, meme-heavy platforms
**Examples**: Platform-specific slang, very recent terms, ironic usage
**Rule**: Only in communities where everyone knows the references

### Calibration Rule

**When in doubt, go one level down.** It's better to sound slightly formal than to use dead slang or confuse the audience.

## Shelf Life

### The ~3 Year Rule

Most internet slang has a shelf life of ~3 years:

- **Year 1**: Emergence, early adopters
- **Year 2**: Peak popularity, mainstream adoption
- **Year 3**: Overuse, starts feeling dated
- **Year 4+**: Dead or ironic usage only

## Re-Calibration Cadence

### Quarterly Check (Every 3 Months)

1. **Review current slang list**: Are any terms declining?
2. **Check new terms**: What's trending on Weibo/Bilibili?
3. **Test with native speakers**: Ask "Is XXX still used?"
4. **Update this reference**: Add new terms, mark dead ones

### Annual Deep Review

1. **Full audit**: Review all slang used in past year
2. **Identify patterns**: What types of terms are emerging?
3. **Predict next year**: Based on current trends
4. **Archive dead terms**: Move to historical section

### Trigger-Based Updates

Update immediately when:
- A term you used gets corrected ("没人说XXX了")
- You encounter unfamiliar slang in native content
- A major platform changes (e.g., new trending mechanism)
- User feedback indicates outdated usage

## Usage Guidelines

### When to Use Slang

✅ Casual chat with peers
✅ Social media posts (match platform)
✅ Memes and jokes
✅ When it adds value (not just filler)

### When to Avoid Slang

❌ Formal writing
❌ Business communication
❌ Mixed-age groups (unless Level 1)
❌ When term is dead or declining
❌ When audience might not understand

### How to Use Slang

1. **Match the level**: Don't use Level 3 slang in Level 1 context
2. **Don't force it**: If it doesn't fit naturally, skip it
3. **Know your audience**: Age, region, platform matter
4. **Stay current**: Re-calibrate quarterly
5. **When in doubt, skip it**: Better to sound plain than cringe

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Using Dead Slang

**Wrong**: 这个设计很给力 (2010s slang)
**Right**: 这个设计很绝 / 这个设计很强

### 2. Overusing Slang

**Wrong**: 我直接破防了家人们，这个绝绝子真的yyds
**Right**: 这个真的破防了

### 3. Wrong Context

**Wrong**: Using 摆烂 in business email
**Right**: Use 暂时搁置 or 需要重新评估

### 4. Mixing Levels

**Wrong**: 尊敬的领导，我们的工作真的很内卷
**Right**: Keep formal contexts slang-free

---

## Key Principles

1. **Slang has shelf life** — ~3 years, then it's dead or ironic
2. **Calibrate by context** — four levels, when in doubt go lower
3. **Platform matters** — B站 slang ≠ 小红书 slang
4. **Audience awareness** — age, region, relationship all affect appropriateness
5. **Re-calibrate quarterly** — slang evolves fast, stay updated
6. **Less is more** — don't force slang, use only when it adds value
7. **Dead slang is worse than no slang** — using outdated terms signals you're out of touch
