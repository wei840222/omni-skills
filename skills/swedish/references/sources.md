# Swedish language sources

Use these pages to check a register label, a du/ni mapping, a particle gloss, or lagom tone. They describe usage; they are not a license to invent slang or a dialect the user did not ask for.

## Register, pronouns, and formality

- **Wikipedia — Swedish language** — overview of Swedish register, orthography, and modern spoken/written norms. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_language
- **Wikipedia — Du-reformen** — documents the 1960s–70s shift toward universal *du* address in Swedish society. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du-reformen
- **Wikipedia — T–V distinction** — second-person formality contrasts; Swedish almost always defaults to *du*, with *ni* rare or marked. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%E2%80%93V_distinction
- **Wiktionary — du** — Swedish second-person singular informal pronoun *du*. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/du#Swedish
- **Wiktionary — ni** — Swedish second-person plural / marked polite form *ni*. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ni#Swedish

## Particles, lagom, and adjacency

- **Wiktionary — ju** — discourse particle marking shared knowledge. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ju#Swedish
- **Wiktionary — väl** — softener / uncertainty particle. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/v%C3%A4l#Swedish
- **Wiktionary — nog** — "probably" / soft prediction particle. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/nog#Swedish
- **Wikipedia — Lagom** — cultural understatement and moderation relevant to Swedish tone calibration. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagom
- **Wikipedia — Comparison of Norwegian Bokmål and Standard Danish** — Scandinavian adjacency context when routing away from Swedish. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_Norwegian_Bokm%C3%A5l_and_Standard_Danish

- **Wikipedia — Danish language** — sibling Nordic routing when the target is Danish. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language
