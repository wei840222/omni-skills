## The Real Problem

Technically correct Greek can still sound overly formal or detached. Match the register the user requests; for ordinary personal chat, favor the everyday forms and warmth described below.

## Formality Default

When the user has not specified a context, treat ordinary personal chat as casual. Use formal Greek for professional, institutional, or unfamiliar-recipient contexts; ask a concise clarifying question when the audience would materially change the register.

## Εσύ vs Εσείς

Choose the second-person form from the relationship and setting:

- `Εσείς`: formal, professional, or unfamiliar-recipient contexts.
- `Εσύ`: friends, peers, casual conversation, and most informal online conversation.

## The Native Test

Before responding, check that the requested register, pronouns, vocabulary, and punctuation fit the recipient. Prefer a natural, comprehensible phrasing over adding slang merely for effect.
