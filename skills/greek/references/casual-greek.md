# Casual Greek

## Particles & Fillers

These make Greek natural:
- `ρε`: casual address, such as `Ρε φίλε` or `Τι λες ρε`.
- `μωρέ`: emphasis, frustration, or affection.
- `δηλαδή`: “I mean” or “that is.”
- `τέλος πάντων`: “anyway.”
- `βασικά`: “basically.”

## Fillers & Flow

Use conversational fillers sparingly when they fit the speaker:
- `Ε, λοιπόν`, `τέλος πάντων`
- `Κοίτα`, `άκου`, `περίμενε`
- `Ξέρεις`, `καταλαβαίνεις`
- `Πάνω κάτω`, `κάπως έτσι`

## Casual Shortcuts

Texting may shorten spoken forms when the user’s channel and tone support it:
- `Τι κάνεις` → `Τι κάν;`
- `Δεν ξέρω` → `Δεν ξέρ`
- `Περίμενε` → `Περίμ`

Keep the full form when clarity, formality, or the recipient relationship calls for it.

## Expressiveness

Choose words that match the speaker’s intended intensity:
- `Καλά` → `Τέλεια`, `Φοβερά`, `Μια χαρά`
- `Άσχημα` → `Χάλια`, `Σκατά`, `Απαίσια`
- `Πολύ` → `Υπερβολικά`, `Τρελά`, `Φουλ`

## Common Expressions

Natural everyday expressions include:
- `Ωραία`, `Τέλεια`, `Οκ τότε`
- `Δεν πειράζει`, `Άστο`
- `Μια χαρά`, `Κομπλέ`
- `Τι να κάνουμε`, `Έτσι είναι`

## Reactions

Match reactions to the conversation rather than inserting them mechanically:
- `Σοβαρά;`, `Αλήθεια;`, `Τι λες;`
- `Ωχ!`, `Πω πω!`, `Θεέ μου!`
- `Τέλειο!`, `Φοβερό!`, `Γαμάτο!`
- `Χαχα` or `lol` in informal text

## Greeklish and Diminutives

Greeklish is common in informal messaging, gaming, and contexts without a Greek keyboard; keep one writing system per short message unless preserving user-supplied mixed script. Diminutive endings such as `-άκι` and `-ούλι` can convey warmth (`λεπτάκι`, `φιλάκι`) when they fit the relationship.

## Register Checks and Sources

- Prefer the recipient's requested formality over a blanket casual default. In unfamiliar or professional contexts, use `Εσείς`; use `Εσύ` for peers and established casual conversation.
- Treat slang and profanity as opt-in: mirror the user's tone, avoid escalating it, and provide a neutral alternative when the audience is unclear.
