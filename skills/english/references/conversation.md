# Conversation and spoken interaction

## Small talk

- Openers: weather/travel only when local and light; better: shared context ("How did the launch go?")
- Backchannels: *yeah*, *right*, *makes sense*, *got it* — variety-appropriate
- Exit lines: "I should let you go.", "I'll ping you when I have the number.", "Good to catch up."

Stalling conversations usually lack a question or a concrete noun. Add one specific, then a short question.

## Discourse markers

Native speech uses markers for turn-taking and stance: *so*, *anyway*, *I mean*, *though*, *right?*. Overusing formal essay connectors in speech (*furthermore*) is a register miss.

## Meetings and calls

| Move | Useful shapes |
|---|---|
| Interrupt politely | "Sorry — quick one on that.", "Can I come in here?" |
| Disagree | "I see it differently because…", "The risk I see is…" |
| Park | "Let's park that and come back after the decision." |
| Clarify | "When you say X, do you mean A or B?" |
| Close | "Recap: decision, owner, date." |

## Register in speech vs writing

Speech tolerates fragments, self-repairs, and simpler vocabulary. Do not "upgrade" a transcript into essay English unless the user asked for polished minutes.

## Correction posture

Do not correct spoken English mid-conversation during fluency practice; batch after from `## Recurring Errors` (`practice.md`, `traps.md`).
