# Business and professional English

One shape per job. Keep the reader's next action obvious.

## Email shapes

| Job | Shape |
|---|---|
| Ask | Context (1 line) → ask → needed-by / options → thanks |
| Decline | Thanks / acknowledgment → clear no → brief reason if useful → optional alternative |
| Chase | Reference prior ask + date → restated need → new deadline or question → offer help |
| Apologize | One apology first → what happened (fact) → fix / next step → prevention if warranted |
| Bad news | Plain statement early → impact → options → owner and timing |
| FYI | Why it matters → the fact → no forced ask |

## Chase cadence

1. Original ask with a date.
2. First chase: short, assumes good faith, restates the artifact needed.
3. Second chase: tighter deadline, CC only when politically required.
4. Escalate with facts, not heat.

Do not write three different emotional tones across chases; keep the same professional rung and increase specificity.

## Apology rule

One apology, first line, then the fix. Multiple apologies transfer discomfort and read junior.

## Understatement and indirectness

British and some Commonwealth professional cultures often soften disagreement. Examples of phrases that may **not** be literal praise or assent in context:

| Phrase | Possible pragmatic reading |
|---|---|
| "That's interesting." | Skepticism or polite non-agreement |
| "I'll bear it in mind." | Low priority / soft no |
| "With respect…" / "With the greatest respect…" | Disagreement incoming |
| "We might want to consider…" | Please change this |
| "Not wrong, but…" | Disprefer the current option |

These are **tendencies**, not universal decoder rings. Read relationship, channel, and prior turns before treating any line as coded rejection. US business email often prefers more direct asks; still avoid unnecessary bluntness on rungs 4–5.

## Meetings

- Open with purpose and end state.
- Disagree on the idea, not the person.
- Park tangents with an owner and a place (doc, ticket, later agenda).
- Close with decisions, owners, dates.

Phrase banks that stay polite across US/UK: "Can I jump in on that?", "Two concerns from my side:", "Let's capture that offline — I'll take it.", "Before we close: owners and dates."
