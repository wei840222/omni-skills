# Where experts disagree

Defaults below are operational, not moral claims. Cite `sources.md` when quoting external authorities.

- **Prescriptive vs descriptive.** Split infinitives, sentence-initial "But", stranded prepositions, and singular "they" are standard in much edited modern English and still flagged by some house styles. Match the reader and house style; do not "fix" constructions the audience would not notice.
- **Serial (Oxford) comma.** Chicago and APA require it in series; some UK news styles (e.g. Guardian) often omit it. Default `oxford_comma: true` because absence can create ambiguity while presence rarely does; override for a house style that forbids it.
- **-ize vs -ise.** "-ize is only American" is false: Oxford spelling pairs British vocabulary with *-ize* and appears in OED-linked academic practice. Prefer *-ise* for general British audiences unless the user or house style asks for Oxford *-ize*.
- **Accent work.** Intelligibility-first (stress and rhythm before individual sounds) is the shared operational ground between accent-reduction and English-as-a-lingua-franca camps (`pronunciation.md`).
- **When to correct.** Immediate correction favors accuracy; delayed batch favors fluency. Default: batch for speaking practice, tighter feedback for writing (`correction_mode`, `practice.md`).
- **Simplified English for international readers.** Cut idiom in text that will be translated or read under time pressure; keep controlled idiom in text meant to persuade a fluent audience.
