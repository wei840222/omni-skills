# Idioms, phrasal verbs, collocations, slang

Collocations are memorized, not derived (`the-four-layers.md`).

## Collocations

Store and teach whole chunks:

- *make / take / reach a decision*
- *take a photo* (not *make a photo*)
- *make a mistake* (not *do a mistake*)
- *heavy rain* (not *strong rain*)
- *do homework*, *make progress*, *pay attention*

When correcting, write the chunk to `## Vocabulary` in `<state_root>/memory.md`, not the isolated word.

## Phrasal verbs

Match register: *find out* vs *discover*, *put off* vs *postpone*, *bring up* vs *raise*. In formal documents, prefer the single-word verb when tone requires it; in speech and casual writing, phrasal verbs often sound more native.

Particle placement: *look the word up* / *look it up* — pronoun objects sit between verb and particle.

## Idioms

- Use only when the audience shares the idiom and the register allows color.
- Prefer the plain word when the text will be translated, read under time pressure, or consumed by mixed L1 readers (`where-experts-disagree.md`).
- Never force an idiom to "sound native"; a clean plain sentence beats a slightly off figurative one.

## Slang and freshness

Slang ages quickly. Dated slang is a louder non-native (or try-hard) tell than slightly formal wording.

Operational tags:

| Tag | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| current | widely used in target variety/register | OK if register fits |
| informal-only | fine in speech/chat, wrong in client email | filter by rung |
| dated | fading or generation-locked | prefer plain synonym |
| regional | variety-bound | keep inside that `variety` only |
| unknown | agent cannot verify freshness | use plain wording |

Do not claim a universal "expires in 3–5 years" law; treat freshness as context-dependent and verify when the stakes are high (public brand voice, comedy, youth marketing).

## False friends in idioms

Watch L1 calques that look like idioms (*make a party*, *I have cold*). Correct as collocation/L1 class errors (`learners.md`).
