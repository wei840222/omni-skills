# Sources

Use these pages to check a register label, a pronoun pair, or a particle gloss. They describe usage; they are not a license to invent slang.

## Register and pronouns

- **Badan Bahasa — Kelaziman Penggunaan Kata Ganti** — `saya`, `aku`, `kami`, `kita`, `kamu`, and `Anda` carry different formality and inclusion. https://badanbahasa.kemendikdasmen.go.id/artikel/4107/kelaziman-penggunaan-kata-ganti
- **Badan Bahasa — Penggunaan Sapaan Anda** — `Anda` is a formal, neutral address, not the default of casual chat. https://badanbahasa.kemendikdasmen.go.id/artikel/1347/penggunaan-sapaan-anda
- **Wikibooks Indonesian — Pronouns** — learner map of formal versus informal pronouns, including Jakarta `gua`/`lu` as colloquial rather than standard. https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Indonesian/Pronouns

## Particles and casual writing

- **Badan Bahasa — Partikel pada Bahasa Gaul** — `dong`, `deh`, `sih`, and `kok` are attested gaul particles; `nih` is a spoken variant of `ini`. https://badanbahasa.kemendikdasmen.go.id/artikel/2861/partikel-pada-bahasa-gaul
- **Universitas Indonesia — Partikel sih, deh, dong, kok** — these particles add interpersonal force; meaning depends on the sentence, not a single English gloss. https://lib.ui.ac.id/detail?id=20302414
- **SEASite — Indonesian slang** — colloquial shortcuts (`nggak`, `udah`, `gimana`, `gitu`) and Jakarta `lu-gue` are informal writing conventions, not EYD replacements. https://seasiatalk.indiana.edu/indonesian/language/slang.php

## Spelling boundary

- **Badan Bahasa — EYD** — formal Indonesian follows Ejaan yang Disempurnakan. Spoken shortcuts stay outside baku drafts. https://ejaan.kemendikdasmen.go.id/
