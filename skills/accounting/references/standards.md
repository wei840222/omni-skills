# Accounting Standards

Use this reference when choosing a reporting framework or citing standard-setters. Prefer the user's stated framework; when treatment differs, name both GAAP and IFRS outcomes.

## International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS)

- Issued by the IFRS Foundation / International Accounting Standards Board (IASB)
- Principle-based global standards used in many jurisdictions outside the United States
- Common touchpoints in this skill: IFRS 15 (revenue), IFRS 16 (leases), IAS 16/38 (PPE/intangibles)
- Overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Financial_Reporting_Standards
- Official starting point: https://www.ifrs.org/

## US Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (US GAAP)

- Default standards for US-based public companies; private entities may follow ASC or special-purpose frameworks
- Codified in the FASB Accounting Standards Codification (ASC)
- Common touchpoints: ASC 606 (revenue), ASC 842 (leases), ASC 360 (PPE)
- Overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generally_Accepted_Accounting_Principles_(United_States)
- Standard-setter: https://www.fasb.org/

## Material GAAP vs IFRS differences to flag early

| Topic | Typical US GAAP note | Typical IFRS note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inventory | LIFO permitted | LIFO prohibited |
| Development costs | Usually expensed | May capitalize when criteria met |
| Leases (lessee) | Finance vs operating under ASC 842 | IFRS 16: nearly all on-balance-sheet finance model |
| Revaluation of PPE | Generally cost model | Revaluation model allowed in some cases |

Always confirm jurisdiction, entity type, and the course/employer framework before prescribing entries.
