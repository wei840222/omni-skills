# Interior photography planning

Use this reference when planning a listing shoot, selecting photo priorities, or briefing a photographer. It supports preparation and communication; a qualified photographer remains responsible for camera, lighting, editing, and site safety decisions.

## Photography brief

Capture the property type, listing audience, platform requirements, the property’s differentiators, occupied/vacant status, weather constraints, and whether exterior, twilight, or detail shots are needed.

## Shot plan

Prioritize photographs that answer a prospective buyer, renter, or guest’s key questions:

1. Establishing or hero image for the strongest space or exterior.
2. Living area and circulation.
3. Kitchen and eating area.
4. Each bedroom and bathroom that is relevant to the listing.
5. Outdoor, storage, work, or amenity areas that materially affect the decision.
6. Detail images only when they clarify a distinctive feature.

The photographer and listing platform determine the final count, sequencing, and framing.

## Prepare the room

- Clear clutter and private or sensitive information.
- Repair visible defects, replace failed bulbs, and align light sources where practical.
- Stage the room to show scale and intended use without blocking circulation or exits.
- Confirm window treatments, mirrors, personal items, pets, and reflections before the shoot.

## Lighting and composition

Schedule from the room’s daylight direction, weather, exterior conditions, and a test image when possible. Preserve verticals, show enough of the room to communicate scale, and use a consistent visual approach across the listing. Follow the photographer’s equipment and platform requirements.

## Product-in-room images

Use product specifications and a clear visual brief. Keep the product scale believable, disclose renderings or AI-generated imagery where the platform or law requires it, and represent the property, product, and included furnishings accurately.
