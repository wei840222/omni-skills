# Constraints and decision traps

Load this reference before a layout, product, material, or staging recommendation.

## 1. Gather space facts before specifics

For a furniture layout or named product, collect:

- room length, width, and ceiling height in the user’s unit system;
- door widths and swing or access constraints;
- window positions and usable wall lengths;
- fixed elements and furniture that must remain; and
- household, accessibility, maintenance, budget, and timeline constraints.

When key measurements are missing, give directional guidance and state which measurement would unlock a specific layout or product comparison.

## 2. Localize purchasing advice

Before naming a retailer, supplier, or product as a purchase recommendation:

1. Ask for the user’s country or city and delivery constraints.
2. Confirm the retailer is accessible in that region.
3. Check current availability, delivery, assembly, return terms, and total cost.
4. Label the recommendation with the checked date and assumptions.

## 3. Keep recommendations within one budget tier

Establish a budget, mid-range, or premium tier from the user’s stated budget and priorities. Explain when a proposed mix crosses tiers, then offer a coherent alternative rather than presenting incompatible expectations as one plan.

## 4. Verify the access route

For a large item, compare the item’s packed and assembled dimensions with every relevant doorway, hallway, stair, elevator, and turning point. Confirm whether it ships flat-packed, can be assembled in the room, or needs a delivery professional. Manufacturer instructions and local site conditions are authoritative.

## 5. Sequence physical work

Coordinate the project in this order unless a qualified professional specifies otherwise:

```text
Structural → electrical/plumbing/HVAC → surfaces → fixed furniture → movable furniture → decor
```

Before flooring, verify the substrate and installation requirements. Before lighting fixtures, verify electrical points and fixture ratings. Before paint selection, view samples under the intended daylight and artificial lighting. Before delivery of movable furniture, confirm the surface is ready and the access route is clear.

## 6. Use staging and photography deliberately

For a sale or rental listing, first define the business goal, audience, budget, listing constraints, and photographer requirements. Prioritize the spaces that communicate the property’s strongest value, reduce visual clutter, and use a shot plan from `references/photography.md`. Describe projected financial impact as an assumption unless local comparable data supports it.

## 7. Surface risk before execution

Escalate to the relevant local authority or qualified professional when the decision involves structure, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire safety, accessibility, permits, landlord approval, or hazardous materials.

## Common decision traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Positive alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Scale hallucination | A generic furniture category has no reliable size | Compare the actual product specification with the measured space and access route |
| Style projection | Popular imagery may not suit the user or building | Start from confirmed preferences, retained pieces, and architectural context |
| Availability assumption | Retail and delivery coverage vary by region | Verify current regional availability before naming a purchase |
| Maintenance blindness | Materials must fit real household use | Ask about pets, children, cleaning capacity, moisture, and sunlight |
| Trend lock-in | Permanent finishes are costly to replace | Reserve trend-forward choices for reversible layers and explain the trade-off |
| Logistics omission | Product fit is not delivery feasibility | Include access, assembly, delivery, and return constraints in the comparison |
