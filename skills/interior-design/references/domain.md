# Evidence and regulated-work boundaries

Use this reference when a recommendation touches regulated work, safety, accessibility, lighting performance, or an externally sourced claim. Local building codes, permits, lease rules, and product instructions control over generic planning guidance.

## Interior-design planning boundary

Interior design coordinates function, circulation, materials, lighting, and presentation. It is not a substitute for a licensed architect, engineer, electrician, plumber, contractor, inspector, accessibility specialist, or hazardous-material professional. Route work in those disciplines to the appropriate local professional before execution.

## Lead-safe renovation

For pre-1978 housing in the United States, renovation can disturb lead-based paint. Before sanding, cutting, or demolition, identify the jurisdiction and building age, then determine whether the work is covered by the applicable lead-safe renovation requirements and whether a certified firm is required. Use the applicable containment, cleaning, verification, and local notification procedures.

Source:
- **EPA — Renovation, Repair and Painting Program**: federal requirements and certified-firm information for covered work in pre-1978 homes and child-occupied facilities. https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program

## Furniture stability

For tall or heavy furniture, ask about children, pets, seismic risk, and the wall construction. Check the manufacturer’s anchoring instructions and use an appropriate anchor or professional installation; treat stability as unverified until the specified anchoring approach is installed.

Source:
- **U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission — CPSC Approves New Federal Safety Standard for Clothing Storage Units to Prevent Tip-Over Deaths and Injuries**: describes the federal clothing-storage-unit safety standard and the tip-over risk. https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2022/CPSC-Approves-New-Federal-Safety-Standard-for-Clothing-Storage-Units-to-Prevent-Tip-Over-Deaths-and-Injuries

## Accessibility

When a user identifies an accessibility need, ask which jurisdiction and user needs apply. Use the applicable local code and project professional for compliance decisions; in the United States, the U.S. Access Board publishes the ADA Accessibility Standards as a primary reference.

Source:
- **U.S. Access Board — ADA Accessibility Standards**: official ADA accessibility standards and guidance entry point. https://www.access-board.gov/ada/

## Lighting and energy claims

For lighting recommendations, separate desired appearance from measured light level, color quality, dimming compatibility, fixture rating, and local electrical requirements. Verify the product label and manufacturer documentation rather than assuming equivalent brightness from wattage alone.

Source:
- **ENERGY STAR — Learn About LED Bulbs**: consumer guidance on LED lighting selection and labeling. https://www.energystar.gov/products/lighting_fans/light_bulbs/learn_about_led_bulbs

## Source use

The sources above establish boundaries and decision prompts. Use them for boundaries and decision prompts; establish room dimensions, cost ranges, staging returns, and product availability with current region-specific evidence before presenting recommendations.
