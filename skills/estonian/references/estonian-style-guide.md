# Estonian Style Guide

Progressive-disclosure detail for casual Estonian phrasing. Load when drafting or repairing informal Estonian after `SKILL.md` has locked the register.

## Formality default

Casual Estonian is understated and direct. Lean towards a casual register. Favor `Tere` or `Hei` over formal greetings, and `Jah` or `Jep` over formal affirmations.

## Sina vs Teie

Critical distinction:

- `Teie`: formal, elderly, professional
- `sina`: friends, peers, internet, casual
- Estonian internet uses `sina`
- Relying exclusively on `Teie` creates a stiff, overly distant tone

## Estonian understatement

Estonians are famously reserved:

- Less enthusiastic than many other cultures
- Understatement is the norm
- `Päris hea` (quite good) = high praise

## Particles and softeners

These make Estonian natural:

- `ju`: shared knowledge (`Sa ju tead`)
- `küll`: reassurance (`Küll saab`)
- `ikka`: "still", emphasis
- `noh`: filler, "well"
- `vist`: "probably"

## Fillers and flow

Real Estonian has fillers:

- `noh`, `nagu`, `siis`
- `tead`, `kuule`
- `tegelikult`, `muide`
- `nii-öelda`, `onju`

## Expressiveness

Keep it understated:

- Hea → `Päris hea`, `Lahe`, `Äge`
- Halb → `Kehv`, `Jama`
- Väga → `Täiega`, `Mega`, `Päris`

Reserve explicit slang for contexts where it is specifically requested; otherwise rely on standard casual terms.

## Common expressions

Natural expressions:

- `Okei`, `Selge`, `Saan aru`
- `Pole hullu`, `Pole probleemi`
- `Tõesti?`, `Päriselt?`, `Mis?`
- `Äge!`, `Lahe!`, `Kõva!`

## Reactions

React naturally:

- `Tõesti?`, `Päriselt?`, `Ei või olla!`
- `Oih!`, `Vau!`, `Jumal!`
- `Äge!`, `Lahe!`, `Nice!`
- `Haha`, `lol` in text when the channel already mixes English
