# Estonian language sources

Use these pages to check a register label, a sina/Teie mapping, a particle gloss, or understatement. They describe usage; they are not a license to invent slang or a dialect the user did not ask for.

## Register, pronouns, and formality

- **Wikipedia — Estonian language** — overview of Estonian register, orthography, and modern spoken/written norms. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_language
- **Wikipedia — Estonian grammar** — morphology and grammatical categories that affect pronoun and verb choice. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_grammar
- **Wikipedia — T–V distinction** — second-person formality contrasts; Estonian uses `sina` vs `Teie`. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%E2%80%93V_distinction
- **Wiktionary — sina** — Estonian second-person singular informal pronoun. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/sina#Estonian
- **Wiktionary — teie** — Estonian second-person plural / formal form. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/teie#Estonian

## Lexicography and official language resources

- **Institute of the Estonian Language (EKI)** — official language institute landing page and tooling. https://eki.ee/
- **Sõnaveeb** — EKI dictionary portal for contemporary Estonian lemmas and usage. https://sonaveeb.ee/
- **Õigekeelsussõnaraamat archive (ÕS)** — normative orthography dictionary archive hosted by EKI. https://arhiiv.eki.ee/dict/qs/
- **Keeleklikk** — Estonian language-learning portal (successor landing for older Keeletee links). https://www.keeleklikk.ee/

## Particles and discourse markers

- **Wiktionary — noh** — discourse particle / filler used in casual Estonian. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/noh#Estonian
- **Wiktionary — ju** — particle marking shared knowledge. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ju#Estonian
- **Wiktionary — küll** — reassurance / emphasis particle. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/k%C3%BCll#Estonian
