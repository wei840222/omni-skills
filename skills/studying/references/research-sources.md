# Research Sources — Studying

Use these sources to ground the learning-science claims in `SKILL.md`; treat individual study contexts and accessibility needs as inputs to adapt the plan.

## Retrieval practice

- **Roediger & Karpicke (2006), Test-Enhanced Learning** — https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x
  - Supports using retrieval practice rather than repeated study for delayed retention.

## Distributed practice and review intervals

- **Cepeda et al. (2008), Spacing Effects in Learning** — https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x
  - Supports scheduling repeated review across the period before a final test; use the learner's deadline and prior performance to choose an interval.

## Successive relearning

- **Rawson & Dunlosky (2011), Optimizing Schedules of Retrieval Practice for Durable and Efficient Learning** — https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023956
  - Supports repeated retrieval across sessions for durable learning.

## Interleaving

- **Rohrer & Taylor (2007), The Shuffling of Mathematics Problems Improves Learning** — https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-007-9015-8
  - Supports mixing already-acquired problem types so learners practice selecting a method.

## Desirable difficulties and judgment of learning

- **Bjork & Bjork (2011), Making Things Hard on Yourself, but in a Good Way** — https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/09/Bjork_Bjork_2011.pdf
  - Supports delayed retrieval as a stronger signal of retention than immediate fluency.

## Learning-technique evidence

- **Dunlosky et al. (2013), Improving Students’ Learning With Effective Learning Techniques** — https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266
  - Supports prioritizing practice testing and distributed practice; use rereading, highlighting, and summarization as a preparation pass that produces retrieval prompts.
