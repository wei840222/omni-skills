# College Targeting

## Score Ranges by College Tier (planning bands)

These are **planning bands**, not guarantees. Always verify the latest published middle-50% or superscore policy on each college's admissions site.

| Tier | Examples | Typical ACT planning band |
|------|----------|---------------------------|
| Ivy+ | Harvard, MIT, Stanford | 34–36 |
| Top 20 | Duke, Northwestern, UCLA | 32–35 |
| Top 50 | Boston U, Wisconsin, UF | 28–33 |
| State flagships | Ohio State, Penn State | 26–31 |
| Regional / broader access | Varies widely | 20–26 |

## Superscore Strategy

Many colleges consider the highest section scores across multiple ACT dates.

**When a retake is high-ROI:**
- One section sits well below the others
- The student has a concrete drill plan for that section
- Time and registration budget allow another date

**Retake planning:**
- Focus prep on the lowest section while doing light maintenance on others
- Confirm whether each target superscores ACT (and whether they require Science)

## Scholarship Thresholds

Automatic merit grids at many public universities jump at round cutoffs (often near 28–30+ and again in the low-to-mid 30s). Treat published scholarship charts as primary; do not invent award amounts.

## Score Submission Strategy

- ACT lets students control which test dates to send in many cases—confirm current MyACT sending rules before advising "hide this date"
- Useful default arc: first official sitting by spring of junior year when possible; fall senior retake if needed
- Build superscore intentionally rather than repeating full-battery cram every time

## Writing and Science requirements

For each college on the list, record:
- ACT Writing: required / recommended / not considered
- ACT Science: required for admission review, recommended, or unused when Composite omits it
- Whether the college publishes separate guidance for enhanced ACT reporting

Most highly selective colleges have de-emphasized Writing since ~2020, but exceptions remain—check, do not assume.
