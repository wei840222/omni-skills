# Section-Specific Strategies

Use `references/exam-config.md` for current timing. Strategies below apply to both enhanced and legacy materials; scale pacing drills to the examinee's actual section length.

## English

**What it tests:** Grammar, punctuation, sentence structure, rhetoric / revision decisions.

**High-ROI topics:**
- Comma rules (most common errors)
- Subject-verb agreement
- Pronoun agreement and case
- Parallel structure
- Redundancy (shorter is usually better)

**Pacing trap:** Rereading the entire passage for every item. Read surrounding sentences first.

**When stuck:** For "best placement" / "which choice" items, eliminate choices that break grammar or add fluff before deep rhetoric analysis.

## Math

**What it tests:** Pre-algebra through trigonometry / early precalculus skills typically taught by early grade 12.

**Topic emphasis (legacy distribution still useful for drills):**
- Pre-algebra / elementary algebra: ~20–25%
- Intermediate algebra / coordinate geometry: ~25–30%
- Plane geometry: ~20–25%
- Trigonometry: ~10%

**High-ROI topics:**
- Word-problem setup (not calculator thrash)
- Coordinate geometry (slopes, midpoints, distance)
- Special triangles (30-60-90, 45-45-90)
- Basic trig (SOHCAHTOA, unit circle)

**Pacing trap:** Burning time on late hard items. Every item has equal weight—park and return.

**Calculator strategy:** Use for arithmetic and graph checks; still write the setup.

## Reading

**What it tests:** Comprehension, evidence, and multi-source reasoning across passages.

**Common passage mix (order may vary by form):**
1. Prose fiction / literary narrative
2. Social science
3. Humanities
4. Natural science

**Strategy choice (pick one and practice it):**
- Read passage first → answer (fast readers)
- Skim → questions → targeted reread (medium)
- Questions first → hunt evidence (very slow readers)

**Pacing trap:** Perfecting one passage. Budget time per passage from the actual section clock and move on.

**Evidence discipline:** Prefer answers anchored in the text. If the choice requires a large inference, re-check the lines.

## Science (when registered)

**What it tests:** Interpretation, analysis, and scientific reasoning from graphs, tables, and experiment summaries—not memorized advanced science facts.

**Passage types:**
- Data Representation (graphs, tables): usually fastest
- Research Summaries (experiments): medium
- Conflicting Viewpoints: hardest—map each view before answering

**Critical insight:** Jump to figures and axis labels first; skim background only as needed.

**Pacing trap:** Reading dense intros carefully before touching questions.

## Writing (optional)

**What it tests:** Argument analysis and composition under time.

**Reliable structure:**
- Intro with clear thesis
- Body paragraphs that engage the given perspectives and your position
- Short conclusion

**Scoring reminder:** Writing is reported separately and does not change Composite.

**Only prep if:** Target colleges require or recommend a Writing score, or the student wants essay practice for placement.
