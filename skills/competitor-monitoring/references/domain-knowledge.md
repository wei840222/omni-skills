# Competitor Monitoring Domain Knowledge

## Strategic Frameworks

When analyzing competitors and organizing dossiers, incorporate standard strategic frameworks to provide depth and structure to the analysis.

### 1. Porter's Five Forces
Use this to assess the overall competitive intensity and attractiveness of the market:
*   **Competitive Rivalry:** How many competitors are there? What is the quality of their products/services?
*   **Threat of New Entrants:** How easy is it for new players to enter the market? (Barriers to entry)
*   **Threat of Substitutes:** Can customers easily find alternative ways to solve their problem?
*   **Bargaining Power of Buyers:** Can customers easily drive prices down?
*   **Bargaining Power of Suppliers:** Can suppliers drive up the cost of inputs?

### 2. SWOT Analysis
Use this for head-to-head comparisons:
*   **Strengths:** What does the competitor do exceptionally well? (Internal)
*   **Weaknesses:** Where are they vulnerable? (Internal)
*   **Opportunities:** What external trends could they capitalize on? (External)
*   **Threats:** What external factors could harm them? (External)

### 3. Feature Matrix (Capabilities)
A standard way to compare technical offerings. Rather than just a checklist, evaluate features on:
*   **Table Stakes:** Features everyone has (no differentiation).
*   **Differentiators:** Features unique to a specific competitor.
*   **Delighters:** Features that customers love, even if not strictly required.

## Key Terminology
*   **Positioning:** How a product is designed to be perceived in the minds of the target market relative to competitors.
*   **Direct Competitors:** Offer the same products/services to the same market.
*   **Indirect Competitors:** Offer different products/services but solve the same problem.
*   **Substitute:** A completely different product or service that a customer could use instead (e.g., using Excel instead of a CRM).
*   **Value Proposition:** A promise of value to be delivered, communicated, and acknowledged.
