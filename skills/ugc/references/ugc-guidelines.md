# UGC guidelines (progressive disclosure)

Operational patterns for authentic short-form user-generated content. Pair with `sources.md` before asserting live policy, product UI, or fee claims.

## Hook (first 1–3 seconds)

- Pattern interrupt or curiosity gap that the body can pay off
- Face close to camera when intimacy helps attention
- Motion in frame one; static opens lose scrollers
- Text overlay that works sound-off
- Surprising claim only when the rest of the video supports it
- "Wait for it" only with a real payoff

## Authenticity over production

- Phone-quality often beats studio polish for native feeds
- Real people and environments over perfect lighting sets
- Conversational speech; avoid teleprompter cadence
- Strategic imperfections can increase trust
- POV / in-use demo beats pure talking-head claims

## Formats that work

- Problem → solution
- Tutorial / demo with product in use
- Before / after transformation
- Genuine reaction
- Story time with product woven in
- Social-proof overlays (reviews/page) when truthful and permitted

## Platform notes (adjust energy; do not identical cross-post)

- **TikTok**: trends/sounds, fast pace, native vertical
- **Instagram Reels**: slightly more polish acceptable; still native-first
- **YouTube Shorts**: can lean informational with longer retention arcs
- Re-check each platform's current creative/ad policies before paid boost

## Trends and sounds

- Trending audio can help distribution; add a unique spin
- Trends expire quickly; maintain a swipe file under `<state_root>/swipe-file.md`
- Original audio is worth testing when on-brand

## Creator brief checklist

- One main benefit (not five)
- Audience description (who should feel spoken to)
- Mandatory elements: product visible, key phrase, CTA
- Execution freedom for the creator
- Reference examples (links the user already owns or can share legally)
- Explicit exclusions (claims, phrases, competitors, unsafe demos)

## Call to action

- Single CTA (link in bio, code, trial, DM keyword—as authorized)
- After value; often last ~2 seconds
- Verbal + on-screen reinforcement
- Genuine urgency only
- Optional soft CTA in caption

## Testing and iteration

- Multiple hooks for the same body
- Multiple creators on the same brief when budget allows
- Variations on winners; kill losers fast
- Track by individual creative ID plus campaign
- Comment language can seed new hooks (with consent/policy care)

## Metrics that matter

- Hook rate / 3-second hold (creative open quality)
- Completion / average watch time (body quality)
- Saves > shares > comments > likes as intent-weighted engagement
- Click-through and cost per result for efficiency
- Directional benchmarks often cited in practice: aim near ≥30% past-3s when measurable; revisit opens below ~20%; short-form completion aims vary by length—treat ≥20% completion/watch as a rough short-form floor only when the platform metric matches. Always label uncertainty.

## Creator selection

- Audience fit over raw follower count
- Content style match to brand energy
- Engagement quality and prior brand work
- Micro-creators often win trust and efficiency

## Volume strategy

- Algorithm learning needs volume; perfect-one-video rarely wins
- Concept × creator matrices
- Repurpose winners with new hooks/lengths
- Seasonal angles when relevant

## Common mistakes

- Too branded too early
- Product appears after viewers already left
- Weak generic open
- No clear CTA
- Creator/audience mismatch
- Single untested creative carrying the campaign

## Creative fatigue

UGC/paid UGC often fatigues within weeks. Maintain a pipeline of new hooks and angles; do not freeze a single winner indefinitely.
