# Partnership frameworks

## Partnership types

| Type | Description | When to use |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Distribution** | Partner sells or promotes your product | Need reach without building a full sales team |
| **Technology** | Product integration | Shared customers want a seamless path |
| **Co-marketing** | Joint campaigns or content | Overlapping audience, complementary offers |
| **Strategic** | Deep operational alignment | Long-term category positioning |
| **Channel** | Resellers, affiliates, referrals | Scale acquisition with clear economics |

## Partner evaluation criteria

Before pursuing any partnership:

- **Strategic alignment** — goals complement rather than cannibalize
- **Capability match** — they can actually deliver
- **Cultural fit** — teams can operate together
- **Economic viability** — unit economics work for both sides
- **Reputation** — association strengthens brand

## Partnership lifecycle

### 1. Identification

- Map the partner universe by type
- Score strategic value and accessibility
- Prefer warm introductions
- Study their partnership history and preferences

### 2. Outreach

- Lead with value for them
- Show homework on their business
- Propose a specific collaboration rather than a vague "partnership"
- Find the right contact (BD, partnerships, exec sponsor)

### 3. Negotiation

- Start with a term sheet on key points
- Define success metrics up front
- Treat exclusivity as expensive; require commitment in return
- Build review periods and exit paths

### 4. Launch

- Joint kickoff with named owners
- Shared communication channel and dashboard
- Regular check-in cadence

### 5. Optimization

- Quarterly business reviews
- Expansion only after metrics clear
- Address friction early
- Document learnings for the next partner

## Red flags

- Exclusivity demand without matching commitment
- No clear owner on their side
- Misaligned urgency or timelines
- Pattern of failed partnerships
- Terms that compromise the core product or brand

## Agreement essentials

- Scope and responsibilities
- Economic terms and payment timing
- Success metrics and reporting
- Term length and renewal
- Exit, IP ownership, data return
- Dispute path

Prefer pilots with shared KPIs before broad exclusivity. For live counters with
hard limits, hand off to `negotiate`.
