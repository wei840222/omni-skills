# Deal negotiation tactics

Orientation for commercial deal design. Live counters with principal hard limits,
autonomy ladders, and commitment gates belong in `negotiate`.

## Pre-negotiation preparation

### Know your position

- **BATNA** — best alternative if this deal does not close; stronger BATNA = more leverage
- **Walk-away point** — define before entering the room
- **Target outcome** — what "good" looks like
- **Opening position** — start with justification, not a naked number

### Know their position

- Constraints (budget, timeline, internal politics)
- Alternatives (who else they are talking to)
- Decision process (decider vs influencers)
- Internal success metrics they must show

### Preparation checklist

- [ ] Researched company and recent news
- [ ] Mapped stakeholders and priorities
- [ ] Prepared supporting data
- [ ] Anticipated objections
- [ ] Listed non-negotiables
- [ ] Listed tradeable concessions

## Tactics

### Opening

- Anchor with a justified first number when you should set the frame
- Listen first; let them reveal priorities
- Ask questions before proposing packages
- Frame value they receive rather than only what you want

### Middle

- Exchange value — every concession gets something back
- Package issues rather than point-by-point whittling
- Use silence carefully; summarize progress often

### Closing

- Use only legitimate urgency (real deadlines, capacity)
- Frame final moves with reasons
- Get verbal alignment, then written terms immediately

## Common scenarios

### They have more leverage

- Emphasize unique value and long-term relationship
- Trade creative terms (case study rights, phased scope) rather than pure price cuts
- Be willing to walk; desperation is visible

### They are aggressive

- Stay calm; name the dynamic without escalating
- Focus on underlying interests rather than fixed positions
- Take breaks when needed

### Multiple stakeholders

- Map the buying committee early
- Find a champion
- Address each stakeholder's concern
- Align approval timing

### Competitive deal

- Differentiate on unique value beyond price
- Understand the competitor offer without trash talk
- Create switching costs only through real product value

## Term sheet essentials

### Economics

- Amount and structure
- Timing and triggers
- Performance components
- Minimum commitments
- Escalation clauses

### Scope

- Included / excluded
- Geo or market limits
- Exclusivity (priced, time-bounded)

### Duration

- Initial term, renewal, notice, auto-renew vs opt-in

### Protection

- IP ownership, confidentiality, non-solicit where lawful
- Indemnification and liability caps

### Exit

- Termination for convenience / cause
- Wind-down obligations
- Data and transition requirements

## Red flags

- Rushed timeline with no clear reason
- Reluctance to put terms in writing
- Constantly changing requirements
- Decision maker never available
- Terms that seem too good
- Pressure to skip diligence
