# Revenue strategy

## Why diversify

- **Risk** — single-source dependency is a survival risk
- **Ceiling** — channels saturate
- **Margin mix** — channels have different economics
- **Resilience** — multi-stream revenue is harder to attack

## Customer concentration

| Concentration | Risk | Action |
|---------------|------|--------|
| Top customer >30% | Critical | Immediate diversification; protect renewal length |
| Top 3 >50% | High | Accelerate net-new and expansion outside the cluster |
| Top 10 >80% | Moderate | Build pipeline depth |
| No customer >10% | Low | Maintain balance |

These bands are operating heuristics for portfolio risk, not accounting standards.
Re-check finance policy with `cfo` when covenants or audit definitions apply.

## Direct vs indirect

**Direct:** higher margin and relationship ownership; slower scale; higher CAC.

**Channel/partner:** faster reach; lower margin and control; depends on partner priorities.

## Pricing models (commercial lens)

| Model | Best for | Watch out |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| Subscription | Ongoing value | Activation and churn |
| Usage-based | Variable consumption | Revenue unpredictability |
| Per-seat | Team collaboration | Seat consolidation |
| Transaction fee | Marketplaces / payments | Volume dependency |
| Hybrid | Complex products | Confusion and quote errors |

Detailed list-price design, WTP studies, and grandfathering mechanics → `pricing`.
Charge implementation → `billing`.

## Channel strategy

1. Direct sales — high touch
2. Inside sales — lower touch
3. Self-serve — product-led
4. Partners — resellers / distributors
5. Affiliates — referral commission
6. Marketplaces — platform listings

For each channel track CAC, ACV, cycle length, support load, margin after channel
cost, and who owns the customer relationship.

### Multi-channel conflict

- Segment rules (size, geo, vertical)
- Deal registration
- Protected accounts
- Differentiated offers by channel
- Transparent conflict resolution

## Pricing principles (deal-facing)

- Value-based rather than pure cost-plus
- Segment-specific where needed
- Simple enough to explain
- Room to negotiate without breaking the floor
- Review on a cadence as markets move

### Price increase playbook

1. Communicate value before the increase
2. Advance notice (commonly 60–90 days for B2B SaaS; confirm contract language)
3. Grandfather or phase loyal cohorts when retention risk is high
4. Justify with delivered improvements
5. Offer downgrade paths
6. Human outreach for large accounts

## Revenue operations

- Clear stage definitions and historical probabilities
- Pipeline reviews and aging policies
- Win/loss analysis
- Forecast = bottoms-up commits + tops-down trends; track accuracy
- Segment new vs expansion vs renewal; include churn in net views

### Weekly dashboard

- Pipeline by stage
- Stage conversion
- Average deal size
- Cycle length
- Win rate
- Revenue by channel/segment
- Churn and expansion revenue

Pipeline system of record → `crm`.
