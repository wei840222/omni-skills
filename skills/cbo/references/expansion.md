# Market expansion playbooks

## Should you expand?

Answer before committing resources:

1. **Is the current market saturated?**
   - If penetration is still low, maximize the core market first
   - Expansion multiplies cost and execution risk
2. **Do you have product-market fit?**
   - Fix retention and core value before multiplying geography
3. **Is demand validated?**
   - Inbound, design partners, competitor proof, or primary research
4. **Can you resource it?**
   - Half-committed expansion fails
   - Prefer a dedicated owner rather than a side project

## Expansion types

### Geographic

**Validate before commit:**

- Demand tests (landing page, waitlist, targeted outreach)
- Interviews with potential customers in-market
- Competitor presence and positioning
- Regulatory and data requirements

| Approach | Investment | Speed | Control |
|----------|------------|-------|---------|
| Distributor | Low | Fast | Low |
| Local partner | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| Direct presence | High | Slow | High |

**Localization checklist:**

- [ ] Language and cultural context (not word-for-word only)
- [ ] Local payment methods
- [ ] Pricing vs purchasing power
- [ ] Legal/compliance (data, consumer protection)
- [ ] Support hours and language
- [ ] Dominant marketing channels in-market

### Vertical

1. Identify transferable value across industries
2. Map required customization
3. Start with design-partner beachheads
4. Hire or borrow vertical expertise
5. Collect reference cases before broad claims

### Upmarket / downmarket

**Upmarket (SMB → enterprise):** longer cycles, more stakeholders, security and
implementation load, different packaging.

**Downmarket (enterprise → SMB):** self-serve paths, lower-touch support, price
sensitivity, simpler SKU, marketing-led acquisition.

## Beachhead strategy

Target segments sequentially:

1. Pick one segment in the new market
2. Achieve credible density there
3. Use proof points for the next segment
4. Expand systematically rather than everywhere at once

**Beachhead criteria:** small enough to win with current resources, large enough
to matter, connected to a bigger opportunity, customers willing to reference.

## Expansion metrics (separate from core)

- CAC by market
- Time to first revenue
- Retention vs core
- NPS or satisfaction by region
- Revenue per customer
- Support ticket ratio

## Common failures

- Too early (before PMF)
- Under-resourced
- Copy-paste GTM without localization
- Rushed partner selection
- Ignoring negative early signals

Re-check live market and regulatory sources in `references/sources.md` before
stating country-specific compliance or payment claims as fact.
