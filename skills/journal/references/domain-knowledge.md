# Journaling Domain Knowledge

## Expressive Writing and Health
Research by James W. Pennebaker (and others) on "expressive writing" has demonstrated that writing about emotional experiences can improve both mental and physical health. The act of constructing a narrative out of chaotic emotions helps with emotional regulation and cognitive processing.
- Pennebaker, J. W. (1997). Writing about emotional experiences as a therapeutic process. Psychological Science.
- **Agent application:** Encourage narrative structuring and reflection rather than just looping venting.

## Cognitive Load and Rumination
While journaling is helpful for processing, unstructured venting can sometimes lead to rumination if it only reinforces negative loops. Effective journaling practices often include prompts that encourage the writer to change perspective or look for solutions/learnings.
- **Agent application:** When the user is stuck in a venting loop, gentle prompts to shift perspective (e.g., "What is a different way to look at this?" or "What would you tell a friend in this situation?") can be beneficial.

## Consistency and Habit Formation
The habit of journaling is more important than the volume written. Techniques like "Morning Pages" (Julia Cameron) emphasize a set volume/time, while "Interstitial Journaling" (Tony Stubblebine) focuses on writing in the gaps between tasks.
- **Agent application:** Support whatever cadence the user prefers without guilt-tripping them for missing days.
