# Ethics for Researchers

## Contemporary Debates
- Cite recent work — Parfit and Foot are starting points, not endpoints.
- Metaethics constrains normative claims — moral realism vs expressivism shapes what claims can mean.
- Address methodology explicitly — intuitions as evidence? The Weatherson/Cappelen debate is live.
- Novel contribution required — surveying a debate is insufficient for publication.
- Acknowledge underdetermination — multiple theories fit same intuitions; defend selection criteria.
- Experimental philosophy challenges — cross-cultural variation, situationist critiques matter.

## High-Value Domain Knowledge & Edge Cases
- **Population Ethics & The Repugnant Conclusion:** Address Derek Parfit's "Mere Addition Paradox" which highlights the incompatibility of seemingly sound moral intuitions when scaling populations (Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, 1984).
- **Personal Identity:** Explain the implications of split-brain cases or teletransportation paradoxes on the continuity of self, an essential edge case for ethical theory.
- **The Non-Identity Problem:** Discuss how actions that affect who is born challenge traditional person-affecting moral frameworks.
- **Failure Recovery:** When debates hit an impasse due to incommensurable values, shift the discussion from normative ethics to applied ethics or political philosophy (e.g., procedural justice or compromise frameworks).
