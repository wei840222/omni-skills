# Basque style guide

Use this reference after selecting the audience and register in `SKILL.md`. Examples illustrate choices; match the user's wording and the recipient rather than forcing every feature into an output.

## Formality and address

- **Casual / known peer:** `Kaixo`, direct `Bai` / `Ez`, and standard `zu` usually fit. Use `hi` / hika only when intimacy and region are explicit.
- **Professional, institutional, unfamiliar, older, or explicitly formal:** retain `zu`, fuller greetings such as `Egun on` or `Arratsalde on`, and avoid peer slang.
- Keep address, verb forms, greeting, and closing in the same register. Ask for the intended audience when it changes the choice materially.

## Batua and dialect flavor

- Default written output to batua unless the user names a dialect or supplies a dialect sample.
- Light regional vocabulary can add warmth; dense dialect morphology without a sample often reads as fake.
- Online mixed audiences usually expect batua with optional local color, not textbook purity or heavy Bizkaiera/Gipuzkera reconstruction.

## Particles, softeners, and fillers

Particles can make a casual message sound less translated when used sparingly:

| Marker | Typical effect | Example shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ba` | soft lead-in / "well" | `Ba, ikusiko dugu.` |
| `ala` | tag / confirmation | `Ondo dago, ala?` |
| `bada` | "so" / "well then" | `Bada, has gaitezen.` |
| `ezta` | "isn't it" soft check | `Erraza da, ezta?` |

Light fillers and discourse glue that can fit peer chat:

- `Ba`, `bueno`, `ondo`
- `Badakizu`, `ikusten?`
- `Beraz`, `dena den`
- `Begira`, `entzun`

A message does not need a filler or particle to be natural. In formal or concise writing, use none.

## Expressiveness and reactions

Prefer intensity that matches the source rather than the safest dictionary gloss:

- Positive: `Ondo` → `Oso ondo`, `Primeran`, `Bikain`
- Negative: `Gaizki` → `Txarto`, `Oso txarto`
- Quantity/intensity: `Asko` → `Oso`, `Izugarri` when the source is emphatic

Natural reactions:

- `Benetan?`, `Egia?`, `Egia al da?`, `Zer?`
- `Aiba!`, `Ostras!`, `Ene!`
- `Bikain!`, `Primeran!`, `Oso ondo!`, `Genial!`

Set phrases:

- `Ados`, `Ondo`, `Vale`
- `Ez dago arazorik`, `Lasai`
- `Zorionak` for congratulations contexts

Peer slang, heavy internet Spanish/French mixing, and text laughter such as `jajaja` are audience-specific. Keep them out of professional, institutional, sensitive, or mixed-audience text.

## Code-switching

- Casual bilingual chat may include Spanish or French inserts when that matches the speaker: `Oso interesting da.`
- Do not code-switch in formal, legal, medical, brand-critical, or explicitly monolingual requests.
- Preserve meaning first; mixing is seasoning, not a requirement.

## Syntax and output check

Basque word order and auxiliaries often differ from English. Prefer a clear natural sentence over a word-for-word English calque, while preserving emphasis and facts.

Before delivery:

1. Confirm `zu` or `hi` matches the recipient and remains consistent.
2. Confirm batua vs dialect intensity matches the request.
3. Remove any filler, slang, intensifier, or code-switch that changes the speaker's implied relationship or tone.
4. Keep names, dates, quantities, commitments, and safety-sensitive wording faithful to the source.
5. For publication, legal, medical, or brand-critical text, label this as a draft and route it to a qualified native reviewer when one is available.
