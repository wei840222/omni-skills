# Basque language sources

Use these pages to check a register label, a zu/hi mapping, a batua vs dialect claim, a particle gloss, or contested terminology. They describe usage; they are not a license to invent slang or a dialect the user did not ask for.

## Language overview and standard

- **Wikipedia — Basque language** — overview of Basque as a language isolate, orthography, and modern written/spoken norms. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language
- **Wikipedia — Standard Basque** — documents batua as the unified standard used in education, media, and much online writing. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Basque
- **Wikipedia — Batua** — redirect/overview of the standard form often labeled Euskara Batua. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batua
- **Wikipedia — Basque dialects** — Bizkaiera, Gipuzkera, and other regional varieties; use only as routing context, not as a free license for dense dialect invention. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_dialects
- **Wikipedia — Euskaltzaindia** — Royal Academy of the Basque Language; institutional context for batua standardization when the academy site is unavailable. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euskaltzaindia

## Address and formality

- **Wikipedia — T–V distinction** — second-person formality contrasts; Basque retains a living zu/hi split that is not universal-du. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%E2%80%93V_distinction
- **Wiktionary — zu** — Basque second-person polite / standard form. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/zu#Basque
- **Wiktionary — hi** — Basque second-person familiar form used in hika contexts. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/hi#Basque

## Lexicon samples used in style guidance

- **Wiktionary — kaixo** — common casual greeting. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/kaixo
- **Wiktionary — bai** — affirmative particle / "yes". https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bai#Basque
- **Wiktionary — ondo** — "well" / "good" baseline intensifier path. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ondo#Basque
- **Wikipedia — Code-switching** — bilingual mixing patterns relevant when Basque speakers insert Spanish or French in casual channels. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code-switching
