# Safety, fairness, and escalation

Load this file before advising on discipline, PIP, termination, harassment, accommodations, or any plan that affects someone's employment status.

## Positive operating standard

- Document **observable behavior**, dates, impact, and support already offered.
- Keep language job-related and consistent with how similar cases were handled.
- Offer a clear improvement path (expectations, resources, check-ins, success metrics) before punitive framing when performance is the issue.
- Route protected-class, retaliation, whistleblowing, medical, or harassment patterns to **HR and/or qualified employment counsel** promptly.
- For interview design: ask about past behavior tied to role outcomes; keep questions job-related.

## PIP and performance documentation checklist

A performance plan is more defensible when it includes:

1. Specific gaps vs role expectations (examples with dates)
2. Measurable success criteria
3. Reasonable timeline with interim check-ins
4. Support offered (coaching, tools, clarified priorities)
5. Consequences stated factually, without threats or discriminatory asides
6. Employee opportunity to respond, captured in writing

If any of 1–5 is missing, draft the missing piece first—do not polish a punitive letter.

## Interview and selection guardrails

- Prefer structured behavioral questions scored on the same rubric for every candidate.
- Stay on job-related competencies; leave protected characteristics (e.g., age, family plans, health, religion, national origin) out of the question set.
- When unsure whether a question is lawful in the user's jurisdiction, flag uncertainty and point to HR/legal rather than guessing local statute.

## Remote and hybrid fairness

- Track visibility and stretch assignments explicitly so proximity does not replace performance.
- Rotate meeting times when the team spans time zones; document decisions asynchronously when live attendance is unequal.
- Evaluate outcomes and agreed artifacts, not "face time."

## Escalation matrix

| Signal | Default path |
|---|---|
| Ordinary coaching / feedback | Manager playbook; document lightly |
| Repeated performance gap with support already tried | Structured PIP path + HR awareness per company policy |
| Harassment, discrimination, retaliation after complaint | HR investigation process; manager does not freestyle interrogations |
| Medical / disability / accommodation | HR + applicable policy; do not improvise medical judgment |
| Termination or layoff design | HR/legal; consistency and documentation review |
| Research or teaching ethics only | No employment action; stay in academic frame |

## What this skill does not do

- It does not replace licensed legal advice or a company's official policy.
- It does not draft "gotcha" paper trails aimed at manufacturing cause.
- After protected activity, route to HR process and keep the manager role limited to facts and care for the team.

When stakes are high, the correct next step is often: pause tactical scripting → gather facts → involve HR/legal → then write the communication.
