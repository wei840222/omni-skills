# Audience playbooks

Use the section that matches the user's role. Keep advice specific to the situation they described.

## Individual contributors: navigating upward

- Decode manager decisions through organizational pressure, budget, and competing priorities before assuming bad faith.
- Separate venting from issues that need action; propose a next step only for the latter.
- Before difficult conversations, check whether the manager's constraints are already visible in prior feedback.
- Build promotion cases from level gaps plus concrete evidence, not tenure narratives alone.
- Prefer problem + proposed option over pure escalation dumps.
- Flag politics when incentives conflict; suggest navigation that protects relationships where possible.
- Translate review phrases into standing and growth signals the IC can act on.
- Weigh visibility, impact, and relationship cost before recommending skipping a level.
- Keep professional documentation habits that protect the person without turning every note into a brief against someone.

## Students: academic foundations

- Choose frameworks by question type (industry structure, internal alignment, external scan, portfolio mix).
- Structure case answers as Issue → Analysis → Recommendation with ownership, timeline, and resources.
- Contrast textbook prescription with how managers adapt under incomplete data.
- Cite original thinkers accurately (Drucker on objectives, Mintzberg on strategy as craft, Kotter on leading change).
- Reject generic analysis that could fit any firm without firm-specific evidence.
- Challenge missing case data before locking a recommendation.
- Integrate concepts across courses when the prompt rewards synthesis.
- Default posture: good management answers are contingent on context, industry, culture, and timing.

## Practicing managers: daily leadership

- Build 1:1 agendas from recent team activity plus one career-development thread.
- When feedback is overdue, draft behavior-based talking points tied to observed work, not labels.
- PIP drafts need clear metrics, reasonable timelines, evidence of support offered, and non-discriminatory language—load safety reference before final wording.
- Interview kits: behavioral questions mapped to role outcomes; screen out illegal or proxy questions.
- Audit delegation for growth distribution and single points of failure.
- Treat early conflict signals as mediation work: interests, process, and behavior—before personality stories.
- Frame upward updates with decision asks executives can answer.
- Watch remote/hybrid fairness: proximity bias, unequal visibility, timezone meeting load.
- Document verbal agreements and performance conversations promptly while facts are fresh.

## Researchers: methodological rigor

- Check whether sample size supports the claimed effect size range; small-N universal claims need caution.
- Flag common-method variance when all variables are single-source self-report.
- Match venue intent: theory-building (often inductive) vs theory-testing (often deductive).
- Call out endogeneity risk in cross-sectional designs; prefer designs or instruments that address it when claiming causality.
- Qualitative work needs sampling logic, coding reliability, and saturation evidence—not anecdote piles.
- Discourage HARKing: separate confirmatory from exploratory analyses; prefer pre-registration when feasible.
- Treat published effects as potentially inflated under publication bias.
- Re-validate adapted scales rather than assuming construct validity transfers.
- Push for boundary conditions and moderators instead of universal laws.
- Close with practitioner-usable implications when bridging rigor and relevance.

## Educators: teaching excellence

- Open cases with protagonist-centered questions before revealing outcomes.
- Aim learning objectives at judgment under ambiguity, not framework recall alone.
- Prefer mixed assessment: simulation, live case, reflection—not exams only.
- Tune facilitation: executives need assumption challenges; undergraduates need foundational models first.
- Make the theory–practice gap explicit via action learning on real organizations.
- Correct misconceptions early: management ≠ control; analysis ≠ guaranteed outcomes; ethics is not a one-week module.
- Debrief experiential exercises; learning concentrates in structured reflection.
- Distribute ethics cases through the term rather than isolating them.
- Use peer learning: study groups, role-plays, structured peer feedback.

## HR and OD professionals: organizational systems

- Diagnose leadership competency gaps before prescribing development programs.
- Base succession on role requirements and evidence, not tenure or favoritism.
- Design 360 processes that protect psychological safety; warn when N is too small for anonymity.
- Apply change frameworks diagnostically (which phase is stuck) rather than as slide decoration.
- Separate culture symptoms from structural root causes (e.g., turnover after misaligned incentives).
- Keep coaching vs mentoring vs managing boundaries explicit in every developmental design.
- Model org-design side effects: span of control, matrix load, unclear decision rights.
- Write compliance-sensitive notes as if counsel will read them.
- Escalate investigations to HR process or external counsel to protect procedural integrity.
- Treat restructuring as a poor substitute for direct performance management when that is the real issue.
