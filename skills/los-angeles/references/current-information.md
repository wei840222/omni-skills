# Current Information and Verification

Los Angeles travel, housing, transit, weather, event, and local-service information changes frequently. Treat numerical ranges, schedules, availability, and service recommendations elsewhere in this skill as planning context rather than current quotes. Before presenting a mutable claim as current, verify it against the applicable primary source and state the date checked.

## Authoritative sources by decision

| Decision | Verify with | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Metro routes, service alerts, fares, and TAP payment | LA Metro | https://www.metro.net/riding/ |
| DMV deadlines, licenses, vehicle registration, and fees | California DMV | https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/ |
| City services, permits, and local government contacts | City of Los Angeles | https://lacity.gov/ |
| County services and public-health information | Los Angeles County | https://lacounty.gov/ |
| Weather, heat, wind, rain, and warnings | National Weather Service Los Angeles/Oxnard | https://www.weather.gov/lox/ |
| Wildfire incidents and evacuation information | CAL FIRE | https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents |
| Airport access and ground transportation | Los Angeles World Airports | https://www.flylax.com/ |
| Rental-market listings and availability | Current listings plus the relevant property manager | https://housing.lacity.org/ |
| Attractions, opening hours, reservations, and tickets | The attraction's official site | Use the venue's official website; do not rely on a historical price table. |

## Use safely

1. Identify whether the request needs a current fact or a general planning comparison.
2. For current facts, open the matching official source before quoting a price, schedule, rule, opening status, or availability.
3. If a source is unavailable or the answer changes by neighborhood, date, provider, or eligibility, say what remains unverified and give the user the source to confirm.
4. Keep general advice conditional: compare commute, budget, accessibility, safety needs, and trip priorities rather than making universal claims about a neighborhood or transport mode.
