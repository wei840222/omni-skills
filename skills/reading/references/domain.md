# Reading strategies and advice

Runtime domain rules for everyday reading coaching. Pair with `references/sources.md` when citing study-technique evidence.

## Before recommending books

1. Ask what they have read and liked — recommendations without taste context waste time.
2. Ask **why** they want this topic — learning, entertainment, or solving a specific problem.
3. Ask available time — 10 minutes/day vs 2 hours changes length, chapter size, and format.
4. Offer **one** next read (plus at most one alternate format of the same pick) before expanding the list.
5. Match format to life: commute → audiobook; fragmented attention → short chapters; deep focus block → print/ebook dense nonfiction.

## Matching approach to goal

| Goal | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Extract specific info | Skim, use index/TOC, read targeted chapters only |
| Deep learning | Slow read, notes, re-read hard sections, retrieve closed-book |
| Entertainment | Linear flow; protect immersion; minimize meta-interrupts |
| Decide if worth finishing | Sample opening + reliable reviews/summary; set a quit checkpoint |
| Research a topic | Multiple sources; cross-reference claims; keep a claim→source map |

Ask what outcome they need instead of assuming cover-to-cover reading.

### Practical method sketches

- **Skim path:** TOC → chapter openers/closers → index terms → only pages that answer the question.
- **Deep path:** preview questions → read a section → closed-book explain → mark misses → optional light re-read of misses only.
- **Entertainment path:** uninterrupted stretches; save analysis for after if they still want it.
- **Research path:** one question per session; stop when the question is answered well enough to act or write.

## Retention that works

Ground tactics in retrieval and spacing (see sources). Prefer:

- Explain-back in their own words — gaps show immediately.
- Connect new ideas to something they already know — isolated facts fade.
- One actionable takeaway per chapter — “What will you do with this?”
- Revisit after ~1 week with “What do you still remember?” — spaced recall beats passive rereading.
- Own-words summary over highlighting stacks — highlighting alone is a weak encoding strategy for most learners.

For durable multi-week review of atomic facts after reading, hand off scheduling detail to `spaced-repetition` / card skills; keep this skill on the reading session itself.

## When to suggest quitting or switching

- They gave a fair sample (about **50+ pages** or a comparable audio stretch) and are not engaged — sunk cost is not a reason to continue.
- They are forcing themselves — reading as punishment kills future reading.
- Level mismatch (far too dense or too thin) — offer a better-leveled alternate on the same goal.
- A summary, article, course chapter, or different book meets the goal faster — optimize for the goal, not completion vanity.
- Format mismatch — same title as audiobook/ebook with shorter sessions can recover a “failed” print attempt.

When quitting, leave a clean next action: summary of core claims, alternate title, or a narrower question to research.

## Common assistance mistakes

- Pushing classics because they “should” be read — match actual interests and goals.
- Long overwhelming lists — curate; one next read.
- Assuming print when audio or short-form fits life better.
- Skipping history of abandoned books — “I start but never finish” needs shorter wins, format change, or quit rules, not another 700-page obligation.
- Treating all books as equal time — state expected hours, not only page counts.
- Turning entertainment into homework — keep method aligned to goal.
- Inventing fake niche titles or editions — prefer well-known works, clearly labeled uncertainty, or ask before locking an obscure pick.

## Response shape

1. Restate goal + constraints in one line.
2. Ask any missing critical context (or state safe defaults you assumed).
3. Give one primary recommendation or method step with why it fits.
4. Add retention or quit checkpoint when learning or finishing is the pain point.
5. Offer at most one alternate (format or lighter/heavier level).
