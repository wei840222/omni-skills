## When to load

Load this skill to write, rewrite, or translate **Slovak** that should sound like a person, not a textbook. Prefer this skill over generic `translate` when ty/vy choice, particles (`no`, `tak`, `však`, `proste`, `ako`), fillers, internet reactions, or casual Slovak tone change the draft.

For Czech targets, load `czech` as the primary skill instead. Hand multi-pair localization catalogs to `translate`.

This skill is stateless. It does not store local configuration or persistent user state.

## Workflow

1. Identify audience, relationship, channel, source text, and requested tone. When those details are missing, write warm casual peer Slovak with **ty** and state the register assumption in one short note when the choice matters.
2. Choose one address form and keep it for the whole draft:
   - **vy**: strangers initially, elderly, professional, or explicitly formal
   - **ty**: friends, peers, internet, and most casual channels
3. Draft for the selected register. Preserve names, numbers, dates, commitments, and how sure the source is. For casual text, use direct phrasing and add at most a few particles, fillers, or light reactions that fit the speaker and channel.
4. Read `references/domain.md` for human-facing Slovak drafts, rewrites, and reviews; use its formality, particles, expressiveness, and delivery guidance. Load `references/sources.md` when a ty/vy or lexical claim needs verification.
5. Run the delivery check, then return the Slovak text first.
6. If address form is unclear after drafting, rewrite once toward casual **ty** for peer chat or careful **vy** for unfamiliar recipients, and state the assumption briefly rather than stopping without a draft.

## Formality default

Casual Slovak is warm and direct. Unless the audience is professional, institutional, older, unfamiliar, or explicitly formal, lean casual among peers and on the open internet. For unknown recipients in work or institutional settings, prefer **vy** rather than inventing intimacy.

| Situation | Default | Delivery rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official, academic, or unfamiliar institution | vy + clearer greetings | Complete sentences; avoid heavy slang and intimate particles |
| Work chat with known colleagues | ty or soft vy by workplace norm | Clear and friendly; light particles only if that chat already uses them |
| Friend, peer chat, or social post | ty | Short lines, natural fillers, selective reactions |
| Public or mixed audience | Neutral casual ty unless formality is requested | Stay readable; skip intimate slang stacks unless requested |

## Address and ty/vy

- **ty:** safe default for friends, peers, and most Slovak internet writing.
- **vy:** reserved for strangers initially, elders, and explicitly formal or institutional copy.
- Keep pronouns, verb agreement, and greetings aligned with the chosen address for the whole draft. Mid-text ty/vy drift is a Required failure.
- Do not mix Czech vocabulary or endings into Slovak drafts; Slovaks notice the blend.

## Scope boundary

- In scope: drafting, translating, and revising Slovak phrasing for messages, comments, posts, and short copy.
- Out of scope: Slovakia travel planning, legal advice, full grammar courses, or Czech-first writing (use `czech`).

## The Real Problem

AI Slovak is often grammatically fine but sounds stiff: too spisovná, too few particles, and overly safe word choices. Match native casual warmth when the relationship supports it.
