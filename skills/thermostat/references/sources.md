# Thermostat research sources

Use these sources when advising on setbacks, heat-pump behavior, freeze protection, or efficiency claims. Prefer the user's equipment manual and utility program guidance when they conflict with general rules of thumb.

## Efficiency and setbacks

- U.S. Department of Energy, [Thermostats and control systems](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/thermostats) — programmable/smart thermostat benefits, typical setback guidance, and why deep setbacks can be counterproductive on some heat pumps.
- ENERGY STAR, [Programmable thermostats](https://www.energystar.gov/products/heating_cooling/programmable_thermostats) — feature expectations for schedules, holds, and energy-saving operation when users actually enable setbacks.
- ENERGY STAR, [Smart thermostats](https://www.energystar.gov/products/heating_cooling/smart_thermostats) — connected-control capabilities (remote adjust, occupancy/geofencing patterns) and the need for correct configuration to realize savings.

## Freeze protection and humidity bounds

- Insurance Information Institute, [Prevent frozen pipes](https://www.iii.org/article/prevent-frozen-pipes) — keep heat on during cold weather; freezing risk rises when indoor temps drop too low for extended periods.
- EPA, [A brief guide to mold, moisture and your home](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) — elevated indoor humidity and moisture support mold growth; avoid prolonged high indoor temperatures with poor ventilation during long absences.

## Practical application in this skill

- Prefer 3–5°F setbacks for heat pumps when deep setbacks may force expensive auxiliary heat.
- Treat 55°F minimum / 85°F maximum as starting vacation bounds unless the user's equipment, climate, or insurer requires stricter limits.
- Route platform-specific automation details through `references/integration.md` and broader home automation through related skill `smart-home` when the request leaves thermostat scope.
