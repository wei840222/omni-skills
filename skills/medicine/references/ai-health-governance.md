# AI health governance reference

Use this reference for AI systems that inform, support, or affect health decisions. It is not a substitute for applicable law, clinical governance, regulatory review, or local institutional policy.

## Practical governance questions

1. **Purpose and users:** Define the intended health task, user role, decision boundary, and who remains accountable for the outcome.
2. **Evidence and limits:** Document validation population, performance limits, uncertainty, bias risks, and circumstances where the system should not be relied on.
3. **Human oversight:** Give users a clear route to question, override, escalate, and report harmful or unexpected output.
4. **Privacy and security:** Minimize health data, protect it throughout its lifecycle, and use access controls proportionate to sensitivity.
5. **Equity and monitoring:** Evaluate performance and burden across relevant populations; monitor drift, incidents, and unintended effects after deployment.
6. **Transparency:** Communicate the system's role, material limitations, data handling, and evidence in language appropriate to affected people.

## Source

- World Health Organization, *Ethics and governance of artificial intelligence for health* (2021): https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240029200
  - The report places ethics and human rights at the centre of AI-for-health design, deployment, and use, and describes six consensus principles for public benefit and accountable governance.
