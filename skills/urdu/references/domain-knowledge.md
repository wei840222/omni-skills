# Domain Knowledge — Urdu Register, Script, and Contact

## Status and script
- Urdu is an Indo-Aryan language written in a Perso-Arabic (Nastaliq) orthography and used widely in Pakistan and the South Asian diaspora.
- Formal writing and education materials typically expect Nastaliq; informal digital spaces frequently accept Roman Urdu.

Sources:
- Urdu language overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu
- Nastaliq / Perso-Arabic orthography notes — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nastaliq
- Urdu alphabet overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_alphabet

## Register: آپ / تم / تو
- `آپ` marks respect/distance; `تم` is the everyday peer default; `تو` is intimate and can sound rude if misapplied.
- AI Urdu often over-selects formal `آپ` and literary vocabulary, which fails the native-sounding test in chat.

- Urdu pronouns / politeness overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu#Pronouns

## Urdu–Hindi continuum
- Colloquial speech overlaps heavily with Hindi, but written Urdu prefers Nastaliq and a higher share of Persian/Arabic-origin lexis.
- Do not “translate into Hindi orthography” when the user asked for Urdu.

Sources:
- Hindustani language continuum — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_language
- Hindi–Urdu controversy / orthographic split overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi%E2%80%93Urdu_controversy

## Particles, fillers, and warmth
Casual Urdu relies on discourse particles (`نا`, `تو`, `ہی`, `بھی`) and fillers (`ویسیے`, `اصل میں`, `سنو`) plus expressive upgrades (`زبردست`, `کمال`) rather than safe literary adjectives alone.

Sources:
- Urdu grammar overview (particles / discourse habits as learner-facing evidence) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_grammar
- Colloquial Hindustani discourse practices overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_language

## Roman Urdu in digital spaces
Romanized Urdu is a practical orthography for keyboards that lack easy Nastaliq input. Accept common chat spellings when the channel is SMS/social.
