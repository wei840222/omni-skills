# Customer Retention and Revenue Strategies (Research)

## Customer Retention Basics

Customer retention refers to the ability of a company or product to retain its customers over some specified period. Successful retention efforts take the entire customer lifecycle into account, from first contact onwards. It requires more than meeting expectations; delivering a consistently high standard of customer service and focusing on customer value over short-term profit maximization is central.

Primary overview:
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_retention

### Revenue Impact

Engaged customers generate significantly more revenue than normal customers. Research popularized by John Fleming and Jim Asplund (Gallup) indicates that engaged customers generate about 1.7× more revenue than average customers, while having engaged employees and engaged customers together can return about 3.4× the norm.

Gallup culture / engagement framing:
- https://www.gallup.com/workplace/236366/right-culture-not-employee-satisfaction.aspx

### Measurement

Customer retention measurement distinguishes between behavioral intentions (for example surveys measuring repurchase intention) and actual customer behaviors (for example accounting measures of purchase volume and frequency). Customer satisfaction is a strong predictor of both. The relationship between different retention metrics varies by customer segment and industry.

### Drivers and Antecedents

- **Customer Satisfaction:** A direct driver of retention across industries.
- **Customer Delight:** Especially for hedonic goods/services, providing exceptionally strong service increases retention.
- **Customer Switching Costs:**
  - *Financial:* Fees to break contracts, lost rewards.
  - *Procedural:* Time, effort, uncertainty in switching.
  - *Relational:* Personal relationships with the brand and employees. Relational switching costs have the strongest association with customer repurchase intentions and behavior.
- **Customer Relationship Management:** A long-term perspective over a transactional one increases retention, though firms must also manage unprofitable customers appropriately.

### Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

CLV enables an organization to calculate the net present value of the profit realized on a customer over a given time period. Retention rate metrics are vital inputs to this formula.

## Service Excellence Standardization

Standards such as The International Standard for Service Excellence (TISSE 2012) focus on the 5 P's (Policy, Processes, People, Premises, Product/Service) and performance measurement to improve customer service practices, ultimately leading to higher customer loyalty and retention.
