# Biotechnology Technical Reference

Load this reference when the user asks about genome editing, advanced biocontainment, or the current evidence behind a technical claim. Treat this page as a conceptual guide, not an operational protocol.

## Genome editing: choose the evidence level

- **CRISPR-Cas9** commonly creates a targeted double-strand break; the cell's repair pathways determine the resulting edit.
- **Base editors** can make defined base conversions without intentionally creating a double-strand break, but target-window, bystander-edit, and off-target risks still require system-specific validation.
- **Prime editors** combine a Cas9 nickase with a reverse transcriptase and a prime-editing guide RNA. They can support specified substitutions and small insertions or deletions without a donor DNA template, but efficiency and edit purity vary by cell type, locus, delivery method, and assay.

For a decision or comparison, ask for the organism or cell type, desired edit, delivery constraints, validation assay, and whether the work is exploratory or translational. Avoid ranking methods as universally superior.

## Biocontainment

Biocontainment combines risk assessment, engineering controls, work practices, and training. Genetic safeguards such as auxotrophy or kill switches can supplement containment, but they do not replace institutional biosafety review, appropriate facilities, or documented procedures.

When a user asks about an engineered organism, identify the host, inserted function, environment, and foreseeable exposure routes. Direct high-risk or dual-use work to the relevant institutional biosafety process.
