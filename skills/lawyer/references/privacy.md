# Privacy and data protection

Load for GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, DPAs, transfers, breach clocks, or privacy policies.

## Role first

Decide **controller vs processor** (or business vs service provider under CCPA/CPRA) before drafting. The same company can be both on different processing activities.

## GDPR-oriented sequence

1. Role and processing purpose
2. Lawful basis (Art. 6) and special-category rules if any (Art. 9)
3. Processor terms (Art. 28-style DPA): instructions, confidentiality, subprocessors, security, deletion/return, audit
4. International transfer mechanism when data leaves the adequate jurisdiction
5. Breach notification clock

### Breach notification (GDPR Art. 33)

Personal-data breaches must be notified to the competent supervisory authority **without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after becoming aware**, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to persons. Notification failure is a separate infringement from the breach itself. Start the clock, preserve logs, involve counsel and DPO in parallel. Confirm current EDPB/authority guidance for the incident type (`references/sources.md`).

## CCPA/CPRA (directional)

Consumer rights (know, delete, correct, opt-out of sale/share) and service-provider contracts differ from GDPR. Do not paste GDPR DPA language unchanged into a California-only deal without checking role and definitions.

## Children, health, biometrics

Heightened regimes (COPPA, HIPAA, state biometric laws). Treat as Red Flags when facts match — statutory clocks and consent rules are unforgiving.

## Drafting hygiene

- Answer security questionnaires with **what is true today**, plus a dated roadmap — not aspirations that become false reps
- Sub-processor lists need notice and objection mechanics when promised
- Do not copy employee/customer raw data into skill memory; store pointers and controllers only
