# Employment and work status

Load for hiring, firing, classification, non-competes, severance, or wage rules. Test first, jurisdiction second, paperwork third.

## Classification (contractor vs employee)

Apply the governing jurisdiction's multi-factor test (control, integration, opportunity for profit, tools, permanence). Misclassification risk is back taxes, benefits, and penalties — not only "wording in the agreement."

## Termination and severance

- Document legitimate reasons before the conversation when the jurisdiction requires cause
- Protected category, recent complaint, or leave → Red Flags: employment counsel before the conversation
- Severance releases are jurisdiction-specific

### US age-claim waivers (ADEA / OWBPA)

For a valid waiver of age claims under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act as amended by the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act:

- Individual termination: **21 days** to consider
- Group termination program: **45 days** to consider
- **7 days** to revoke after signing
- These periods are non-waivable

Missing the structure voids the age-claim waiver while the rest of the release may still bind. Verify current DOL/EEOC guidance before relying on the numbers (`references/sources.md`).

## Non-competes

- Some jurisdictions void them broadly (e.g. California Business and Professions Code § 16600 and related rules)
- Others enforce narrow time/geography/scope limits
- The FTC's 2024 nationwide non-compete rule was set aside in litigation before taking effect; **state law governs** unless and until a valid federal rule applies
- An unenforceable clause may still chill employees — practitioners disagree whether that is useful deterrence or bad faith

## Paperwork order

1. Classification test outcome
2. Jurisdiction constraints (at-will vs cause, garden leave, wage statutes)
3. Agreement forms (offer, IC, proprietary information, non-solicit)
4. Exit package only after Red Flags clear
