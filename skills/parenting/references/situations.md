# Situations — screens, school, hard topics, burnout, referral

## Screen time

- Blanket limits ignore context — educational vs passive, solo vs co-viewing
- “No screens” as moral judgment is rarely practical; ask what actually worries them (content, duration, displacement of sleep/play/family time)
- Quality and co-engagement beat raw minutes when the concern is passive consumption
- Guilt is not a strategy — offer one concrete family media rule the household can keep

## School and learning

- Ask the specific concern before generic advice — grades, social friction, motivation, homework battles differ
- Learning differences are common — read struggle as a need for different support, not lack of effort
- Homework battles: check whether the fight is about the work or about control/autonomy
- Teacher conflict: gather the full picture before taking sides
- Not every child thrives in one traditional path — acknowledge alternatives without selling a brand

## Tricky topics

- Age-appropriate honesty beats comfortable lies — adjust detail level, keep truthfulness
- Follow the child’s lead on depth — answer what was asked, then check if they want more
- “I am unsure, let’s find out together” is a valid answer
- Normalize hard topics (death, bodies, emotions) in a calm, matter-of-fact tone
- Manage adult discomfort privately — do not unload it onto the child

## Self-care reality

- “Take time for yourself” without naming barriers is empty — ask what is actually possible this week
- Parental burnout is real — not weakness or failure
- Good-enough parenting is the goal; perfection is not
- Seeking support is strength — normalize help from partner, family, community, professionals
- Parent wellbeing affects child wellbeing — self-care is maintenance, not selfishness

## When to refer out

| Signal | Route |
|--------|-------|
| Persistent behavioral concerns | child psychologist / behavioral specialist |
| Developmental questions or delays | pediatrician, developmental specialist |
| Mental health (parent or child) | licensed therapist |
| Safety, abuse, imminent harm | appropriate authorities / local emergency channels |
| Medical questions | clinicians — this skill does not diagnose or prescribe |

Stay in-skill only for everyday coaching once red flags are absent or already being handled by professionals.
