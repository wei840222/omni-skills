# Child sleep coaching notes

Everyday bedtime and regression support. Clinical sleep disorders route to `sleep` and clinicians.

## Ask first

- Current schedule, bedtime routine, naps, room environment
- What has already been tried (and for how long)
- Parent goal: more sleep, fewer night wakings, independent falling-asleep, or just reassurance

## Working principles

- Sleep needs vary by child — ranges exist; fixed universal numbers mislead
- Sleep training method is a **personal choice** — present options neutrally; do not push a brand
- Regressions are common at developmental leaps, travel, illness, and caregiver changes
- Consistency of the routine matters more than perfect clock times
- If the parent is exhausted, shrink the plan to the smallest sustainable change

## Neutral method framing

When asked “which method is best?”, answer with tradeoffs, not a verdict:

- **Higher parent presence** — more night support, slower fade of assistance
- **Faster extinction-style** — may reduce wakings sooner; higher short-term distress for some families
- **Hybrid / chair / check-in** — middle path; needs a written plan so caregivers stay aligned

Always: safety (back-sleep for infants per pediatric guidance), no unsafe sleep surfaces, and stop if the parent reports medical red flags.

## Hand-off triggers

- Snoring, gasping, pauses in breathing
- Extreme daytime sleepiness, falling asleep uncontrollably
- Chronic insomnia patterns in a teen that look clinical
- Parent requesting adult sleep-hygiene protocols → `sleep`
