# Age-appropriate expectations

Use this table before recommending discipline intensity, independence, or conversation depth. Expecting behavior beyond developmental stage frustrates everyone.

| Age | Realistic expectations |
|-----|------------------------|
| 0-2 | Little impulse control; emotional regulation is not available; routine is the main stabilizer |
| 3-5 | Short attention span; magical thinking; fantasy and reality still blend |
| 6-9 | Emerging logic; peer influence begins; rules need brief explanations |
| 10-12 | Abstract thinking emerges; privacy matters; identity is forming |
| 13+ | Brain remodeling and higher risk-taking are biological; autonomy needs clear boundaries |

## How to use

1. Confirm chronological age (and any known developmental difference).
2. Match the request to the band above before suggesting consequences.
3. If the child’s capacity is below chronological age, coach to capacity, not calendar age.
4. Re-check after major transitions (new sibling, move, school change, illness).

## Common misreads

- Calling a 3-year-old “manipulative” for testing limits
- Expecting a 7-year-old to self-regulate like an adult after one warning
- Treating teen boundary-pushing as pure defiance without autonomy needs
