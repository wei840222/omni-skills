# Taste anti-patterns

## Unaware confidence

Weak taste often arrives with ready explanations:

- “Minimalism feels lazy — where is the personality?”
- “I like things that make a statement”
- “If it catches the eye immediately, it works”
- “More options mean better value”

Treat confident rationales as hypotheses to test, not proof of quality.

## “More is better” family

| Manifestation | Reality |
|---|---|
| Layer three textures instead of one | Confusion ≠ richness |
| Feature-rich layouts praised as valuable | Clutter ≠ value |
| Negative space called unfinished | Restraint ≠ laziness |
| “Needs more visual interest” | Busy ≠ interesting |

Core error: equating quantity with quality.

## “Impressive = good” family

| Manifestation | Reality |
|---|---|
| Gold/chrome/metallic everywhere | Luxury signals ≠ taste |
| “Looks premium” as sole metric | Expensive cues ≠ craft |
| Fake marble / fake leather textures | Imitation ≠ sophistication |
| “I’ve never seen anything like it” | Novelty ≠ quality |

Core error: conflating status signals with aesthetic excellence.

## Safe-average problem

Default taste gravitates to the middle:

- Not too minimal, not too busy
- Not too bold, not too subtle
- Not too trendy, not too classic

Result: committee design — defensible, forgettable, mediocre.

Strong taste often sits at an extreme executed with conviction:

- Radically minimal
- Radically specific
- Radically restrained (one thing done well)

## Recognition failures

| Remark | Often missing |
|---|---|
| “Anyone could make that” | Restraint and judgment cost |
| “It’s just a white rectangle” | Proportion, weight, breath, content relation |
| “There’s nothing there” | Negative space mistaken for emptiness |

## Trend trap

Trends supply belonging, easy decisions, and temporary relevance.

Taste supplies enduring quality recognition, independent judgment, and resistance to fashion churn.

Full-current-fashion outfits often date faster than simple, well-fit basics.

## Effort visibility

Weak taste makes effort obvious: mood-board collage energy, anxious signaling, performing cleverness.

Strong taste makes choices feel inevitable — as if no other option would fit.

## Rationalization trap

Worst loop: decide first, invent justifications second.

When arguments only defend a first impulse, evaluation has stopped. Keep the model open to being wrong; write corrections into `<state_root>/` instead of protecting ego.
