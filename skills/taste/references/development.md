# Developing taste — training aesthetic judgment

## Three requirements

1. **Volume with attention** — Interrogate work; do not only consume it
2. **Making imperfect work** — The gap between taste and ability is the training signal
3. **Studying the edges** — Near-misses teach more than flawless masterpieces

## Volume with attention

For each piece, ask:

- Why does this work?
- What changes if the bottom third is covered?
- Where does the eye go first, second, third?
- What would improve it?
- What would ruin it?

Repeated interrogation turns composition parsing into fluent reading.

## Making imperfect work

Taste usually outruns ability for a long time. That discomfort is useful: it forces vocabulary for what is sensed but not yet executable. Produce work, feel the gap, name the gap.

## Studying the edges

Compare near-identical pairs:

- Why does A land and B fail when they share ~90% of traits?
- What is the remaining 10%?
- Build words for that difference

## Calibration process

1. Expose to range (exemplary and poor, not only mediocre)
2. Compare deliberately side by side
3. Label verbally — words anchor perception
4. Check judgments against trusted reviewers or outcomes
5. Apply the same standards to self-made work

Passive consumption is insufficient.

## Expert convergence

Experts often agree more than novices because they weight similar features, share canonical exemplars, and share distinction vocabulary. When judgments diverge wildly from domain experts, inspect which features are being weighted.

Philosophical framing (stable-domain): judgments of taste combine a subjective response with a claim that others ought to share the verdict under appropriate conditions; aesthetic properties depend on nonaesthetic features even when no simple laws of taste exist (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Aesthetic Judgment; see `sources.md`).

## Preference versus quality

Train the split:

- “I dislike this, but it is excellent”
- “I enjoy this, but it is mediocre”

For each reaction, ask whether the response tracks craft structure or personal familiarity/affect.

## Environmental validity

Domain noise differs:

| Domain | Signal character |
|---|---|
| Product/UI design | Relatively stable craft principles |
| Writing | Style-dependent; craft still detectable |
| Fashion | Faster trend cycles; context-heavy |
| Music | Genre-specific; needs domain exposure |

Match confidence to domain stability. Stay humble in noisier domains until user-specific patterns exist in `<state_root>/`.

## Long game

1. Consume widely with attention
2. Feel taste–ability gaps
3. Make things anyway
4. Study what strong practitioners value
5. Build distinction vocabulary
6. Check judgments against outcomes
7. Repeat

No shortcut replaces accumulated exposure with engaged attention.
