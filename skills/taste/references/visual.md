# Visual taste — design judgment

## Fast impression, slow justification

Users form design-related attitudes extremely quickly (on the order of 50 ms in controlled studies). Aesthetic-usability research also shows that attractive interfaces are often perceived as easier to use. Practical implication: hierarchy, calm scanning paths, and restraint decide the first verdict; ornament rarely rescues a weak structure.

Sources: NN/g first-impressions and aesthetic-usability articles (see `sources.md`).

## Physical read

After enough calibrated exposure, treat bodily cues as trained pattern recognition:

- Where does the eye land first, second, third?
- Does attention rest or thrash?
- Is there one hierarchy or a fight?

## Correct vs good

**Correct** follows checklists: centered blocks, matching palette, grid compliance, accessible contrast floors.

**Good** knows when to break rules: intentional asymmetry, tension that resolves, whitespace that breathes, rhythm with syncopation.

Most “correct” work feels dead because it optimizes for meeting defensibility. Good work includes choices justified mainly by the result.

Accessibility contrast floors remain non-negotiable craft constraints (WCAG 2.1 Understanding 1.4.3: 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text). Taste chooses how to meet them with conviction, not whether to ignore them.

## Layout tests

1. Cover half the composition — does the remainder still hold?
2. Squint until it blurs — is hierarchy still obvious?
3. Imagine 50% larger and 50% smaller — which direction improves it?
4. Remove one element — essential loss or noise reduction?

## AI-generated design failure modes

| Failure | Why it reads wrong |
|---|---|
| Statistical average | Mean of training mass, not a confident specific choice |
| No tension | Everything resolves too smoothly |
| Hedging everywhere | Satisfies all constraints, masters none |
| Generic “good” | Pattern-matched to safe, not distinctive |

AI defaults to the committee version. Taste requires individual conviction plus user calibration.

## Common visual taste failures

| Pattern | Problem |
|---|---|
| Every corner filled | Fear of emptiness |
| Competing focal points | No hierarchy decision |
| Decoration that fail to serve | Space felt “empty” |
| Too-perfect symmetry | Templated, not designed |
| Gradient overuse | Compensating for weak composition |
| Drop shadow on everything | Un-earned depth |

## Typography taste

- One typeface is usually enough
- Two typefaces maximum, with clear role split
- Three typefaces is a smell
- Script + modern + serif stacks usually collapse

Letter-spacing sins: default tracking on large display text; tight tracking on body; auto-everything without optical checks.

## Color taste

- More colors ≠ more interesting
- Restraint first; accent as punctuation
- If hierarchy needs more than three colors, hierarchy is unclear
- “Pops of color” often confess structural weakness

For full palette engineering, token pipelines, or CVD-safe systems, hand off to `design` / `color` after the taste call is made.
