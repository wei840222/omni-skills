## Aesthetic judgment (stable-domain theory)

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Aesthetic Judgment — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aesthetic-judgment/
  - Judgments of taste couple a subjective pleasure/displeasure response with a normative claim of broader validity
  - Aesthetic properties depend on nonaesthetic properties; interesting nonaesthetic→aesthetic laws are widely treated as unavailable (anomalousness)
  - Useful framing for separating personal preference from craft evaluation without pretending taste is pure relativism

## Visual first impressions and perceived usability

- Nielsen Norman Group — The Aesthetic-Usability Effect — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/
  - Attractive designs are often perceived as easier to use; aesthetics influence subjective usability
- Nielsen Norman Group — The First Impression of a Design Takes Only 50 Milliseconds — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/first-impressions-design/
  - Extremely fast visual attitude formation; hierarchy and clarity matter before decoration

## Prose scanning and plain language

- Nielsen Norman Group — How Users Read on the Web — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/
  - Scanning, subheads, short paragraphs, one idea per paragraph
- Digital.gov plain language guide — https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/
- Digital.gov plain language principles — https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles/
  - Audience focus, short sections, active voice, familiar terms

## Accessibility contrast floors (craft constraint)

- W3C WAI — Understanding Success Criterion 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) — https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html
  - Normal text contrast minimum 4.5:1; large text 3:1 under WCAG 2.x AA framing used here as the default craft floor
