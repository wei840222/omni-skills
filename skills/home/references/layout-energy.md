# Layout, energy, organization, plants, budget

## Furniture and layout

- Measure doorways, stair turns, and elevator cabs before buying large pieces.
- Keep walking paths about 90 cm / 36 in where people move regularly.
- Rugs: large enough for all furniture legs or at least the front legs —
  floating postage-stamp rugs make rooms feel unfinished.
- Lighting layers: ambient + task + accent. A single overhead fixture flattens
  the room.

## Energy and climate

- Thermal curtains and draft stoppers are low-cost comfort levers.
- Prefer LED replacements when upgrading from inefficient bulbs; verify base
  and lumen needs before bulk buys.
- Ceiling fan direction: counterclockwise in summer for cooling breeze;
  clockwise on low in winter to push warm air down without a draft.
- Deep HVAC equipment choices and smart thermostats hand off to `smart-home`
  or `house` as appropriate.

## Organization

- Pick storage that is easier than living with the mess.
- Use vertical space for rarely used items; keep daily items at reachable height.
- One-in, one-out helps chronic clutter without complex systems.
- Prefer simple category labels over elaborate taxonomies that rot.

## Plants and nature

- Ask about real light (hours, direction, obstruction), not “bright room.”
- Overwatering kills more houseplants than underwatering; when unsure, wait.
- Starter plants: pothos, snake plant, ZZ plant. Reserve fiddle-leaf fig and
  similar drama plants for users with stable light and care habits.
- Ongoing care logs and diagnosis → `plants`. Outdoor beds → `garden`.

## Budget sequencing

- Paint often gives the highest visual ROI before replacing furniture.
- Fix substrate and moisture problems before new finishes.
- Mattress and primary seating affect daily life more than decorative objects.
- For large spends, compare current local comps via `buy` rather than memorized
  sale calendars alone.
