# Sources

Use these pages to check a register label, a te/ön mapping, or a particle gloss. They describe usage; they are not a license to invent slang or a dialect the user did not ask for.

## Register and formality

- **Wikipedia — Hungarian language** — overview of Hungarian as a Uralic, agglutinative language with flexible word order and rich morphology relevant to register choices. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language
- **Wikipedia — T-V distinction** — documents second-person formality contrasts such as Hungarian *te* vs *ön* / *maga* used in polite address. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%E2%80%93V_distinction
- **Wiktionary — ön** — Hungarian second-person polite pronoun *ön* (formal you). https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%C3%B6n#Hungarian
- **Wiktionary — te** — Hungarian second-person singular informal pronoun *te*. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/te#Hungarian
- **Wiktionary — maga** — additional polite second-person form *maga* used in formal address. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/maga#Hungarian

## Particles, morphology, word order

- **Wikipedia — Hungarian grammar** — agglutination, definite/indefinite conjugation contrasts, and topic-prominent word-order tendencies that affect natural delivery. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_grammar
- **Wiktionary — hát** — discourse particle / filler glosses used in casual Hungarian. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/h%C3%A1t#Hungarian
- **Wiktionary — ugye** — tag-like particle often glossed toward confirmation ("right?"). https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ugye#Hungarian
- **Wiktionary — csak** — common softener / "just" particle in conversational Hungarian. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/csak#Hungarian
