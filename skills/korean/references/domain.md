# Korean language rules

Use this guide for human-facing Korean drafts. It describes common register and style patterns; it does not license inventing a dialect, identity, or intimacy the user did not request.

## The real problem

Model Korean is often grammatical and still wrong at the register: one notch too formal, particle-heavy, and structured like a textbook essay. Natural writing restores contractions, appropriate endings, and only the fillers or slang that fit the channel.

## Speech levels

- 합쇼체 (-습니다 / -ㅂ니다): very formal; news, presentations, ceremonial copy
- 해요체 (-아/어요): polite default; strangers, mixed audiences, ordinary work chat
- 해체 / 반말 (-아/어): casual; friends, peers, explicit casual requests
- Online and texting among peers often use 반말 or light 해요체
- Keep one level for the whole draft; do not mix endings awkwardly

## Contractions

Casual Korean contracts heavily when the relationship supports it:

- 하는 것 → 하는 거
- 무엇 → 뭐
- 그것 → 그거
- 나는 → 난
- 너는 → 넌
- 것이 → 게
- 아니야 → 아냐

Prefer full forms in formal or unfamiliar-recipient writing.

## Sentence endings and reactions

These add nuance in casual channels; they are optional, not a quota:

- ㅋㅋㅋ: laughter (more ㅋ usually reads funnier)
- ㅎㅎ: softer laugh
- ㅠㅠ / ㅜㅜ: sadness or frustration
- ~: softening (밥 먹었어~)
- ㄱㄱ: 고고 / let's go
- ㅇㅇ: 응응 / yeah

Natural reactions when the tone fits: 진짜?, 헐, 대박, 미쳤어?, 뭐야?, 아 ㅋㅋ

## Particles

In casual speech, drop particles when clarity survives:

- 을/를 often dropped
- 이/가 often dropped
- 은/는 sometimes dropped

Keep particles when needed for clarity, emphasis, or formal register.

## Fillers and flow

Optional conversational choices:

- 음, 어, 그
- 아니 (sentence starter)
- 근데, 그래서, 그러니까
- 막, 좀, 되게
- 진짜, 완전, 레알

Keep factual messages direct; insert a filler only where it helps the speaker's flow.

## Expressiveness

Match the source sentiment and intensity rather than replacing every ordinary word:

- 좋다 → 대박, 미쳤다, 쩐다 when hyperbole is intended
- 나쁘다 → 별로, 최악, 구리다 when stronger criticism is intended
- 많이 → 완전; reserve 개 / 존나 for clearly crude or peer-slang contexts
- 예쁘다 → 이쁘다, 존예 only when the voice already supports that slang

## Internet slang boundaries

Modern casual Korean may use:

- ㅋㅋㅋ, ㄹㅇ (레알/리얼)
- 개 as intensifier: 개웃김, 개맛있어
- 존나 as crude intensifier
- 헐, 대박, 미쳤다
- 인정, 공감
- ㄴㄴ (노노), ㅇㅋ (오케이)

Do not force slang into professional, institutional, or unfamiliar-recipient copy. Crude intensifiers need an explicit casual/peer frame.

## Common expressions

Choose expressions that fit the speech act:

- 알겠어 / 알았어, ㅇㅇ
- 뭐해?, 밥 먹었어?
- 그치?, 맞아맞아
- 별로야, 그냥 그래
- 대박, 헐, 미쳤다

## Aegyo / cute writing

Common in some casual contexts, never required:

- 네 → 넹, 넵
- 응 → 웅
- 안녕 → 안뇽
- Adding ㅎ: 좋아용, 고마워용

Use only when the speaker, channel, or sample already leans that way.

## The "Native Test"

Before sending: would a Korean reader mark this as stiff, over-complete, or textbook-formal for the chosen audience? If yes, adjust speech level, contractions, and filler density—without inventing intimacy or slang the relationship does not support.
