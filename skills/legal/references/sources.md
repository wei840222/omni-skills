# Source starting points

These are verification entry points, not a complete world library. Always record jurisdiction, instrument identifier, and retrieval date for material claims.

## United States federal

- **Statutes (U.S. Code):** Office of the Law Revision Counsel — https://uscode.house.gov/
- **Regulations:** eCFR — https://www.ecfr.gov/
- **Federal court rules:** U.S. Courts Rules & Policies — https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/current-rules-practice-procedure
- **Supreme Court materials:** https://www.supremecourt.gov/

## United States consumer / commercial orientation (examples)

- **California statutes portal (for CA hypotheticals):** California Legislative Information — https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/
- **UCC text and enactment status (commercial sales baseline):** Uniform Law Commission UCC page — https://www.uniformlaws.org/acts/ucc
- **FTC consumer protection materials:** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance

## General verification workflow

1. Identify forum, issue date, and whether the question is civil, criminal, administrative, or contractual.
2. Locate the controlling primary text from an official publisher when available.
3. Check amendments, effective dates, and local implementing rules before stating a current requirement.
4. Separate source text from analysis and confidence labels.
5. For non-U.S. matters, use that jurisdiction's official legislature, gazette, court, or regulator site; do not transplant U.S. doctrines by name alone.

## Citation hygiene

- Prefer primary authority over blogs and secondary explainers.
- If only secondary sources are available, label them and lower confidence.
- Never invent case names, statute numbers, or pin cites.
- When consequences are material, tell the user to verify currentness in an authoritative system or with local counsel.
