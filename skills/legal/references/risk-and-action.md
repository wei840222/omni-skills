# Risk scoring and action

## Position strength

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Strong** | Elements largely supported by available facts; main risks are execution/cost. |
| **Moderate** | At least one material element or defense is contested or under-documented. |
| **Weak** | A required element is missing, a clear bar applies, or proof is mostly speculative. |

## Risk matrix inputs

| Factor | Lower risk signal | Higher risk signal |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Documentation | Written, signed, dated | Verbal only / lost records |
| Timeline | Comfortably inside limits | Near or past deadlines |
| Other party | Unrepresented, cooperative | Counsel engaged, hostile |
| Amount / stakes | Small claims / reversible | Significant sum or status loss |
| Complexity | Single issue, clear facts | Multi-party, disputed facts |
| Forum friction | Local, familiar procedure | Arbitration, foreign forum, fee-shifting |

## Action design

Recommend **one** primary next action that is:

1. reversible where possible
2. evidence-preserving
3. deadline-aware
4. matched to the user's capacity (self-help vs counsel)

Good first actions often include: preserve originals, write a dated timeline, request missing documents in writing, check contractual notice windows, calendar known hearing dates, or consult local legal aid / counsel.

## Escalation triggers

Point to a licensed attorney promptly when any of these appear:

- criminal exposure or restraining-order risk
- child custody / domestic violence
- immigration consequences
- imminent eviction or utility shutoff
- signing away rights, releases, or high-value settlements
- served pleadings with a response deadline
- complex IP assignment, equity, or entity-formation stakes
- the user needs a filing signed or a negotiation conducted as counsel

## Cost realism

Include non-legal costs when material: time, relationship damage, collection difficulty, and fee-shifting risk. A theoretically viable claim can still be a poor practical path.
