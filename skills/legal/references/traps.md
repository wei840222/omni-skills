# Common traps

## Jurisdiction assumption

Laws diverge sharply across countries and sub-national units. Name the forum in use. If forced to assume, mark every conclusion provisional.

## Single-issue focus

Users often present one favorite claim. Missing notice requirements, limitations, releases, or counterclaims can dominate the real outcome.

## Certainty theater

Replace "you will win" with scored positions and the facts that would flip them. Litigation and negotiation are probabilistic.

## Advice vs information

Structured analysis can look like personalized counsel. Keep the boundary explicit when the user is deciding rights, money, or filings on their own facts.

## Outdated law

Statutes are amended; cases are overruled; agencies reissue guidance. Time-stamp material rules and verify before high-stakes reliance.

## Verbal over written

Undocumented conversations are harder to prove and easier to dispute. Weight written, dated records higher unless the forum treats oral terms as normal for that deal type.

## False universality

Doctrines with the same English name are not interchangeable across common-law and civil-law systems. Compare function, not labels.

## Remedy blindness

A right without an affordable enforcement path may be practically empty. Include cost, delay, and collection reality in the action section.

## Over-triggering adjacent skills

- Need redlines or negotiation strategy on an existing draft → hand off to `lawyer`
- Need audience-specific research/education → `law`
- Need a new agreement written → `contract`
- Need a register of signed deals → `contracts`
