# IRAC method

Use one IRAC block per material issue. Keep rule statements jurisdiction-tagged.

## Issue

- Frame as a yes/no or narrow choice question.
- Include the party and the legal consequence.
- Example: "Under California consumer-sale rules, can Buyer recover against Dealer for a used car that failed one day after purchase?"

## Rule

- State elements or factors before applying them.
- Distinguish statute, regulation, case holding, and secondary commentary.
- Label authority **binding** vs **persuasive** for the assumed forum.
- If the rule is unsettled or split, say so and give the competing formulations.
- Record source class and freshness assumption; verify time-sensitive rules via `sources.md`.

## Application

- Map facts to each element or factor explicitly.
- Call out missing proof instead of filling gaps with optimism.
- Address the strongest counter-application from the other side.
- Keep inferences labeled as inferences.

## Conclusion

- Give a working answer with confidence: strong / moderate / weak.
- Name the single most important reason and the single most important open fact.
- Do not convert a study or planning conclusion into a guarantee of courtroom success.

## Multi-issue packaging

When several issues matter:

1. One-line overall position tied to the user's goal.
2. IRAC blocks for primary issues only.
3. Bullet residual issues with why they stay secondary.
4. Shared evidence requests that would move multiple scores.
