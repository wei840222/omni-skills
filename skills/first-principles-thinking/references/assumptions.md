# Assumption traps — first principles

Load this file when inventorying hidden assumptions or stress-testing constraint claims.

## Seven hidden assumption classes

### 1. Historical

**Pattern:** “We have always done it this way.”

Examples of the *pattern* (not timeless facts): QWERTY lock-in narratives, factory-era schedule defaults, calendar-tied review rituals.

Questions:

- When did this start, and why?
- Does the original reason still apply?
- If starting fresh today, would we still choose it?

### 2. Authority

**Pattern:** “Experts / leaders say X.”

Questions:

- What evidence supports the claim?
- What would falsify it?
- Where have authorities been wrong on similar claims?

### 3. Analogical

**Pattern:** “It is just like X, so Y applies.”

Questions:

- What are the underlying mechanics of each side?
- Where does the analogy break?
- Which differences actually matter for this decision?

### 4. Social

**Pattern:** “Everyone does it” / “no one does it.”

Questions:

- Is this optimal or merely common?
- Who benefits from the norm staying fixed?
- What happens if we ignore it under controlled risk?

### 5. Resource

**Pattern:** “We cannot afford it” / “no capacity.”

Questions:

- Is this a hard limit or a prioritization choice?
- What changes if the scarce resource appears tomorrow?
- What constraint sits behind the stated resource limit?

### 6. Technical

**Pattern:** “The technology cannot do X.”

Questions:

- Is this a physics limit or an engineering limit at current maturity?
- What is the theoretical maximum?
- What is improving, and at what observed rate (with sources)?

### 7. Market

**Pattern:** “Customers want / do not want X.”

Questions:

- Is this stated preference or revealed behavior?
- Has the underlying need been tested in another form?
- Are we solving the right job-to-be-done?

## Detection phrases

| Phrase | Likely hidden move |
| --- | --- |
| “Obviously…” | Something is not being questioned |
| “Everyone knows…” | Consensus treated as truth |
| “It is impossible to…” | Difficulty confused with impossibility |
| “The problem is…” | Problem framing may be wrong |
| “We tried that and…” | Conditions may have changed |
| “The data shows…” | Interpretation may be overfitted |
| “Best practices say…” | Best for whom, when, and under which constraints? |

## Assumption stress test

1. **State it explicitly** as a clear claim
2. **Find the origin** of the belief
3. **Seek disconfirmation** — what would prove it wrong?
4. **Imagine the opposite** — what if the reverse were true?
5. **Test minimally** — cheapest validation that changes the decision

## Alien test

Explain each constraint to a listener with no human institutional context:

- “That is how it is done” fails
- “Experts believe” fails without evidence
- “It is too hard” fails without a physics or legal proof

If the justification collapses under that test, treat the claim as an assumption until verified.

## Recovery when an assumption is found

1. Keep it temporarily — assumptions often had a past reason
2. Understand the history
3. Re-check current validity
4. Price the cost of challenging it
5. Test in a small, reversible slice first

Most assumptions were once workable solutions. Some still are. First principles decides which.
