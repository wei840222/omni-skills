# Safety & Scam Prevention

## Urgent Escalation Signals

- "I'll send my driver to pick up" + overpayment
- Wants to pay outside the platform
- Sends check/money order for MORE than asking
- Requests for off-platform contact or unnecessary personal details before an offer
- Excessive urgency + unusual payment method
- "Is this still available?" followed by copy-paste scam message
- QR code payment requests (fake payment confirmation)
- "I'll buy it sight unseen at full price" on high-value items
- Requests your email to "send payment" — phishing for account credentials

When one or more signals appear, end the transaction path, preserve the message, verify any transaction in the official app or website, and report or block through the platform when appropriate. A single ordinary question is not conclusive; base the response on the payment and delivery behavior as a whole.

A low offer alone is a negotiation event, not proof of fraud. Treat the combination of third-party pickup, overpayment, off-platform payment, or verification-code requests as the escalation signal.

Describe a suspicious payment as unverified rather than predicting its outcome. The seller can establish the next safe action—end the path, preserve evidence, and verify in the official service—without claiming that a payment will fail, reverse, use stolen credentials, or that a particular amount proves fraud. Use this form: "The payment is unverified. End this transaction path, preserve the message, and check only the official service."

## Common Scam Patterns

- **Fake payment confirmation emails:** A spoofed email may claim that PayPal, eBay, or another platform confirmed payment. Verify the transaction by opening the platform directly from a trusted bookmark or app.
- **Overpayment + refund request:** Buyer sends a payment claim for more than asking, then asks for the difference back. Treat the claim as unverified until the transaction appears in the official account and the payment is settled under the applicable terms.
- **Shipping label scam:** "I've prepaid shipping, just send the item." Verify who controls the shipping label, address, tracking, and protection requirements in the official payment or platform flow before releasing the item.
- **Google Voice / verification-code scam:** A buyer asks for a code to "verify" the seller. Keep every verification code private. The FTC explains that a scammer can use a seller's number and code to create a Google Voice number that conceals the scammer's identity.
- **Payment-upgrade trick:** A buyer claims that an external email, agent, or fee is required to receive payment. Verify the transaction only in the official service and decline any unrelated payment or account-change request.

## Payment Safety

| Method | Risk | Use when |
|--------|------|----------|
| **Cash (in person)** | Counterfeit and personal-safety risk | Public local pickup after checking notes and item |
| **Platform payment** | Terms and eligibility vary | Transactions that stay in the platform's current flow |
| **PayPal Goods & Services** | Protection is conditional | Remote sale when the transaction is eligible and the seller can meet its terms |
| **Venmo/Zelle** | Limited recourse and impersonation risk | Trusted counterparties only, after reviewing current terms |
| **Wire transfer, gift cards, crypto** | High irreversibility or weak consumer recourse | Use only under a separately verified, appropriate commercial process; they are not the default for an ordinary peer sale |

**PayPal eligibility:** PayPal's US [Seller Protection Program](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/seller-protection) can require an eligible transaction, shipment to the Transaction Details address, and valid proof of shipment or delivery. Confirm the current transaction details and terms before shipping; eligibility is not guaranteed.

## Meeting Strangers

**Safe locations:**
- Police station parking lots (many have designated exchange zones)
- Bank lobbies (cameras, security)
- Busy coffee shops during day
- Inside malls

For first-time buyers, use a public, staffed, daylight location and bring another person or share the plan with a trusted contact.

**Bring:**
- A friend or let someone know where you are
- Your phone, charged
- Only the item (leave valuables in car)

## Furniture / Large Items

If they must come to your home:
- Have someone else present
- Meet outside first (driveway/curb)
- Keep them in the entry area
- Arrange during daylight

## "Buyer Says It's Broken" After Pickup

**Prevention:**
- Document item working before meetup (video)
- Have them test it before paying
- Get confirmation text: "Item received as described"

**If disputed:**
- Preserve the listing, messages, photos, serial numbers, and test evidence.
- Follow the platform dispute process for platform sales.
- For a material dispute, check the applicable local consumer and contract rules before deciding a refund or other remedy.
- Reconcile any return through a documented, traceable process that accounts for the item's condition and delivery evidence.

## Lowballers at Meetup

They show up with less cash than agreed:
> "That's not what we agreed. It's $X or no deal."

End the meetup if the agreed terms are not met. This protects the seller from a common pressure tactic.

## Shipping Safely

- Use tracking and retain the packing, label, and delivery evidence required by the payment platform or carrier.
- Choose signature confirmation and insurance based on item value, carrier coverage, and the current platform or payment-protection terms.
- Ship to the address shown in the verified transaction details when the protection terms require it; evaluate an address change as a new transaction path before sending the item.
