# Science reference sources

Use these primary or authoritative sources to verify science claims before presenting them as current consensus. Prefer the domain primary source for the specific claim.

## General literature and education

- Google Scholar — https://scholar.google.com/ — discovery of peer-reviewed literature; always open the primary paper before citing a result.
- PubMed — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ — biomedical and life-science literature index.
- arXiv — https://arxiv.org/ — open preprints in physics, math, CS, and related fields; distinguish preprint from peer-reviewed version.
- NIST Chemistry WebBook — https://webbook.nist.gov/chemistry/ — evaluated chemical and physical property data.
- NASA Science — https://science.nasa.gov/ — space and Earth-science educational and mission resources.
- NOAA Education — https://www.noaa.gov/education — weather, climate, and ocean education resources.
- USGS Education — https://www.usgs.gov/products/education — earth-science and natural-hazard education resources.
- PhET Interactive Simulations — https://phet.colorado.edu/ — research-based interactive science/math simulations for instruction.
- Next Generation Science Standards — https://www.nextgenscience.org/ — U.S. K–12 science learning framework reference (local curricula may differ).
- IUPAC Gold Book — https://goldbook.iupac.org/ — chemical terminology definitions.

## Verification practice

When a claim is time-sensitive or quantitative, open the primary page, record the date or version checked, and separate textbook consensus from a single study or preprint.
